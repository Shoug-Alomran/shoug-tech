1. Reflexivity & Irreflexivity

Notes

  1. 1

    Reflexive: (a, a) ∈ R for EVERY a. Irreflexive: (a, a) ∉ R for every a.

Slide 25 / 93

Difference between Not Reflexive and Irreflexive

Notes

  1. 1

    "Not reflexive" ≠ "irreflexive": a relation can be neither (some loops but not all).

  2. 2

    Trap Not reflexive just needs ONE missing loop; irreflexive needs ALL loops missing.

Exam angle

PredictionQ1

A relation that is not reflexive must be irreflexive.

Show answer

False — It can have some loops and not others.

2. Symmetry & Antisymmetry

Notes

  1. 1

    Symmetric: aRb ⇒ bRa. Antisymmetric: aRb and bRa ⇒ a = b.

  2. 2

    A relation can be both (e.g. equality) or neither.

Exam angle

PredictionQ1

R = 1,1), (1,2), (2,1 on {1, 2} is:

  1. Antisymmetric, not symmetric
  2. Symmetric, not antisymmetric
  3. Both
  4. Neither
Show answer

B. Symmetric, not antisymmetric

PredictionQ2

A relation can be both symmetric and antisymmetric.

Show answer

True — E.g. 1,1), (2,2.

Equivalence Relations

Notes

  1. 1

    Equivalence relation: reflexive + symmetric + transitive (e.g. congruence mod m). Its equivalence classes partition the set.

  2. 2

    Memory trick Equivalence = RST: Reflexive, Symmetric, Transitive.

Exam angle

PredictionQ1

Which is an equivalence relation on ℤ?

  1. a ≡ b (mod 4)
  2. a | b
  3. a < b
  4. a ≠ b
Show answer

A. a ≡ b (mod 4)

PredictionQ2

A partial order is reflexive, transitive and:

  1. Antisymmetric
  2. Irreflexive
  3. Symmetric
  4. Total
Show answer

A. Antisymmetric
