Twisted pairs

5
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Four pairs, each a different colour, inside one jacket.

Twisted-pair cable

  • A twisted pair consists of: two insulated copper wires in a regular

spiral pattern①

  • A wire pair acts as a single

communication link

  • Twisted to reduce electrical

interference from similar pairs close by (more twists means better quality)②

  • Used in:
    • Telephone network Between house and local exchange (subscriber loop)
    • LAN③
6
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Two insulated copper wires twisted together; one pair = one link.
  2. Twisting reduces interference (crosstalk) from neighbouring pairs. More twists = better quality.
  3. Used in the telephone subscriber loop and in LANs.
🎯 How this can come up · 2 questions
MCQ

Why are the wires in a twisted pair twisted?

Correct: To reduce interference from nearby pairs.

True / False

More twists per length gives better quality twisted-pair cable.

True.

Twist length: Category 3 vs. Category 5 UTP

(a) Category 3 UTP: twist length of 7.5 cm to 10 cm

(b) Category 5 UTP: twist length 0.6 cm to 0.85 cm

7
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Cat 5 is twisted far more tightly than Cat 3, so it carries higher frequencies with less crosstalk.

Why twisting cancels interference

a) Two parallel wires: the wire nearer the noise source picks up more, so a difference (noise) remains.

b) Twisted pair: the interference affects both wires equally overall, so the unwanted radiation is largely canceled.①

8
Exam weight ●●○ Medium
  1. The receiver looks at the DIFFERENCE between the two wires. Twisting makes the noise equal on both, so it subtracts out.
🎯 How this can come up · 1 question
Written

Explain how twisting a pair of wires reduces noise.

In parallel wires one wire is always closer to the noise source and picks up more noise, so a difference remains. Twisting swaps the positions all along the cable, so both wires pick up the same total noise; the receiver uses the difference between them, and the noise cancels.

UTP and STP cables

UTP: Unshielded Twisted Pair

STP: Shielded Twisted Pair

9
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. STP adds a metal shield (foil or braid) around the pairs.

STP vs. UTP

Shielded Twisted Pair (STP)

  • Metal braid or covering that reduces interference
  • More expensive
  • Harder to handle (thick, heavy)①

Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP)

  • Cheapest type of cable
  • Easiest type to install
  • Suffers from external electromagnetic (EMI) interference②
10
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. STP: shield → less EMI, but more expensive and harder to handle.
  2. UTP: cheap and easy, but suffers from EMI. UTP is what most LANs use.
Exam trapUTP is not “worse” for every case: it is the default in LANs because it is cheap and easy.
🎯 How this can come up · 2 questions
MCQ

Compared with UTP, STP:

Correct: Reduces interference but costs more and is harder to handle.

Scenario

A factory floor has heavy motors next to where the network cables will run. UTP or STP, and why?

STP. The motors cause electromagnetic interference (induced noise); the metal shield in STP reduces it. UTP would suffer from the EMI. (Fiber would be immune altogether.)

Categories of unshielded twisted-pair cables

  • CAT5e: 100 MHz · 1000 Mbps · UTP or STP①
  • CAT6: 250 MHz · 1000 Mbps · UTP or STP
  • CAT6a: 500 MHz · 10 Gbps · UTP or STP
  • CAT7: 600 MHz · 10 Gbps · shielded only
  • CAT8: 2000 MHz · 25 Gbps or 40 Gbps · shielded only

Shielded CAT8: up to 40 Gbps over 24 m②

11
Exam weight ●●○ Medium
  1. Higher category = higher bandwidth (MHz) and data rate.
  2. From CAT7 up the cable must be shielded.

UTP connectors

RJ = Registered Jack

12
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. RJ-45 (Registered Jack) is the 8-pin connector of twisted-pair Ethernet.

The RJ-45 connector pinout

  • Pin 1 TD+: Transmit Data positive · Pin 2 TD-: Transmit Data negative
  • Pin 3 RD+: Receive Data positive · Pin 6 RD-: Receive Data negative①
  • Pins 4, 5, 7, 8 N/C: No Connection / Not Connected → unused pins②
13
Exam weight ●●○ Medium
  1. 10/100 Ethernet uses only 4 pins: 1 & 2 transmit, 3 & 6 receive.
  2. 4, 5, 7, 8 are unused (Gigabit Ethernet uses all 8).
🎯 How this can come up · 2 questions
MCQ

In 10/100 Ethernet, which RJ-45 pins are used to transmit?

Correct: 1 and 2.

MCQ

Which pins receive data?

Correct: 3 and 6.

Crossover cable

In a crossover cable, one end’s transmit pins (1, 2) are wired to the other end’s receive pins (3, 6).

14
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Crossover swaps 1↔3 and 2↔6, so TX on one side meets RX on the other.

Ethernet cabling: straight-through vs. crossover

Straight-through cabling

  • PC to switch/hub
  • Router to switch/hub

Pins 1→1, 2→2, 3→3, 6→6①

Crossover cabling

  • PC to PC
  • Hub to hub
  • Switch to switch
  • Hub to switch

Pins 1→3, 2→6, 3→1, 6→2②

15
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Different kinds of device (PC ↔ switch, router ↔ switch) → straight-through.
  2. Same kind of device (PC ↔ PC, switch ↔ switch, hub ↔ hub, hub ↔ switch) → crossover, or TX meets TX.
Memory trick“Unlike → straight, like → cross.” (Router ↔ PC counts as “like”: both are end devices.)
🎯 How this can come up · 3 questions

False. Switch to switch needs a crossover (unless auto-MDIX).

Coaxial cable

  • Inner copper wire (the conductor) inside insulation
  • Outer conductor of copper mesh (braid or foil) that shields it
  • Outer jacket
16
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Coax = a central copper wire + an outer copper mesh as a shield. Used in cable TV and old bus Ethernet (10Base5, 10Base2).

Optical Fiber

  • Consists of three concentric sections: fiber core and cladding, a glass or plastic cover, and a plastic jacket
    • Core: one or more very thin strands or fibers made of glass or plastic①
    • Each fiber is surrounded by its own cladding, a glass or plastic coating that has optical properties different from the core
    • Jacket: a plastic or other material acts as a layer to protect against moisture, crushing, and other environmental dangers.②
17
Exam weight ●●○ Medium
  1. Core (carries the light) → cladding (different optical property, reflects the light back in) → jacket (protection).
🎯 How this can come up · 1 question
MCQ

The layer of a fiber that keeps the light inside the core is the:

Correct: Cladding.

Optical Fiber

18
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Light travels by reflecting off the core/cladding boundary.

Fiber Optics Properties

Advantages

  • Higher bandwidth: data rates of hundreds of Gbps
  • Smaller size & weight
  • Lower attenuation (signal loss)
    • Greater repeater spacing: can run 50 km at least without repeaters①
  • No crosstalk (no light leaking)
  • Not affected by noise
  • Highly secure (no light leaking)

Disadvantages

  • Needs special skills to install
  • Cost: more expensive②
19
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Fiber: more bandwidth, less attenuation (50+ km without repeaters), immune to noise and crosstalk, very secure.
  2. Downsides: expensive, needs skilled installers.
Exam trapFiber is NOT cheaper or easier to install. Those are its two disadvantages.
🎯 How this can come up · 3 questions
MCQ

Which is a disadvantage of fiber-optic cable?

Correct: It needs special skills to install.

True / False

Fiber-optic cable is not affected by electromagnetic noise.

True.

Written

Give three advantages of fiber over copper.

Higher bandwidth (hundreds of Gbps), lower attenuation so repeaters can be 50 km or more apart, immunity to noise and crosstalk, smaller size and weight, and better security because no light leaks out.

UTP cabling vs. fiber-optic cabling

UTP cabling

  • Bandwidth: 10 Mb/s – 10 Gb/s
  • Distance: relatively short (1 – 100 m)
  • Immunity to EMI and RFI: low
  • Immunity to electrical hazards: low
  • Media and connector cost: lowest
  • Installation skills: lowest①

Fiber-optic cabling

  • Bandwidth: 10 Mb/s – 100 Gb/s
  • Distance: relatively long (1 – 100,000 m)
  • Immunity to EMI and RFI: high (completely immune)
  • Immunity to electrical hazards: high (completely immune)
  • Media and connector cost: highest
  • Installation skills: highest②
20
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. UTP: up to 100 m, cheap, easy, but not immune to EMI.
  2. Fiber: up to 100 km, immune to EMI and electrical hazards, but the most expensive.
🎯 How this can come up · 2 questions
MCQ

The maximum distance of a UTP segment in the table is about:

Correct: 100 m.

Scenario

Two buildings 2 km apart must be linked, and a high-voltage line runs between them. Which cable?

Fiber. UTP only reaches about 100 m and is not immune to EMI or electrical hazards; fiber reaches tens of kilometres and is completely immune.

Wireless transmission waves (Figure 7.19)

Electromagnetic spectrum for wireless communication

As we move from left to right, the frequency increases.

Radio waves and microwaves: 3 kHz – 300 GHz · Infrared: 300 GHz – 400 THz · then visible light (400 – 900 THz)①

23
Exam weight ●●○ Medium
  1. Radio < 300 MHz < microwave < 300 GHz < infrared < 400 THz < visible light.
Memory trickOrder by frequency: Radio → Microwave → Infrared → Light (“Really Many Infrared Lights”).

Radio waves

  • Less than 300 MHz①
    • Omnidirectional (signal propagates in all directions)
    • Can Travel long distances
    • Broadcast radio (AM, FM), TV②

Exam weight ●●○ Medium

Exam weight ●●○ Medium

🎯 How this can come up · 1 question
MCQ

Radio waves are used for which type of communication?

Correct: Multicast (radio and TV).

Microwaves

  • 300MHz to 300GHz (sub range from the Radio waves)①
    • Travel in one direction (Unidirectional = line-of-sight propagation = straight lines)
    • Can be used for point to point for example between buildings to connect their LANs
    • high frequency microwaves, difficult to pass walls (if receivers inside buildings)
      • Higher frequency → shorter wavelength → more difficulty passing through obstacles such as walls.②
    • Used in Wireless networks, satellite, cellular phones.
      • microwaves carry information through the air.③
26
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Microwave: 300 MHz – 300 GHz, unidirectional (line of sight), can link two buildings point to point.
  2. Higher frequency → shorter wavelength → harder to pass through walls.
  3. Used in wireless networks, satellite and cellular phones.
Exam trapRadio is OMNIdirectional; microwave is UNIdirectional (line of sight).

Infrared (IR Wireless)

  • 300GHz to 400THz①
    • Have a very large bandwidth
    • Local- short distance communication because they cannot penetrate walls
    • Line-of-sight propagation (directional)
    • Used in local point-to-point Transmission
    • Applications: remote controls, some wireless headphones, and some wireless computer peripherals.②
    • Use Infrared Data Association (IrDA) protocol
    • Cannot be used under the sun because of the interference with the sun infrared rays
    • Replaced by Bluetooth Technology③
28
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Infrared: 300 GHz – 400 THz, very large bandwidth, line of sight, short range (can’t pass walls).
  2. Remote controls and some peripherals (IrDA protocol). Doesn’t work in sunlight. Mostly replaced by Bluetooth.

🎯 How this can come up · 1 question
Written

Compare radio waves, microwaves and infrared by direction and typical use.

Radio (< 300 MHz): omnidirectional, long range, multicast such as radio and TV. Microwave (300 MHz – 300 GHz): unidirectional line of sight, unicast such as cellular, satellite and wireless LANs. Infrared (300 GHz – 400 THz): line of sight, short range inside a room, such as remote controls.
