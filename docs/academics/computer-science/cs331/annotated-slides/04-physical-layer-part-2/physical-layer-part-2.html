Slide 8 / 29

Why twisting cancels interference

Notes

  1. 1

    The receiver looks at the DIFFERENCE between the two wires. Twisting makes the noise equal on both, so it subtracts out.

Exam angle

PredictionQ1

Explain how twisting a pair of wires reduces noise.

Compare with the model answer

In parallel wires one wire is always closer to the noise source and picks up more noise, so a difference remains. Twisting swaps the positions all along the cable, so both wires pick up the same total noise; the receiver uses the difference between them, and the noise cancels.

Slide 10 / 29

STP vs. UTP

Notes

  1. 1

    STP: shield → less EMI, but more expensive and harder to handle.

  2. 2

    UTP: cheap and easy, but suffers from EMI. UTP is what most LANs use.

  3. 3

    Trap UTP is not “worse” for every case: it is the default in LANs because it is cheap and easy.

A factory floor has heavy motors next to where the network cables will run. UTP or STP, and why?

Slide 15 / 29

Ethernet cabling: straight-through vs. crossover

Notes

  1. 1

    Different kinds of device (PC ↔ switch, router ↔ switch) → straight-through.

  2. 2

    Same kind of device (PC ↔ PC, switch ↔ switch, hub ↔ hub, hub ↔ switch) → crossover, or TX meets TX.

  3. 3

    Memory trick “Unlike → straight, like → cross.” (Router ↔ PC counts as “like”: both are end devices.)

Slide 19 / 29

Fiber Optics Properties

Notes

  1. 1

    Fiber: more bandwidth, less attenuation (50+ km without repeaters), immune to noise and crosstalk, very secure.

  2. 2

    Downsides: expensive, needs skilled installers.

  3. 3

    Trap Fiber is NOT cheaper or easier to install. Those are its two disadvantages.

Exam angle

PredictionQ1

Which is a disadvantage of fiber-optic cable?

PredictionQ2

Fiber-optic cable is not affected by electromagnetic noise.

PredictionQ3

Give three advantages of fiber over copper.

Compare with the model answer

Higher bandwidth (hundreds of Gbps), lower attenuation so repeaters can be 50 km or more apart, immunity to noise and crosstalk, smaller size and weight, and better security because no light leaks out.

Exam angle

PredictionQ1

Compare radio waves, microwaves and infrared by direction and typical use.

Compare with the model answer

Radio (< 300 MHz): omnidirectional, long range, multicast such as radio and TV. Microwave (300 MHz – 300 GHz): unidirectional line of sight, unicast such as cellular, satellite and wireless LANs. Infrared (300 GHz – 400 THz): line of sight, short range inside a room, such as remote controls.
