Data Link Layer: Multiple Access Protocols

How many stations share one link: random access (CSMA and its three persistence methods, CSMA/CD), controlled access (reservation, polling, token passing) and channelization (FDMA, TDMA, CDMA). The favourite questions compare them: who is fair, who can collide, and who is stuck at R/M even when alone on the link.

Multiple Access

  • Problem: when two or more nodes transmit at the same time, their frames collide and the link bandwidth is wasted during the collision①
    • How do we coordinate the access of multiple sending/receiving nodes to the shared link?
  • Solution: a protocol to coordinate the transmission of the active nodes
  • These protocols are called Medium or Multiple Access Control (MAC) protocols and belong to a sublayer of the data link layer called MAC (Medium Access Control)②
4
Exam weight ●●○ Medium
  1. Collision = two frames on the medium at once → both destroyed, bandwidth wasted.
  2. MAC protocols decide who sends when. They live in the MAC sublayer.

Data link layer: two sublayers

  • Data link control (LLC): responsible for error and flow control①
  • Multiple-access control (MAC): responsible for framing, MAC address and multiple access control②

Random Access

Random access protocols:

  • No station is superior to another station and none is assigned control over another.①
  • A station with a frame to be transmitted can use the link directly, based on a procedure defined by the protocol, to decide whether or not to send.②
7
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Random access: no master, no turns. Each station decides for itself when to send, so collisions are possible.

Random Access – Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA)

  • Based on the fact that in a LAN the propagation time is very small
  • If a frame is sent by a station, all stations know immediately, so they can wait before starting to send
    • A station with frames to send should sense the medium for another transmission (carrier) before it starts its own①
  • This can reduce the possibility of collision but it cannot eliminate it.②
    • A collision can only happen when more than one station begins transmitting within a short time (the propagation time period)③
8
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. CSMA = listen before you talk.
  2. It reduces collisions but can’t eliminate them: a station far away may not hear a frame that already started.
  3. Collisions only happen within the propagation time window.
Exam trap“CSMA eliminates collisions” ✗. It only reduces them.

Random Access – Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA)

  • The vulnerable (unsafe) time for CSMA is the maximum propagation time
  • Maximum propagation delay (t_prop): the time it takes a bit of a frame to travel between the two most widely separated stations.①

Station A begins transmitting at t = 0; the farthest station B receives the first bit at t = t_prop②

9
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Vulnerable time = max propagation delay between the two farthest stations.

Random Access – Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA)

  • The longer the propagation delay, the worse the performance of the protocol.①
  • During this propagation period, a distant node might think the channel is still idle and start transmitting.
10
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Long cables = longer window where distant stations wrongly think the line is free.

Types of CSMA Protocols

Different CSMA protocols decide:

  • What should a station do when the medium is idle?
  • What should a station do when the medium is busy?
  1. Non-persistent CSMA
  2. 1-persistent CSMA
  3. p-persistent CSMA
11
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Three persistence methods: they differ in what to do when the line is BUSY and when it is IDLE.

Nonpersistent CSMA

A station with frames to send senses the medium:

  1. If the medium is idle, transmit; otherwise go to 2.
  2. If the medium is busy, (backoff) wait a random amount of time and repeat 1.①
  • Non-persistent stations are deferential (they respect others)②
  • Performance:
    • Random delays reduce the probability of collisions, because two stations with data will wait different amounts of time.
    • Bandwidth is wasted if the waiting time (backoff) is large, because the medium stays idle after a transmission ends even if stations have frames to send.③
12
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Non-persistent: busy → walk away for a random time, then check again.
  2. Fewer collisions, but the line can sit idle (wasted).
🎯 How this can come up · 1 question
MCQ

In non-persistent CSMA, if the medium is busy the station:

Correct: Waits a random time and senses again.

1-persistent CSMA

  • To avoid idle channel time, the 1-persistent protocol is used
  • A station wishing to transmit listens to the medium:
  1. If the medium is idle, transmit immediately;
  2. If the medium is busy, continuously listen until it becomes idle, then transmit immediately with probability 1 (100%)①
  • Performance
    • 1-persistent stations are selfish②
    • If two or more stations become ready at the same time, a collision is guaranteed③
13
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. 1-persistent: busy → keep listening → the instant it’s idle, send (probability 1).
  2. No idle time, but if two stations were waiting, they BOTH send → guaranteed collision.
Memory trickNon-persistent = polite (walks away). 1-persistent = selfish (stares at the line).
🎯 How this can come up · 2 questions
MCQ

Which persistence method guarantees a collision when two stations wait for the same busy channel?

Correct: 1-persistent.

True / False

1-persistent CSMA sends with probability 1 as soon as the channel becomes idle.

True.

P-persistent CSMA

  • Time is divided into slots; each slot typically equals the maximum propagation delay
  • A station wishing to transmit listens to the medium:
  1. If the medium is idle: transmit with probability p, OR wait one time slot with probability 1 – p, then repeat listening to the medium.①
  2. If the medium is busy, continuously listen until idle and repeat step 1.②
  • Performance:
    • Reduces the possibility of collisions, like non-persistent
    • Reduces channel idle time, like 1-persistent③
14
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. p-persistent: idle → send with probability p, or wait one slot with probability 1 - p.
  2. Best of both: fewer collisions (like non-persistent) and less idle time (like 1-persistent).
  3. Slot = maximum propagation delay.
🎯 How this can come up · 2 questions
MCQ

In p-persistent CSMA, a time slot equals:

Correct: The maximum propagation delay.

Written

Compare non-persistent, 1-persistent and p-persistent CSMA when the channel is busy and when it becomes idle.

Non-persistent: if busy, wait a random time then sense again (fewer collisions, wasted idle time). 1-persistent: if busy, keep listening and send immediately with probability 1 when idle (no idle time, collisions guaranteed if several are waiting). p-persistent: keep listening; when idle send with probability p or wait a slot with probability 1 - p (reduces both collisions and idle time).

CSMA/CD (Collision Detection)

  • CSMA (all previous methods) has an inefficiency:
    • If a collision occurs, the channel is unstable until the colliding packets have been fully transmitted①
  • CSMA/CD (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection) overcomes this as follows:
    • While transmitting, the sender is listening to the medium for collisions.
    • The sender stops transmission if a collision has occurred, reducing channel wastage.②

CSMA/CD is widely used in star topology if a hub is used③

17
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Plain CSMA keeps sending the whole (already ruined) frame. CSMA/CD listens WHILE sending and stops as soon as it detects a collision.
  2. Used on shared Ethernet (bus or hub).

MCQ

What does CSMA/CD add to CSMA?

Correct: Listening while transmitting and stopping on a collision.

How does a node detect a collision?

Transceiver: a node monitors the medium while transmitting. If the observed power is more than the transmitted power of its own signal, a collision has occurred.①

18
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Detection = the energy on the wire is higher than what I am sending → someone else is sending too.

CSMA/CD Protocol

  • Use one of the CSMA persistence algorithms (non-persistent, 1-persistent, p-persistent) for transmission
  • If a collision is detected by a station during its transmission, it should:
    • Abort transmission, and
    • Transmit a jam signal (48 bits) to notify the other stations of the collision, so that they discard the transmitted frame, and to make sure the collision signal stays until it is detected by the furthest station①
    • After sending the jam signal, back off (wait) for a random amount of time, then
    • Reapply the CSMA persistence algorithm used②
19
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. On collision: ABORT → send a 48-bit JAM → random BACKOFF → try again with the same persistence method.
Memory trickCSMA/CD collision steps: “Abort, Jam, Back off, Retry.”

Performance of Random Access Protocols

  • Simple and easy to implement
  • Decentralized (no central device that can fail and bring down the entire system)
  • In low traffic, packet transfer has low delay
  • However, in heavier traffic, packet delay has no limit.①
  • In some cases, a station may never have a chance to transfer its packet (unfair protocol)
  • A node that has frames to transmit can transmit continuously at the full rate of the channel (R) if it is the only node with frames②
  • If M nodes want to transmit, many collisions can occur and the maximum average throughput for each node will not be R/M
    • (R/M = ideal average rate per node if the channel were divided equally with no overhead or collisions.)③
20
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Random access: simple, decentralized, great at low load; UNFAIR and unbounded delay at high load.
  2. Alone on the link → full rate R. Busy link → each node gets less than R/M because of collisions.

Controlled access (scheduling)

  • Provides in-order access to the shared medium so that every station has a chance to transfer (fair protocol)①
  • A node is limited to throughput equal to R/M②
  • Eliminates collisions completely
  • Three methods for controlled access:
    • Reservation
    • Polling
    • Token passing③
21
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Controlled access: stations take turns → fair, no collisions, but capped at R/M.
Exam trapRandom = can collide, unfair, R when alone. Controlled = never collides, fair, R/M.

MCQ

The three controlled-access methods are:

Correct: Reservation, polling, token passing.

1-Reservation access method

  • Stations take turns transmitting a single frame at a

full rate (R) bps①

  • Transmissions are organized into variable length cycles
  • Each cycle begins with a reservation frame that consists of N minislots, one minislot for each of the N stations②
  • When a station needs to send a data frame, it makes a

reservation in its own minislot.

  • By listening to the reservation interval, every station knows which stations will transfer frames, and in which order.
  • The stations that made reservations can send their data frames after the reservation frame.③ 22
22
Exam weight ●●○ Medium
  1. Each cycle starts with a reservation frame of N minislots (one per station). A station sets its minislot to reserve a turn.
  2. Everyone hears the reservations, so everyone knows the order. Then the reserved data frames follow.
🎯 How this can come up · 1 question
MCQ

In the reservation method, a reservation frame contains:

Correct: One minislot per station.

2- Polling

  • Stations take turns accessing the medium
  • Two models: Centralized and distributed polling
  • Centralized polling
    • One device is assigned as primary station and the others as secondary stations①
    • All data exchanges are done through the primary
    • When the primary has a frame to send, it sends a select frame that includes the address of the intended secondary
    • When the primary is ready to receive data it sends a Poll frame for each device to ask if it has data to send or not. If yes, data will be transmitted otherwise NAK (Negative Acknowledgement) is sent.②
    • Polling can be done in order (Round-Robin) or based on predetermined order
  • Distributed polling
    • No primary and secondary
    • Stations have a known polling order list which is made based on some protocol
    • station with the highest priority will have the access right first, then it passes the access right to the next station (it will send a pulling message to the next station in the pulling list), which will passes the access right to the following next station, ... (Reservation and Token Passing are examples of Distributed Polling)③ 23
23
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Centralized polling: a PRIMARY controls everything. SELECT = primary wants to send to a secondary. POLL = primary asks a secondary if it has data (data or NAK back).
  2. Distributed polling: no primary; stations follow a known order and pass the right to send along. Reservation and token passing are distributed polling.
Memory trickSELect = primary SEnds. POLL = primary coLLects.

Token-passing network

  • Implements a distributed polling system
  • A special frame called the token rotates around the ring.①
  • Any station that wants to send data has to wait for a free token to arrive.
  • The station makes the token busy by inserting its data into it and putting in the destination address.
  • The destination station receives the data and changes the token back to free.②
25
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. Only the station holding the free token may send → no collisions.

Channelization

Channelization is a multiple-access method in which the available bandwidth of a link is shared in time, frequency, or through code, between different stations.①

  • Frequency-Division Multiple Access (FDMA)
  • Time-Division Multiple Access (TDMA)
  • Code-Division Multiple Access (CDMA)②
27
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Split the channel by frequency (FDMA), by time (TDMA) or by code (CDMA).

Frequency-division multiple access (FDMA) (Figure 12.21)

28
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Each station has its own frequency band, all the time.

Channelization: FDMA

  • FDMA: Frequency Division Multiple Access
    • The transmission medium is divided into M separate frequency bands①
    • Each station transmits continuously on its assigned band at an average rate of R/M②
    • A node is limited to an average throughput of R/M even when it is the only node with frames to send③
29
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. FDMA: own band, send continuously, R/M even when alone (other bands sit empty).
🎯 How this can come up · 1 question
MCQ

In FDMA, a station that is the only one with data can send at:

Correct: R/M.

Time-division multiple access (TDMA) (Figure 12.22)

30
Exam weight ●○○ Low
  1. Each station has its own time slot on the whole bandwidth.

Channelization: TDMA

  • TDMA: Time Division Multiple Access
    • The entire bandwidth is a single channel whose capacity is shared in time between M stations①
    • A node must always wait for its turn until its slot arrives, even when it is the only node with frames③ to send②
    • A node is limited to an average throughput of R/M even when it is the only node with frames
31
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. TDMA: whole bandwidth, but only in your slot. Still R/M on average even when alone.
Exam trapFDMA, TDMA (and controlled access) cap a lone station at R/M; random access lets it use all of R.
🎯 How this can come up · 2 questions
True / False

In TDMA, a station that is the only one with frames can use every time slot.

False. It must wait for its own slot.

Written

Compare FDMA and TDMA.

FDMA divides the channel by frequency: each station gets its own band and sends continuously. TDMA divides it by time: each station uses the whole bandwidth but only in its own slot. Both limit a station to R/M even when it is the only one sending.

Channelization: CDMA

  • CDMA: Code Division Multiple Access
    • In CDMA, one channel carries all transmissions simultaneously①
    • Each station codes its data signal with a specific code before transmission
    • The receivers use these codes to recover the data of the desired station②
32
Exam weight ●●● High
  1. CDMA: everyone sends at the same time on the same frequency; codes separate them.
🎯 How this can come up · 1 question
