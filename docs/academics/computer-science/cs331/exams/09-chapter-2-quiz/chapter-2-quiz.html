3.
Signals

A signal has a frequency of 4 MHz. Its period is µs.

T = 1 / f = 1 / 4,000,000 = 0.25 µs.
4.
Signals

Read the figure: the wave has a period of ms and a frequency of kHz.

Two full cycles fit in 1 ms, so one cycle (the period) is 0.5 ms and f = 1 / 0.5 ms = 2 kHz.
5.
Signals

True or false: A composite signal is made of many simple sine waves with different frequencies.

A simple signal is one sine wave; a composite signal is a sum of many.

λ = speed / f = 3 × 10⁸ / 10⁶ = 300 m.

2. Bit rate and digital transmission/4

1.
Bit rate and digital transmission

A bit rate of 2 Mbps gives a bit interval of µs.

Bit interval = 1 / bit rate = 1 / 2,000,000 = 0.5 µs.
2.
Bit rate and digital transmission

A bit interval of 100 µs gives a bit rate of kbps.

Bit rate = 1 / 0.0001 = 10,000 bps = 10 kbps.
3.
Bit rate and digital transmission

Which is an advantage of digital transmission?

Repeaters regenerate the bits, removing noise instead of amplifying it.
4.
Bit rate and digital transmission

Rate prefixes step by × 1000; bytes to bits × 8.

4. Delay and performance/5

1.
Delay and performance

A signal travels 12,000 km through cable at 2.4 × 10⁸ m/s. The propagation time is ms.

1.2 × 10⁷ m / 2.4 × 10⁸ m/s = 0.05 s = 50 ms.
2.
Delay and performance

Sending a 5,000-byte message on a 1 Mbps link takes ms (transmission time).

5,000 × 8 = 40,000 bits; 40,000 / 10⁶ = 0.04 s = 40 ms.
3.
Delay and performance

With propagation 50 ms, transmission 40 ms, queuing 5 ms and processing 1 ms, the latency is ms.

Latency = propagation + transmission + queuing + processing = 96 ms.
4.
Delay and performance

A 10 Mbps link with a 20 ms delay holds bits (bandwidth-delay product).

10⁷ × 0.02 = 200,000 bits.
5.
Delay and performance

True or false: Doubling the bandwidth halves the propagation time.

Bandwidth changes the transmission time; propagation depends only on distance and speed.
