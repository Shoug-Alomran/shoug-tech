Slide 25 / 58

Gradient Descent vs. Newton's Method

Notes

  1. 1

    Comparison points 3 and 4: gradient descent is robust and works on non-convex problems. Newton is fast near the minimum but can diverge or land on saddle points.

Exam angle

PredictionQ1

Compare gradient descent and Newton's method on derivatives, cost per iteration and behaviour on non-convex problems.

Compare with the model answer

Gradient descent uses first derivatives, is cheap per iteration and works on non-convex problems, possibly stopping at a local minimum. Newton uses the Hessian, is expensive per iteration and is fast near the minimum, but on non-convex problems it can reach saddle points or diverge.

Slide 31 / 58

Mathematical Foundation of Gradient Descent

Notes

  1. 1

    Substituting the step gives f(x) minus eta times the squared gradient norm, which cannot be negative, so f falls for a small enough eta.

Exam angle

PredictionQ1

Explain why the update x - eta grad f(x) decreases f.

Compare with the model answer

By a first-order Taylor expansion, f changes by about -eta times the squared gradient norm. That norm is non-negative, so f decreases for a small enough eta.

Understanding Bias vs. Variance

Notes

  1. 1

    Bias comes from a model too simple. Variance comes from a model too sensitive to the particular training set.

Exam angle

PredictionQ1

High variance means the model is:

  4. High variance means it captures random noise.

PredictionQ2

Define bias and variance, and say which causes underfitting and which causes overfitting.

Compare with the model answer

Bias is error from overly simple assumptions and causes underfitting. Variance is error from sensitivity to the training set and causes overfitting.
