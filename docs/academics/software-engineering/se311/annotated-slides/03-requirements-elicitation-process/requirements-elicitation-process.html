                    When are we done?

                    U sers can’t think of any more use cases or user stories

                    • U sers propose new scenarios that don’t lead to any new functional
                    • requirements
                    • Users repeat issues that were previously covered
                    • Suggested new features or requirements are all out of scope
                    • Proposed new requirements are all low priority
                    • Developers and testers review the requirements have few questions

                    Asked in a real exam

                    ★ Quiz 1 (Ch. 1–3) · Q11: when elicitation is done
                    🎯 How this can come up · 1 question
                    Written

                    When do you know you are done with elicitation? Give two signs.

                    Users can’t think of any more use cases or user stories; new suggestions are out of scope or already captured (repeating earlier requirements); remaining ideas are “nice to have” for future releases; the team can explain the requirements well enough to build and test.

                    Exam weight ●○○ Low
                    1. Challenges: stakeholders unavailable, don’t know what they want, use jargon, conflict; scope creep; solution ideas disguised as requirements.

                    Final Remarks

                    • Requirements elicitation is perhaps the most challenging, critical, error-prone, and communication-intensive aspect of software development
                    • Try to extract the essence of stakeholders’ requirements and invent new ways for them to better perform their tasks
                    • Remember, elicitation is incremental
