Know who cares,
then tell them the truth.

Stakeholder management is developing the right strategy for every person or group the project affects, so they stay engaged across the life cycle. It rests on communication: who needs what information, why, when, how, and from whom, delivered honestly. This chapter covers the register and plan, engagement levels, the mapping models, push/pull/interactive communication, ethics, and the reports that keep expectations realistic.

01 — STAKEHOLDER MANAGEMENT

Strategies for everyone the project touches

Stakeholder management is the process of developing appropriate management strategies for all project stakeholders. Its goal is to engage them effectively throughout the life cycle, analyzing their needs, interests and potential impact, and to give the project a plan for interacting with them in the project's interest.

Inputs, tools and outputs

process
Items
Inputs Project charter (authorizes the project, names key stakeholders such as the sponsor and client) · procurement documents (reveal external stakeholders: vendors, contractors, partners) · enterprise environmental factors (culture, regulations, market) · organizational process assets (templates, past stakeholder lists, lessons learned, communication policies)
Tools and techniques Stakeholder analysis (who they are, their interests, influence and impact) · expert judgment · meetings and company visits
Outputs Stakeholder register · stakeholder management plan
The stakeholder register
The primary output of Identify Stakeholders. For each stakeholder: name · position · location · role in the project · contact information · major requirements · main expectations · potential influence · the life-cycle phase of most interest · a classification (internal/external; supporter/neutral/resister; high/medium/low influence, power, impact or interest).
Engagement levels
Unaware: unaware of the project and its impacts
Resistant: aware, and resistant to the changes
Neutral: aware, neither supportive nor resistant
Supportive: aware, and supportive of the changes
Leading: aware, and actively engaged in making the project succeed

The stakeholder management plan

supplements the register

It identifies the strategies needed to engage stakeholders, adding to the register:

• desired and current engagement levels of key stakeholders
• scope and impact of the change on each stakeholder
• interrelationships and overlap between stakeholders
• communication requirements
• information to distribute (language, format, content, level of detail), why, and its expected impact on engagement
• time frame and frequency of distribution
Sensitive document. The PMBOK warns that information given to resistant stakeholders can be damaging, so think about who sees the plan and what is shared.
The agile view
Identifying stakeholders is valuable: the more inclusive your understanding, the better. Agile favors transparency (it builds trust) and uses information radiators, such as a project wiki, that make progress and impediments visible to everyone. That minimizes miscommunication and short-circuits rumors.
Tailoring information per stakeholder
Stakeholder management sometimes tailors information access and flow to each stakeholder. That can be justified on a volatile, highly politicized project, but it carries risk.
Champion: often a senior leader, sponsor or influential stakeholder who backs the project.
02 — STAKEHOLDER REPORTING IN AGILE AND DEVOPS

Iterative work is hard to see from outside

Agile and DevOps need proper tooling for reporting to key stakeholders. A complex tool chain has grown up to manage agile projects, because iterative work makes it hard for stakeholders to tell what is happening, or whether the project is on time and on budget.
Those stakeholders include not only Dev and Ops but management, product management, marketing, sales and finance, and the problem grows when they look across multiple projects. Business value metrics dashboards give managers real-time, automated reporting on best-practice KPIs, and customers can set targets to see which agile processes need improving.
03 — COMMUNICATION

The most important factor in engaging stakeholders

Communication planning overlaps with identifying, analyzing, prioritizing and engaging stakeholders.

❓
Memory hook
Who · What · Why · How · When · Who has it
The six questions for planning communication: who needs information, what each stakeholder needs, why share it, the best way to provide it, when and how often, and who has the information. Information may be internal or external, sensitive or public, general or detailed; analyzing all of this is the foundation of the communication plan.
Communication concepts
Communication passes through a channel and is distorted by noise. Types include push, pull and interactive (see 05).

Number of communication channels for N people = N(N − 1) / 2.

Blockers: no agenda, poor timing and similar barriers.
For a successful meeting
• send notice in advance
• set a time limit
• regular, but not too frequent
• a clear purpose and agenda announced beforehand
• assign action items with clear deadlines and owners
• document and publish the minutes

Plan communications

who, when, what channel, how often

Consider culture, expectations, technology, methods, and established policies and procedures. Then plan what to communicate:

Type Examples
Project documents Charter, project management plan
Schedule and resources WBS, resource needs, meeting schedule, work assignments (upcoming and current)
Status General status, impacts to and from other projects, performance reports
Risks New risks, uncertainties, realized risks (problems)
Change control Scope changes, communications with the change control board (CCB): sponsors, PMs, technical leads, QA and others who approve changes
The communication management plan is the primary output of Plan Communications.
04 — ETHICAL AND PROFESSIONAL RESPONSIBILITY

Communication is not effective unless it is honest

Ethical responsibility
Communication is at the heart of ethical behavior for a PM or team member:
• try to understand the truth
• be careful: your perception of the truth may be false
• seek the whole truth
• be truthful in all communications
• communicate honestly even if the project is in trouble
• create an environment where others tell the truth; telling it consistently shows that only the truth is acceptable
Professional responsibility
• accept only assignments you are qualified for (lack of qualification is a major risk)
• protect proprietary information; report unethical behavior and violations
• keep an attitude of mutual cooperation
• consider other projects and operations when asking for resources
• protect team members' reputations
• ask which communication method stakeholders prefer
• be direct with conflict: discuss it openly with the other party, not behind their back
• look for conflicts of interest and disclose them
05 — STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT

Six steps, and the right way to talk

The six steps
1. Identify your stakeholders
2. Understand them
3. Analyze them
4. Prioritize them
5. Engage them
6. Monitor them
Identifying stakeholders means
1. Analyze stakeholders continuously · 2. Create and maintain a stakeholder register · 3. Create and execute a stakeholder management strategy
What engaging involves
Working collaboratively with stakeholders to introduce the project, elicit requirements, manage expectations, resolve issues, negotiate, prioritize, solve problems and make decisions.

It needs soft skills (active listening, interpersonal skills, conflict management) and leadership skills (establishing the vision, critical thinking).

Push, pull and interactive communication

communication methods
Method Examples Notes
Push Memos, emails, status reports, voice mail One-way, to individuals or groups. You can't immediately gauge reaction or understanding, so use it deliberately
Pull Intranet policies and templates, internet searches, online repositories Sought by the stakeholder; used for indirect sensing of stakeholder concerns; suits large audiences
Interactive Meetings, calls, video conferences Two-way and real time; best for building shared understanding
Quick feedback loops (with every method) confirm how far the message was heard, whether stakeholders agree with it, any nuanced or unintended messages they picked up, and other insights.
06 — STAKEHOLDER MAPPING MODELS

Three ways to prioritize

Power/interest grid

prioritize communication effort

Two dimensions: the stakeholder's power (influence) and their interest in the project.

Quadrant Strategy
High power · high interest Manage closely: engage regularly and manage expectations
High power · low interest Keep satisfied: meet their needs and actively consult
Low power · high interest Keep informed: maintain their interest
Low power · low interest Monitor: provide information as needed
Salience model
Prioritizes stakeholders by how much attention they demand, using three attributes: power, legitimacy and urgency. All three make a definitive stakeholder; two make dominant (power + legitimacy), dangerous (power + urgency) or dependent (legitimacy + urgency); one makes dormant, discretionary or demanding.
Stakeholder knowledge base chart
Shapes communication tactics by how much a stakeholder knows and whether they support or oppose:

Aware · opposition: may be a risk; needs management
Aware · support: keep informed so they keep championing
Ignorant · opposition: more understanding may change their attitude
Ignorant · support: engage them to keep them on board and strengthen support
07 — MANAGING EXPECTATIONS AND REPORTING

Reports that help, not hide

Managing expectations applies communication methods, interpersonal skills and management skills to the people important to the project.

Items that may need regular communication
• schedule, budget, forecasts
• risk register, issue log, change log
• items outside the project's control that affect it
Making reports and metrics useful
Collect, analyze, package and send information to stakeholders, with the right level of detail and channel.
• don't let reports stop you managing the project
• measure against a consistent baseline
• be truthful, with accurate metrics
• cover all three sides of the triangle plus quality
• enhance with forecasts · get feedback · use several report types

Performance report types

know the question each answers
Report Answers
Status Where is the project against the performance measurement baseline?
Progress What has been accomplished?
Trend Is performance improving or deteriorating?
Forecasting What are the predictions for the future?
Variance How do actual results compare with the baselines?
Earned value Where is the project in pure earned value / cost management terms?
Lessons learned What could be useful for future projects?
Conclusion: apply communication concepts to stakeholders and the team alike; communication is only effective if it is ethical and honest; expectation management starts with understanding each stakeholder and choosing the best communication for them; view reports and metrics from the stakeholder's perspective.

COMMON MISTAKES

Where exam answers usually go wrong

6 places this chapter's terms get mixed up.

1. Register vs plan
✗ WRONG“The stakeholder plan lists names, roles and contact details.”
✓ RIGHTThat is the register. The plan adds strategies: desired vs current engagement, communication requirements, distribution.
2. Push vs pull
✗ WRONGCalling a portal or wiki “push”.
✓ RIGHTPull: stakeholders fetch it when they need it. Push is sent to specific people (emails, memos, reports).
3. Keep satisfied vs keep informed
✗ WRONGHigh power, low interest → keep informed.
✓ RIGHTHigh power, low interest → keep satisfied. Low power, high interest → keep informed.
4. Status vs progress report
✗ WRONGUsing the two names interchangeably.
✓ RIGHTStatus: where we stand against the baseline. Progress: what was accomplished in a period.
5. Neutral vs unaware
✗ WRONGA stakeholder who doesn't react is “unaware”.
✓ RIGHTUnaware stakeholders don't know about the project; neutral ones know and neither support nor resist.
6. Honesty under pressure
✗ WRONGSoftening bad news to keep stakeholders calm.
✓ RIGHTCommunicate honestly even when the project is in trouble; only the truth should be acceptable.
POP QUIZ

Click a question to reveal the answer

No tracking: click a question (or Reveal) to check yourself.

1. What are the two outputs of stakeholder identification and analysis?
The stakeholder register and the stakeholder management plan.
2. List five things the stakeholder register holds for each stakeholder.
Any five of: name, position, location, role, contact info, major requirements, expectations, potential influence, phase of most interest, classification.
3. Name the five engagement levels.
Unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive, leading.
4. Why is the stakeholder management plan sensitive?
It records frank assessments; sharing it with resistant stakeholders can be damaging.
5. What is an information radiator?
A tool such as a project wiki that makes progress and impediments visible to every interested stakeholder.
6. How many communication channels for 9 people?
9 × 8 / 2 = 36.
7. Give the six questions for planning communication.
Who needs it, what they need, why share it, how best to provide it, when and how often, who has it.
8. What is the weakness of push communication?
It is one-way, so you can't immediately gauge reaction or understanding.
9. What makes a stakeholder “definitive” in the Salience model?
Having all three attributes: power, legitimacy and urgency.
10. Which strategy fits an aware stakeholder who opposes the project?
Treat them as a risk that needs active management.
11. Which report answers “is performance improving or deteriorating?”
The trend report.
