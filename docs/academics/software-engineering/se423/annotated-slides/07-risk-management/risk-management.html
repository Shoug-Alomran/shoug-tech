Risk Management

Identify → analyze → plan → monitor, for threats. Both finals asked for two risk-identification techniques plus how you would assess, mitigate, prioritize and monitor risks, so know at least three techniques, risk exposure (RE = P × I), the probability/impact matrix and the five threat strategies cold.

PredictionQ2

List two software risk identification techniques and explain how you would mitigate, prioritize and monitor the risks (Final 251).

Show answer

Identification: (1) brainstorming with a diverse team, and the Delphi technique where experts answer anonymously to avoid groupthink; (2) checklist analysis built from past projects and the lowest level of the RBS (also: root-cause/5-whys, assumptions analysis, interviews, SWOT). Prioritize: estimate probability and impact, compute risk exposure RE = P × I, and place each risk in the probability-impact matrix; actively manage only the top-10 high risks. Mitigate: choose a strategy per threat: avoid (remove the cause), transfer (contract or insurance), mitigate (more testing, simpler design), or accept with a contingency reserve; give every risk an owner. Monitor: keep a top-10 risk list and the risk register updated, review risks in weekly meetings and status reports, watch triggers, and hold reassessments and audits at milestones.

PredictionQ1

How does a risk assessment matrix help decide which risks need immediate action?

Show answer

It combines each risk’s probability and impact into a single rating (low, medium, high, extreme) on one grid, so risks become comparable. Risks in the high-probability/high-impact cells get formal response plans and owners immediately; medium ones are planned or monitored; low ones go on a watch list. This focuses limited time and money on the few risks that matter most (80/20).
