Overview

Change Management

Integrated change control and the CCB, plan-driven vs. agile change, the types and costs of maintenance, Lehman’s laws, roll-out and migration (flash-cut, parallel, staged, back-out), and how to recover a project in trouble. No past paper had a question on this deck alone, but the scope statement as a change-control baseline, crashing/fast-tracking and the “feature shipped without testing” case all lead back here.

Types of Maintenance

Notes

  1. 1

    Corrective = fix faults.

  2. 2

    Adaptive = new environment (OS, hardware, platform).

  3. 3

    Perfective = new or changed functionality.

  4. 4

    Memory trick C-A-P: Correct it, Adapt it, Perfect it.

  5. 5

    Trap Porting to a new OS is ADAPTIVE, not perfective. Adding a feature is PERFECTIVE.

Slide 47 / 65

Migration Strategies

Notes

  1. 1

    Immediate replacement = fastest, riskiest. Parallel = safest but costly (run both). Staged = one part at a time.

  2. 2

    Even the fastest cut still needs a back-out plan.

  3. 3

    Memory trick Flash = fast, Parallel = safe, Staged = step-by-step.

  4. 4

    Trap Parallel operation MITIGATES risk; it is not the fastest. Immediate replacement is the fastest.
