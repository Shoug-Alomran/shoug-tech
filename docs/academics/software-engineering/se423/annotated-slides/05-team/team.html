Overview

Team

The most examined chapter in SE423: both 251 midterms took most of their short questions from it, and both finals have a whole team-based collaboration question. It has three parts. Team development: Tuckman’s stages, team objectives and structures, and the team needs pyramid (trust → managed conflict → commitment → accountability → results) with RACI and skills matrices as the accountability tools. Team models: business, democratic, chief-programmer, skunk works, SWAT, and why large teams need hierarchy (n(n−1)/2 channels). Managing people: motivation and personality types, cohesiveness and groupthink, staff selection, working environments, shaping project culture, technical vs. adaptive leadership, and reaching consensus.

The instructor’s style: questions ask you to apply an idea to a team situation (“team members disagree: how do you encourage collaboration?”). Saying one word (“negotiation”) is not enough; explain what you do, how, and why.

Team pyramid

Trust → managed conflict → commitment → accountability → results. Each layer needs the one below; trust is the foundation.

Tuckman

Forming → storming (competing for leadership) → norming (agree how to work) → performing → adjourning.

RACI

R does the work, A owns the outcome (exactly one per task), C gives input (two-way), I is kept updated (one-way).

Team models

Business (tech lead + equals, most common), democratic (whole team decides), chief-programmer (surgical), skunk works, SWAT.

Leadership

Technical leadership for problems solvable by known means; adaptive leadership for new or complex ones. Most IT projects need both.

Consensus

Not unanimous agreement: everyone feels heard and the group moves forward committed. Persuade, don’t dictate.

Exam focus

Midterm 251 (both versions): MCQs on Tuckman’s storming stage, the foundation of teamwork and the advantage of a cohesive team; short questions on good-team characteristics, vulnerability-based trust, healthy conflict, the best time to reinforce accountability, business vs. democratic teams, technical vs. adaptive leadership and a RACI table; a case on accountability after an untested release. Finals: commitment, trust and conflict, accountability and transparency (Final 242 Q5), the communication-channel formula (Final 242), a CI/CD consensus decision and a milestone celebration (Final 251 Q5). The instructor’s 2026 midterm asked how a PM encourages collaboration when members disagree.

Notes

  1. 1

    Leadership is the capacity to mobilize a group to solve its most challenging problems; it is wrong to think it is about authority.

  2. 2

    Technical leadership (a.k.a. authority) works when the problem can be solved by known means: the leader directs, instructs and applies best practices.

  3. 3

  4. 4

Slide 72 / 77

Technical vs. adaptive leadership

Notes

  1. 1

    Approach: technical leadership solves problems by known means; adaptive leadership approaches problems that known means can’t solve.

  2. 2

    Motivation driver: technical uses the leader’s personal vision (“here’s what I see for us”); adaptive uses a shared sense of purpose (“why does this matter?”).

  3. 3

    Tool for motivation: technical relies on personality: charisma, expertise, confidence, decisiveness. Adaptive relies on progress: learning, iteration and visible movement toward clarity. In short: technical = “follow me, I know”; adaptive = “let’s figure it out together.”

Exam angle

Past exam · Midterm 251 · V1 · Short Q5 (also V2 Short Q5)Q1

How can a project manager balance technical leadership with adaptive leadership in a software team?

Show answer

First diagnose the problem: can it be solved by known means? When it can (a coding standard, a deployment the team has done before), use technical leadership: give direction from expertise and vision, or delegate it to someone who can lead it. When it can’t (new technology, unclear requirements), use adaptive leadership: frame the purpose, lead with questions, let the team experiment and learn, and motivate through visible progress. Most IT projects mix both, so the PM switches between the two as the situation changes, delegating the known parts and focusing on the unknown ones.

Share an experience from a software project you were involved in where collaboration among team members significantly contributed to its success. Describe the specific strategies or techniques you employed to foster collaboration.

Show answer

Any honest experience earns marks if it names concrete techniques. For example: in a course project building a booking app, we split the work by feature with clear owners, met twice a week for short stand-ups, tracked tasks on a shared board, used pull requests so every change was reviewed by a teammate, and paired up on the hardest integration work. Exam angle

Past exam · Final 251 · Q5.2Q1

You are leading a cross-functional team tasked with selecting a continuous integration/continuous deployment (CI/CD) platform for an upcoming project. The team includes software developers, DevOps engineers, and QA testers, each with distinct expectations regarding automation workflows and pipeline management. Describe your approach to collaboratively choosing the most suitable CI/CD platform.

Show answer

Run the consensus steps. Pose the decision in a kickoff workshop where developers, DevOps engineers and QA testers each list their needs (build speed, deployment control, test automation). Agree the criteria and their weights together: integration with the repository and tools, automation and test support, pipeline flexibility, scalability, security, cost, ease of use and vendor support. Shortlist options, weigh each against the criteria (a weighted scoring table, or a short proof of concept), converge on the one with the most benefit for the project, then work with those who preferred others so they buy in. Don’t just vote or dictate, and watch for passive-aggressive agreement.
