Q6.2–6.4 · Gantt chart: what, advantages, limitations

PredictionQ1

What is a Gantt chart, what does it show, and give two advantages and two limitations.

Compare with the model answer

A Gantt chart (Henry Gantt) is a task table plus a horizontal bar chart against a time axis. Each bar is an activity or WBS phase; it shows start/end dates, durations, planned vs actual progress, and can show milestones (zero-length diamonds, e.g. “requirements signed off”) and dependencies (arrows, e.g. testing starts after coding finishes). Advantages: easy to understand and communicate; easy to update. Limitations: low information density and unwieldy for large projects; dependencies and the critical path are not always visible, so you can’t see the effect of an early or late start.

Q6.1 · how crashing and fast-tracking affect cost and risk

PredictionQ1

How can crashing and fast-tracking affect overall project cost and risk?

Compare with the model answer

Both shorten the schedule without changing scope. Crashing adds resources (overtime, extra staff) to critical-path activities, which raises cost directly, gives diminishing returns, and new people can add communication overhead and quality risk (Brooks’s Law). Fast-tracking runs sequential activities in parallel; cost rises less, but risk rises because work starts on assumptions, causing rework when earlier results change. Neither can compress much more than ~25% (Boehm).

Q2.3 · scheduling techniques and staying on track

PredictionQ1

Identify three scheduling techniques you would use for a 12-month project and how each helps with delays or overlapping activities (Final 242).

Compare with the model answer

(1) WBS plus network diagram with the Critical Path Method: forward/backward pass shows ES/EF/LS/LF and slack, so you know which activities would delay the end date and which have room. (2) Gantt chart: shows overlapping activities, milestones and planned vs actual progress, making slippage visible to everyone. (3) PERT three-point estimates for uncertain tasks (and/or critical chain buffers): gives expected durations and variance, and buffers protect the end date. Resource leveling and fast-tracking/crashing manage overlaps and recover delays.

What does a burndown chart measure and how are its axes labeled? How does it show ahead/behind?

It measures work remaining over time. Y-axis: remaining work (story points, tasks or hours). X-axis: time (days of the sprint or sprints). An ideal straight line goes from total work to zero at the deadline. If the actual line is above the ideal, the team is behind schedule; below means ahead; a flat line means no progress. It gives daily visibility, forecasts completion and gives early warning of slippage.
