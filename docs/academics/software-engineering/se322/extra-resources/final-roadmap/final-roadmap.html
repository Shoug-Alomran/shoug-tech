Look-alikes

These are the pairs and trios that get mixed up. The question under each title is the one that tells them apart.

Architectural style vs architectural pattern vs design pattern

Ask yourselfHow much of the system does it shape?

ConceptTell-tale signExample
Architectural styleThe overall template for a family of systemsLayered, data flow
Architectural patternA reusable solution for the whole system's structureMVC, broker, pipes-and-filters
Design patternA class-level solution inside one subsystemObserver, Builder, Adapter
StudyBreakdownCh 4 L1SlidesCh 4 L1AnnotatedCh 4 L1BreakdownCh 2 L1SlidesCh 2 L1AnnotatedCh 2 L1BreakdownCh 6 L1SlidesCh 6 L1AnnotatedCh 6 L1
Quick testYou choose Observer so the views refresh when the model changes. Which level are you working at?

Design pattern: it organizes classes inside the system. MVC, the architecture, happens to use it.

Architecture vs detailed design

Ask yourselfAre you looking at the box from outside or opening it?

ConceptTell-tale signExample
ArchitectureBlack box: major components and connectors, mostly quality requirementsComponent diagram, 4+1 views
Detailed designWhite box: classes, algorithms and interfaces inside one componentClass and sequence diagrams
StudyBreakdownCh 1SlidesCh 1AnnotatedCh 1BreakdownCh 2 L1SlidesCh 2 L1AnnotatedCh 2 L1BreakdownCh 5 L1SlidesCh 5 L1AnnotatedCh 5 L1
Quick testDeciding that the payment service talks to the order service through a broker: architecture or detailed design?

Architecture: it is about components and how they connect.

Factory Method vs Abstract Factory vs Builder

Abstract FactorySeveral related products that must matchPC / Mac / Linux test factories
BuilderOne complex object assembled in the same stepsDirector: addCPU, addRAM, addStorage

ConceptTell-tale signExample
AbstractionFocus on essentials, defer detailsFunctional SEND, data MESSAGE
EncapsulationEnforces the abstraction by hiding internalsPrivate fields, public methods
Interface vs implementationSeveral implementations behind one stable interfaceList: ArrayList, LinkedList

Functional requirement vs quality attribute vs tactic

Ask yourselfWhat it does, how well, or the design decision that achieves it?

ConceptTell-tale signExample
Functional requirementWhat the system shall doUsers can reset their password
Quality attributeHow well, judged on the whole systemAvailable 99.99% of the time
TacticA design decision that controls an attribute's responseHeartbeat, active redundancy

Trade-off pointAffects several attributes in opposite directionsEncryption: security up, performance down
