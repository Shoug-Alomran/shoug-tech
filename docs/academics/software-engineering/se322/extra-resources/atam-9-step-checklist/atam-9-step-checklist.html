Chapter 08 · Architecture Evaluation

The ATAM 9-step checklist

Every step of the Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method — what happens, who leads it, and what it produces. Tick each step once you can explain it without looking, then test yourself at the bottom.

The cast

Three participant groups (+ the architect)

Evaluation team3–5 experienced architects, external to the project. Roles: team leader, evaluation leader, scenario scribe, proceedings scribe, questioner.
Project decision makersPeople with authority to change the project: project manager, customer, manager.
Lead architectPart of the project side. Presents the architecture and walks scenarios through it.
Architecture stakeholdersDevelopers, testers, integrators, maintainers, performance engineers, users. Join in Phase 2.
Evaluators · Project deciders · Architecture stakeholders = EPA
The three groups. Remember that the stakeholders only arrive at Step 7.
The 9 steps

Checklist

3 Presents → Identify · Generate · Analyze → Brainstorm · Analyze → Present
1–3 Present (method, business, architecture) · 4 Identify · 5 Generate tree · 6 Analyze · 7 Brainstorm · 8 Analyze again · 9 Present results
0 / 9 steps mastered
Phase 1 · Steps 1–6Evaluation team + project decision makers · 1–2 days, then a 2–3 week break
Phase 2 · Steps 7–9Evaluation team + decision makers + stakeholders · 2 days

Lightweight ATAM: for small, low-risk projects. Takes half a day to a day, internal staff only. Step 1, Step 7 and Step 8 take 0 hours: everyone already knows the method, internal stakeholders give their scenarios in Step 5, and all analysis happens in Step 6, which is the longest step (2–3 hrs).

Required ATAM outputs

  • Documentation of the selected architecture, mainly the SAD (Software Architecture Document)
  • An evaluation report that recaps the process, captures the scenario analysis, and explains the candidate architectures and the reasoning behind the choice
