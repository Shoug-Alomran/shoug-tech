Final Exam Cheat Sheet

Lecture 1: Enterprise Architecture: Introduction, Purpose and Elements

Definition

  • Holistic analysis, design, planning, implementation
  • EA = Strategy + Business + Technology

Six core elements (BAGS FM)

  • Best practices, Artifacts
  • Governance, Standards
  • Framework, Methodology

Management program

  • Strategic alignment
  • Standardized policy
  • Decision support
  • Resource oversight

Method provides

  • Current views (as-is)
  • Future views (to-be)
  • EA Management Plan

Meta-discipline

  • Umbrella for best practices
  • Resolves battle of best practices

Do not fall for

  • Meta doesn't mean it replaces the others

EA3 Cube

Five levels

  • Products & Services

Six analysis elements

  • Framework, Components
  • Current, Future
  • Management Plan, Threads

Terms

Lecture 4: EA as a Management Program

Program vs project

  • Project: ends on delivery
  • EA: never finishes
  • Needs governance, staff, budget

Four parts

  • 1 Program Management: how it runs
  • 2 Current Summary: what exists
  • 3 Future Summary: where we go
  • 4 Glossary & Refs: what terms mean

Horizons

  • Tactical: 1-2 years
  • Strategic: 3-10 years

Do not fall for

  • "We finished the EA"

Lecture 6: EA Value and Risk

Three pillars

  • Planning
  • Decision-making
  • Communication

Ten value factors

  • Shorter planning cycles
  • Better planning meetings
  • Shorter decision cycles
  • Better reference information
  • Fewer duplicate resources
  • Reduced re-work
  • Better resource integration
  • Fewer people in a process
  • Improved communication
  • Reduced cycle time

Six costs

  • Staffing & administration
  • Stakeholder sessions
  • Repository technology
  • Documentation
  • Modeling tools
  • Ongoing updates

Cost pattern

  • Initial setup above maintenance
  • Labour is largest

Four risks (FLAgS)

  • Financial
  • Loss of personnel
  • Acceptance
  • Schedule

Do not fall for

  • Not a one-time activity

Lecture 7: Models of Organizations and Enterprises

Leavitt Diamond

  • Structure, People, Tasks, Technology
  • Change one, affect all

Parsons/Thompson

  • Institutional: society, legitimacy
  • Managerial: coordinates
  • Technical: core work

ONM

  • Executive Team: direction
  • Functional Teams: semi-autonomous
  • Independent Workers: freelancers
  • Flatter, faster

Change management

  • Involve stakeholders
  • Communicate regularly
  • Allow input
  • Manage expectations

Segmented approach

  • One segment at a time
  • Lower cost, lower risk

Do not fall for

  • The ONM still has management

Lecture 8: Business Continuity Planning

BCP definition

  • Proactive; critical functions and threats
  • Training, testing, maintenance
  • Reviewed at least annually

Life cycle

  • Identify, Analyze, Design
  • Execute, Measure

Four phases

  • Mitigation, Preparedness
  • Response, Recovery

DR

  • Optimal where two costs cross
  • Hot: full, immediate, costly
  • Cold: empty, slow, cheap

Senior management

  • Defines worst-case scenarios
  • Approves funding and staffing
  • Ensures testing and training

Lecture 9: Linking Strategy, Business and Technology

Strategy elements

  • Goals: what we want
  • Initiatives: what we do
  • Measures: did it work

Business level

  • Support for goals
  • Documented activities
  • Supporting technologies

Direction

  • Strategy → Business → Technology
  • Top-down and bottom-up together
  • Technology must not drive strategy

Do not fall for

  • An initiative isn't a goal

Lecture 10: EA Technology Planning and Intelligent Enterprise Systems

Five technologies

  • Web services: shared protocols
  • Cloud: on-demand shared pool
  • SOA: reusable services
  • Virtualization: many on one machine
  • Grid: dispersed resources together

Cloud control

  • IaaS: OS and apps
  • PaaS: your app
  • SaaS: user settings only

Examples

  • IaaS: Amazon EC2
  • PaaS: Google App Engine
  • SaaS: Gmail, Office 365

SOA

  • Before: siloed, brittle
  • After: shared, interoperable

Digital transformation

  • Driven by Big Data and IoT
  • Five architect traits

Do not fall for

  • More control means more responsibility
