The big picture

In one sentenceEA fails when it ignores people, so three classic models show how organizations are structured, and how to bring people along when things change.

You'll be given a scenario and asked to apply a model: which components of the Leavitt Diamond are affected, or which Parsons/Thompson level a task belongs to. The change-management ideas apply to any workplace.

Where this fits

Lecture 6's "lack of acceptance" risk is mainly a people problem. This lecture explains how to reduce it. The models also map onto the cube.

By the end you can

  • Explain why people matter in EA
  • Apply the Leavitt Diamond to a change
  • Describe the Parsons/Thompson levels
  • Describe the Organizational Network Model
  • Define enterprise and segment, and list four ways to reduce resistance

Lesson 1

Leavitt Diamond: pull one corner, all move

Must knowSlides 3-10

EA is as much about people and social interaction as about processes and resources. An architect has to understand how occupants live, not just draw walls and wiring.

Harold Leavitt's 1965 model says a change in any of four components will affect the others.

Component Includes
Structure How labour, authority, decisions and communication are organized
Technology IT, buildings, machines, hardware and software
People Employees and others associated with the organization
Tasks The work being done
Memory trick

Leavitt's SPoTT

Structure, People, Tasks, Technology. Spot the four corners.

Worked example

A new logistics IT system (slide 9)

It enters through Technology. People need training. Tasks change, since order tracking is now automated. Structure may shift when a new team takes over.

Try itGovernment introduces new VAT rules. How does this hit the diamond?

It comes from the surroundings and affects tasks (new calculations), technology (billing systems), people (training), and potentially structure (a new tax compliance role).

Remember
  • EA is as much about people as it is about processes
  • Leavitt Diamond: structure, technology, people, tasks
  • A change in one affects the others
  • External changes can enter from the surrounding world
Lesson 2

Parsons/Thompson: three levels

Must knowSlides 11-15

Talcott Parsons (1950s) and James Thompson (1960s) describe three levels:

Level Purpose Spherical view Maps to EA3 Cube
Institutional Relates to the wider society; establishes legitimacy and higher-level support Outermost ring Goals & Initiatives
Managerial Mediates between organization and task environment; coordinates internal affairs Middle ring Products & Services, Data
Technical Performs the production work, protecting the core Innermost core Systems, Networks
Think of it like this

A hospital

Institutional: the board talking to regulators and the public. Managerial: administrators setting schedules for doctors and buying supplies. Technical: surgeons operating.

Memory trick

I'M Technical

Institutional, Managerial, Technical, from outside to inside.

It's still used: slide 15 says it remains an authoritative reference for developing EA views of structure and process.

Remember
  • Institutional level relates to society and establishes legitimacy
  • Managerial level mediates and coordinates internal resources
  • Technical level performs the core work
  • The model is still a valid reference for EA views
Lesson 3

The Organizational Network Model

Must knowSlides 16-21

Global telecommunications made location less relevant for some work, creating regional and global organizations and self-employed knowledge workers. The ONM reflects that. It's a flatter structure of cooperative networks.

Role Does what
Executive Team (centre) Sets policy and goals, approves resources, provides general direction
Functional Teams Semi-autonomous; manage programs, lines of business and projects
Independent Workers Individuals inside or outside the boundary, such as freelancers

Benefits: fewer unneeded layers of management, faster response by creating or changing teams, and the ability to reach across organizations. Functional teams correspond to lines of business in the cube.

Exam trap

The ONM still has management

It's flatter, not leaderless. The executive team still sets direction.

Remember
  • ONM is based on cooperative networks of workers and semi-autonomous teams
  • The executive team sets policy, goals and direction
  • It removes unneeded management layers for flexibility
  • Functional teams relate to lines of business
Lesson 4

Enterprises, culture and change

Must knowSlides 22-28

Organization vs enterprise: both have culture, structure, goals and resources. An enterprise can be a subset of an organization or span several organizations.

The segmented approach builds EA one segment at a time. Each segment is a complete stand-alone architecture, and they aggregate into one picture. That means lower cost for a first version and a lower-risk rollout.

Culture is an amalgamation of values, beliefs, habits and preferences. An EA must reflect it to be realistic.

Change will happen anyway, but without EA it happens in a disjointed way. EA makes change driven by strategy and business requirements, and less by new technology.

People resist change because it feels threatening, and giving them some control limits this.

Way to reduce resistance Meaning
Involve stakeholders Give them "a place at the table"
Communicate regularly Formal and informal
Allow for input Let them shape the change
Manage expectations Be clear about what will really happen
Memory trick

ICE the resistance, with Input

Involve, Communicate, manage Expectations, and allow Input.

Change management is setting expectations and involving stakeholders so they have some control over the change, and so may accept it more readily.

Remember
  • An enterprise can be part of one organization or span several
  • Segmented approach builds a piece at a time for lower cost and risk
  • EA makes change driven by strategy rather than technology
  • Four ways to reduce resistance: involve, communicate, allow input, manage expectations
