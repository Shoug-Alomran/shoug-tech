Final Exam Cheat Sheet

Chapter 3: Demand and Supply

Curves

  • Own price: move along
  • Anything else: shift

Demand shifters (RIPE)

  • Related goods
  • Income
  • Preferences, Population
  • Expected prices, Expected income

Supply shifters (FRETS, N)

  • Factor prices
  • Related goods
  • Expected prices
  • Technology
  • Suppliers
  • Nature

Equilibrium

  • Qd = Qs
  • Below: shortage, price up
  • Above: surplus, price down

Shifts

  • D up: P up, Q up
  • S up: P down, Q up
  • Same direction: Q sure
  • Opposite: P sure

Do not fall for

  • Expected price works opposite ways

Chapter 4: Elasticity

Formula

  • ε = %ΔQ ÷ %ΔP
  • %Δ = Δ ÷ average × 100
  • Report as positive

Ranges

  • 0: perfectly inelastic (vertical)
  • Below 1: inelastic
  • 1: unit elastic
  • Above 1: elastic
  • Infinite: perfectly elastic (horizontal)

Total revenue

  • Elastic: P up, TR down
  • Inelastic: P up, TR up
  • Unit: TR unchanged

Determinants

  • Close substitutes
  • Share of income
  • Time to adjust

Other elasticities

  • Income +: normal, −: inferior
  • Cross +: substitutes, −: complements
  • Supply rises with time

Do not fall for

  • Slope isn't elasticity

Chapter 5: Efficiency and Equity

Surplus

  • CS = value − price
  • PS = price − marginal cost
  • Triangle: ½ × base × height
  • Total = CS + PS

Efficiency

  • D = MB, S = MC
  • Efficient where MB = MC
  • Under or overproduction: deadweight loss

Obstacles

  • Regulations, taxes, subsidies
  • Externalities, public goods
  • Monopoly, transaction costs

Fairness

  • Results view: utilitarianism
  • Rules view: symmetry principle

Do not fall for

  • Efficiency isn't fairness

Chapter 6: Government Actions in Markets

Ceilings and floors

  • Ceiling binds below equilibrium: shortage
  • Floor binds above equilibrium: surplus
  • Both: deadweight loss, unfair

Tax method

  • Set (buyers' price − sellers' price) = tax
  • Solve for Q, then both prices
  • DWL = ½ × tax × ΔQ

Incidence

  • Inelastic demand: buyers pay
  • Inelastic supply: sellers pay
  • Perfectly inelastic: no DWL

Quotas and subsidies

  • Quota: P up, MC down, underproduction
  • Subsidy: P down, MC up, overproduction

Illegal goods

  • Seller penalty: S left, P up
  • Buyer penalty: D left, P down

Do not fall for

  • No deadweight loss in one case

Chapter 8: Utility and Demand

Budget

  • Income up: parallel outward shift
  • Price up: rotates inward

Utility

  • MU = ΔTU ÷ ΔQ
  • MU diminishes

Best choice

  • Spend all income
  • MU₁/P₁ = MU₂/P₂
  • Huda: 2 novels, 4 cups, 252 units

Predictions

  • Own price down: Qd up
  • Income up: normal goods up

Paradox of value

  • Water: big TU, small MU
  • Diamonds: small TU, big MU
  • Price follows MU

Behavioural

  • Bounded rationality, willpower, self-interest
  • Endowment effect

Do not fall for

  • TU rises while MU falls

Chapter 9: Possibilities, Preferences and Choices

Budget line

  • PsQs + PfQf = Y
  • Real income = Y ÷ P (intercept)
  • Relative price = Ps ÷ Pf (slope)
  • Price change: rotates
  • Income change: parallel shift

Indifference curves

  • Equal satisfaction
  • Higher is better
  • MRS = steepness, diminishes
  • Substitutes: straight
  • Complements: L-shaped

Best choice

  • On budget line
  • Highest curve reached
  • MRS = relative price

Rakan

  • C: 4 sessions, 6 kg
  • J: 9 sessions (price 8)
  • K: 6 sessions (income 96)
  • Substitution +2, income +3

Do not fall for

  • Upward-sloping demand is extremely rare

Chapter 11: Output and Costs

Time frames

  • Short run: plant fixed
  • Long run: all variable
  • Sunk costs are irrelevant

Product

  • MP = ΔTP ÷ ΔL
  • AP = TP ÷ L
  • MP crosses AP at max AP
  • Diminishing returns: fixed plant

Cost formulas

  • TC = TFC + TVC
  • MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ
  • ATC = AFC + AVC

Shapes

  • TFC flat; AFC falls
  • AVC, ATC U-shaped
  • MC cuts both at minimum

Qamar

  • Min AVC 24 at 30 cakes
  • Min ATC 50 at 48 cakes

Shifts

  • Rent up: TFC, TC, AFC, ATC
  • Wage up: also MC, AVC

Do not fall for

  • A fixed-cost rise doesn't change MC

Chapter 12: Perfect Competition

Conditions

  • Many firms, identical products
  • Free entry and exit
  • No advantage, full information

Firm's demand

  • Horizontal at market price
  • MR = P

Output rule

  • MR = MC, MC rising
  • Al-Bustan: 7 boxes, profit 126

Results

  • P > ATC: profit
  • P = ATC: break even (41)
  • P < ATC: loss
  • Profit = (P − ATC) × Q

Shutdown

  • At minimum AVC (34, 5 boxes)
  • Loss = TFC there
  • P < AVC: shut down

Do not fall for

  • The market's demand isn't horizontal
  • A loss doesn't always mean shutting down

Chapter 13: Monopoly

Monopoly features

  • One firm
  • No close substitutes
  • Barrier to entry

Barriers

  • Natural: economies of scale
  • Ownership: key resource
  • Legal: franchise, licence, patent, copyright

Revenue

  • Must cut price on all units
  • MR < P

Graph steps

  • MR = MC: quantity
  • Up to demand: price
  • ATC at that quantity
  • Profit = (P − ATC) × Q

Sahra Tours

  • 4 tours, 120 SAR
  • ATC 75, profit 180

Do not fall for

  • The price isn't read where MR = MC

Chapter 14: Monopolistic Competition

Features

  • Many firms
  • Differentiated products
  • Free entry and exit
  • Compete on quality, price, marketing

Short run

  • MR = MC, price from demand
  • Busy: profit 3,200
  • Slow: loss 200
  • Profit = (P − ATC) × Q

Long run

  • Profit attracts entry
  • Loss causes exit
  • Firms break even

Comparison

  • All use MR = MC
  • Only monopoly keeps profit
  • PC demand flat; others slope down

Do not fall for

  • Monopolistic competition doesn't keep profits
