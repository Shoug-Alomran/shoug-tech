EXAM PLAN

How each question type is marked, and how to answer it

The practice major mirrors the format: mostly multiple choice, plus a few short answers that ask you to explain and connect.

A

Multiple choice

  1. Underline the trigger word: own price, substitute, inferior, below equilibrium, elastic.
  2. Decide the curve or the formula before reading the options.
  3. Cross out options that confuse a movement with a shift, or MR with price.
  4. For a number, compute it in the margin; most wrong options are the inverse or the forgotten average.
B

Short answer

  1. Define the term in one sentence from the slides.
  2. Apply it to the case in the question (name the curve, the shift, the direction).
  3. Show the numbers or the steps if there are any.
  4. Conclude with the effect on price, quantity, revenue or profit.
C

Calculations

  1. Write the formula in words first.
  2. Show each step on its own line; never round in the middle (the handwriting).
  3. Report elasticity as a positive number, and interpret it.
  4. State the unit (SAR, units, %).
CheckElasticity with the midpoint method: going up or down must give the same answer.

CHAPTER 02 · THE ECONOMIC PROBLEM

PPF, opportunity cost, efficiency and trade

BreakdownMind mapChapter quiz

The slide notes say the questions are MCQ, and that you must understand PPF graphs, opportunity cost calculations, and comparative versus absolute advantage.

1

Reading the PPF

The PPF is the boundary between what can and can't be produced with the resources and technology available.

PointStatusWhy
On the PPFAttainable and efficientAll resources used
InsideAttainable, inefficientUnused or misallocated resources
OutsideUnattainableNot enough resources

It bows outward because resources aren't equally productive in every activity, so opportunity cost increases.

F1

Opportunity cost

OC of X = amount of the other good given up ÷ amount of X gained
Pizza / cola table (slides)
E (4 pizzas, 5 cola) → F (5, 0) Pizza +1, cola −5 → OC of 1 pizza = 5 ÷ 1 = 5 cans OC of 1 can = 1 ÷ 5 = 1/5 pizza (the inverse)
MnemonicGive-up over Get. The cost of one good is the inverse of the cost of the other.
2

Allocative efficiency

Every point on the PPF is production-efficient; only one is allocatively efficient: where MB = MC.

IfThen
MB > MCProduce more
MB < MCProduce less
MB = MCAllocative efficiency

MC rises (rising opportunity cost); MB falls (each extra unit is worth less — the handwriting's "one car is essential, a fourth isn't").

TrapProduction efficiency (on the PPF) isn't allocative efficiency (the one point where MB = MC).
F2

Comparative vs absolute advantage

Comparative
Producing at a lower opportunity cost
Absolute
Producing more with the same resources

OC of 1 smoothie1 salad5 salads

Liz specializes in smoothies, Joe in salads. Trading at 2 salads per smoothie, each gains 5 smoothies and 5 salads an hour and both consume outside their own PPF.

MnemonicComparative is about costs; Absolute is about amounts.
TrapNobody has a comparative advantage in everything: the costs are inverses, so each person is cheaper at something.
3

Growth and coordination

Growth comes from technological change and capital accumulation (including human capital). Its cost is less consumption now; the PPF shifts out later.

Four coordinating institutions: firms, markets, property rights, money.

Circular flow: households sell factors (labour, land, capital, entrepreneurship) in factor markets and buy in goods markets; money flows the opposite way to goods. The handwriting: label every flow.

Shifters, equilibrium and predicting changes

1

Movement along vs shift

Law of demand: higher price, smaller quantity demanded (substitution effect + income effect). Law of supply: higher price, greater quantity supplied (rising MC).

Trap"Price rose so demand fell" is wrong: price changes quantity demanded, a movement along the curve.

PRACTICE

Exam-style questions with worked answers

Answer each one on paper first, then open the answer. Show all answers at the top opens every one.

Calculation · Ch 2

From D (3 pizzas, 9 cola) to E (4 pizzas, 5 cola), what is the opportunity cost of one pizza, and of one can of cola?

Show answer

Pizza rises by 1 and cola falls by 4. OC of a pizza = 4 ÷ 1 = 4 cans. OC of a can = 1 ÷ 4 = 1/4 pizza (the inverse).

Calculation · Ch 2

Sara can bake 20 cakes or 10 pies an hour; Omar 12 cakes or 4 pies. Who has the comparative advantage in each good?

Show answer

OC of a cake: Sara 10/20 = 0.5 pie; Omar 4/12 = 1/3 pie → Omar has the comparative advantage in cakes.

OC of a pie: Sara 2 cakes; Omar 3 cakes → Sara has it in pies. Sara has the absolute advantage in both.

Short answer · Ch 2

Explain how specialization and trade make both Liz and Joe better off.

Show answer

Each specializes in the good with the lower opportunity cost (Liz smoothies, Joe salads) and trades at a price between their two costs. Both end up consuming combinations outside their own PPF: 5 more smoothies and 5 more salads each per hour.

Headline · Ch 3

"The price of coffee rises sharply." What happens to the demand for tea, and for sugar used in coffee?

Show answer

Tea is a substitute: demand for tea increases (shifts right). Sugar is a complement: demand for sugar decreases (shifts left).

Calculation · Ch 3

Qd = 19 − 3P and Qs = 1 + 3P. Find the equilibrium, then the situation at P = 4.

Show answer

19 − 3P = 1 + 3P → P = 3, Q = 10. At P = 4: Qd = 7, Qs = 13 → a surplus of 6, so the price falls back toward 3.

Short answer · Ch 3

Explain the difference between a change in demand and a change in quantity demanded.

Show answer

A change in the good's own price causes a change in quantity demanded, a movement along the curve. A change in any other factor (RIPE) causes a change in demand, a shift of the whole curve.

Calculation · Ch 4

Price rises from 8 to 12 and quantity falls from 50 to 30. Find ε, classify demand, and check with total revenue.

Show answer

%ΔQ = 20 ÷ 40 = 50%. %ΔP = 4 ÷ 10 = 40%. ε = 50 ÷ 40 = 1.25 → elastic.

TR falls from 8 × 50 = 400 to 12 × 30 = 360: price up, revenue down, which confirms elastic demand.

Calculation · Ch 4

Income rises 10% and the quantity of bus rides demanded falls 5%. Income elasticity? What kind of good?

Show answer

−5 ÷ 10 = −0.5. Negative, so bus rides are an inferior good.

Short answer · Ch 4

State three factors that influence the price elasticity of demand.

Show answer

The closeness of substitutes, the proportion of income spent on the good, and the time elapsed since the price change.
