The big picture

In one sentenceA consumer gets the most satisfaction from a budget by spending it so that the last riyal on every good brings the same extra utility, and that rule explains why demand curves slope down.

This chapter is on Major 2. Handwriting on slide 1 says it appears in section D with no graphing, and the textbook slides point to book questions on page 232, Q1 to 13. Expect calculations from utility tables, plus the water and diamonds paradox as an MCQ.

Lesson 1

The budget line

Must knowSlides 3-5

Huda has 96 SAR a month. A novel costs 24 SAR and a cup of juice 12 SAR.

Her budget equation
24N + 12J = 96

She can afford at most 4 novels (96 ÷ 24) or 8 cups (96 ÷ 12), or any combination on or inside the line joining them.

Change Effect on the line
Income rises Shifts outward, same slope
A price rises Rotates inward on that good's axis, and the slope changes
Worked example

Slide 5

With 120 SAR she could buy up to 5 novels or 10 cups: a parallel shift out. If juice rose to 24 SAR, she could afford only 4 cups instead of 8.

Remember
  • The budget line shows the limit of consumption possibilities
  • A rise in income shifts it outward and parallel
  • A rise in a price rotates it inward
  • The budget line shows what's possible; preferences decide the choice
Lesson 2

Total and marginal utility

Must knowSlides 6-8

Marginal utility = change in total utility ÷ change in quantity.

Juice cups 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8
TU 48 90 120 144 162 174 183 189
MU 48 42 30 24 18 12 9 6
Novels 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8
TU 60 108 150 186 216 240 258 270
MU 60 48 42 36 30 24 18 12

Each extra cup adds less than the one before: diminishing marginal utility.

Think of it like this

The first glass of water when thirsty

The first glass is wonderful. The fifth is barely welcome. Total utility keeps rising, but each extra glass adds less.

Exam trap

TU rises while MU falls

Diminishing marginal utility doesn't mean total utility is falling. TU keeps climbing, just more slowly.

Remember
  • MU = change in TU ÷ change in quantity
  • Marginal utility diminishes as consumption increases
  • Total utility rises at a decreasing rate
Lesson 3

Finding the best choice

Must knowSlides 9-15

Method 1: spreadsheet

List the combinations that spend all 96 SAR and add up total utility.

Row Novels TU novels Juice TU juice Total
A 0 0 8 189 189
B 1 60 6 174 234
C 2 108 4 144 252
D 3 150 2 90 240
E 4 186 0 0 186

Consumer equilibrium: 2 novels and 4 cups, with total utility 252.

Method 2: the marginal rule

Utility-maximizing rule
Spend all income, and set MUN/PN = MUJ/PJ

At row C: MUN/PN = 48/24 = 2, and MUJ/PJ = 24/12 = 2. They are equal, so row C is the best choice.

Memory trick

Bang for your Buck is Balanced

MU per riyal is the "bang for your buck". At the best choice, every good gives the same bang per riyal.

Exam tip

Why the rule works

If juice gives more utility per riyal than novels, move a riyal from novels to juice. Total utility rises. Keep moving until the ratios are equal.

Remember
  • Find the affordable combination with the highest total utility
  • Rule: spend all income and equalize marginal utility per riyal
  • Huda's equilibrium: 2 novels and 4 cups, total utility 252
Lesson 4

Predictions

Must knowSlides 16-24
Situation P novel P juice Income Novels Juice
Start 24 12 96 2 4
Novel price falls 12 12 96 5 3
Juice price rises 12 24 96 6 1
Income rises 12 12 120 6 4
Step by step

The novel price falls from 24 to 12

  1. At the old choice of 2 novels and 4 cups, 24 SAR is left unspent.
  2. MUN/PN is now 48/12 = 4, more than MUJ/PJ = 2.
  3. Buy more novels, which lowers MUN, and less juice, which raises MUJ.
  4. New equilibrium: 5 novels and 3 cups, both ratios 2.5, total utility 336.

What this predicts:

  • Own price falls → quantity demanded rises. Demand curves slope down.
  • Another good's price changes → demand for this good shifts. Here, cheaper novels lowered juice demand.
  • Income rises → demand for a normal good rises. Both novels and juice are normal for Huda.
Remember
  • A lower price raises the quantity demanded, so demand slopes down
  • A change in another good's price shifts demand
  • Higher income increases demand for normal goods
Lesson 5

The paradox of value and behavioural economics

Must knowSlides 25-29

Why is water, essential to life, cheap, while diamonds are expensive? Distinguish total utility from marginal utility.

Water Diamonds
Quantity we consume Huge Tiny
Total utility Large Small
Marginal utility Small Large
Price Low High

Price matches marginal utility, not total utility. Since we use so much water, the last litre adds little.

Memory trick

Price follows the Margin

Price tracks the margin. Water's total value is huge, but its last unit is cheap.

Behavioural economics studies how the limits of the human brain affect decisions:

Bound Meaning Example
Bounded rationality Limited ability to compute, so people rely on rules of thumb Copying others' choices
Bounded willpower Making choices we know we'll regret Scrolling instead of studying
Bounded self-interest Sometimes suppressing self-interest Donating to charity

The endowment effect: valuing something more just because you own it. Neuroeconomics studies brain activity during economic decisions.

Remember
  • Price reflects marginal utility, not total utility
  • Water: large total utility, small marginal utility
  • Three bounds: rationality, willpower, self-interest
  • Endowment effect: valuing what you own more highly
