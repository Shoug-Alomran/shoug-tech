Lesson 1

The PPF and opportunity cost

Must knowSlides 3-17

The PPF is the boundary between the combinations of goods that can be produced and those that can't.

Possibility Pizzas (millions) Cola (millions of cans)
A 0 15
B 1 14
C 2 12
D 3 9
E 4 5
F 5 0
Point Status Why
On the PPF Attainable and efficient Using all resources
Inside the PPF (Z) Attainable but inefficient Resources unused or misallocated
Outside the PPF (W) Unattainable Not enough resources
Step by step

Calculate opportunity cost from the table

  1. Moving from E to F: pizzas rise by 1 million, cola falls by 5 million cans.
  2. Opportunity cost of 1 pizza = 5 million ÷ 1 million = 5 cans of cola.
  3. Going the other way, from F to E: cola rises by 5 million and pizzas fall by 1 million.
  4. Opportunity cost of 1 can of cola = 1 ÷ 5 = 1/5 pizza.
Memory trick

Give-up over Get

Opportunity cost of X = quantity of the other good given up, divided by the quantity of X you get.

Exam tip

Inverses

The opportunity cost of cola is the inverse of the opportunity cost of pizza. If one pizza costs 5 colas, then one cola costs 1/5 of a pizza.

Why does it bow outward? Resources aren't equally productive in all activities. The first pizzas use workers good at pizza. Later, you pull in your best cola workers and give up much more cola for each extra pizza. So opportunity cost increases.

Try itFrom A to B and from B to C, what's the opportunity cost of a pizza?

A to B: 1 can of cola per pizza. B to C: 2 cans. It's increasing, as expected.

Remember
  • The PPF is the boundary between what can and can't be produced
  • On the PPF is efficient, inside is inefficient, outside is unattainable
  • Opportunity cost = amount given up ÷ amount gained
  • Cost of one good is the inverse of the cost of the other
  • The PPF bows outward because opportunity cost increases
Lesson 2

Which point on the PPF? Allocative efficiency

Must knowSlides 18-35

All points on the PPF are production-efficient. To choose among them, compare costs and benefits.

  • Marginal cost (MC): the opportunity cost of one more unit. It increases as you make more.
  • Marginal benefit (MB): what people will give up for one more unit. It decreases as you have more. The handwriting's example: if you only own one car, it's essential; a fourth isn't.

Allocative efficiency occurs where MB = MC: you produce the most-valued combination.

If Then
MB > MC Producing too little; make more
MB < MC Producing too much; make less
MB = MC Allocative efficiency
Exam trap

Production efficiency isn't allocative efficiency

Every point on the PPF is production-efficient. Only one is allocatively efficient.

Remember
  • Marginal cost rises and marginal benefit falls
  • Allocative efficiency occurs where marginal benefit equals marginal cost
  • A point on the PPF may still not be allocatively efficient
Lesson 3

Gains from trade

Must knowSlides 36-60

A person has a comparative advantage in an activity if they can do it at a lower opportunity cost than anyone else. An absolute advantage means being more productive overall.

Per hour Liz Joe
Max smoothies 30 6
Max salads 30 30
Opportunity cost of 1 smoothie 1 salad 5 salads
Opportunity cost of 1 salad 1 smoothie 1/5 smoothie

Liz has the comparative advantage in smoothies (1 salad beats 5). Joe has it in salads (1/5 smoothie beats 1).

Step by step

Specialize and trade

  1. Before trade. Liz makes 15 smoothies and 15 salads. Joe makes 5 and 5.
  2. Specialize. Liz makes 30 smoothies. Joe makes 30 salads.
  3. Trade. Liz sells Joe 10 smoothies and buys 20 salads (a price of 2 salads per smoothie).
  4. After. Liz has 20 smoothies and 20 salads. Joe has 10 and 10.
  5. Gains. Each gains 5 smoothies and 5 salads an hour. Both end up outside their own PPF.
Think of it like this

The surgeon and the receptionist

A surgeon might also type faster than the receptionist. That's absolute advantage. She still doesn't type her own letters: each hour of typing costs far too much surgery. That's comparative advantage.

Memory trick

Compare Costs, Absolute Amounts

Comparative is about costs. Absolute is about amounts.

Noted in class

Comparative advantage in real life

The handwriting's example: Saudi Arabia specializes in oil, at a low opportunity cost, then trades with other countries.

Exam trap

A person can't have comparative advantage in everything

Even if someone has absolute advantage in both goods, as Liz nearly does, each person has a comparative advantage in something, because costs are inverses of each other.

Remember
  • Comparative advantage is the lower opportunity cost
  • Absolute advantage is greater productivity
  • Specializing and trading lets both parties consume outside their PPFs
  • A price between the two opportunity costs benefits both
Lesson 4

Growth and coordination

Must knowSlides 61-72

Economic growth is the expansion of production possibilities. Two key factors are technological change and capital accumulation (including human capital). The handwriting calls this "most important".

Growth has a cost: resources used to build capital can't make consumption goods today. Sacrifice pizzas today for more ovens, and the PPF shifts further out in future.

Coordination relies on four institutions: firms, markets, property rights and money.

Real goods flow the opposite way. Households supply labor, land, capital and entrepreneurship through factor markets, and firms supply goods and services through goods markets.

Remember
  • Growth comes from technological change and capital accumulation
  • Growth costs current consumption
  • Four coordinating institutions: firms, markets, property rights, money
  • Households supply factors, and firms supply goods

Quick reference

PPF

  • On: efficient
  • Inside: inefficient
  • Outside: unattainable
  • Bows out: rising opportunity cost

Opportunity cost

  • Given up ÷ gained
  • Inverse for the other good

Efficiency

  • MB falls, MC rises
  • MB = MC: allocative efficiency

Trade

  • Comparative: lower opportunity cost
  • Absolute: more output
  • Specialize in comparative advantage
  • Both end up outside their PPF

Growth

  • Technology and capital
  • Cost: less consumption now

Coordination

  • Firms, markets
  • Property rights, money
