The big picture

In one sentencePut the budget line together with indifference curves, and the best choice is where the line just touches the highest reachable curve, so the marginal rate of substitution equals the relative price.

This chapter is on Major 2. The handwriting on slide 1 says to practise calculations and graphs, and that it will be easy if you do. It's the graph version of Chapter 8, and the split between substitution and income effects is a common question.

Where this fits

It reaches Chapter 8's conclusions without measuring utility. The budget line is the same idea, and indifference curves replace utility tables.

  • Write the budget equation and find real income and relative price
  • Explain how price and income changes move the budget line
  • Define indifference curves and a diminishing MRS
  • Find the best affordable choice

Lesson 1

The budget line, again

Must knowSlides 3-9

Rakan has 144 SAR a month. A swimming session costs 18 SAR and fruit costs 12 SAR per kg.

Budget equation
PsQs + PfQf = Y → 18Qs + 12Qf = 144
Qf = Y/Pf − (Ps/Pf)Qs → Qf = 12 − 1.5Qs
Term Formula Rakan On the graph
Real income (in fruit) Y / Pf 144 / 12 = 12 kg The y-intercept
Relative price of a session Ps / Pf 18 / 12 = 1.5 The size of the slope
Change Effect
Price of a session falls to 8 Line rotates outward around the y-intercept; up to 18 sessions
Price rises to 36 Rotates inward; up to 4 sessions
Income falls to 72 Shifts left in parallel; real income 6 kg
Remember
  • Budget equation: price × quantity of each good adds up to income
  • Real income is the intercept: income ÷ price
  • Relative price is the slope: Ps ÷ Pf
  • A price change rotates the line, an income change shifts it in parallel
Lesson 2

Indifference curves

Must knowSlides 10-14

An indifference curve joins combinations the consumer likes equally well. A preference map shows many of them: higher curves are preferred.

The MRS is how much of good y someone will give up for one more x while staying on the same curve. It's the steepness of the curve.

Worked example

Slide 13: diminishing MRS

At C (4 sessions, 6 kg), the tangent falls 12 kg over 8 sessions: MRS = 1.5. At G (8 sessions, 3 kg), it falls 6 kg over 16 sessions: MRS = 0.375.

The more sessions he has, the fewer kg of fruit he'll give up for another one.

Goods Shape MRS
Ordinary Bowed toward the origin Diminishes
Perfect substitutes (two brands of water) Straight lines Constant
Perfect complements (left and right gloves) L-shaped
Memory trick

Steep is Strong desire

A steep curve means a high MRS: a strong desire for the x good. A flat curve means you'd give up little for more of it.

Remember
  • An indifference curve shows combinations liked equally
  • Higher curves are preferred
  • MRS is the magnitude of the slope and diminishes for ordinary goods
  • Perfect substitutes are straight lines; perfect complements are L-shaped
Lesson 3

The best affordable choice

Must knowSlides 15-16
Step by step

Three conditions

  1. On the budget line: all income is spent.
  2. On the highest indifference curve that the budget allows.
  3. Where MRS = relative price, so the curve just touches the line.

Rakan's best point is C: 4 sessions and 6 kg, where MRS = 1.5 = relative price. At B, MRS > relative price, so he should buy more sessions. At D, MRS < relative price, so fewer.

Try itWhat does it mean when MRS exceeds the relative price?

The consumer values one more session more than its cost in fruit, so buying more sessions moves them to a higher curve.

Remember
  • Best choice: on the budget line, on the highest reachable curve
  • At the best choice, MRS equals the relative price
  • Rakan's best choice is 4 sessions and 6 kg of fruit
Lesson 4

Price effect, income effect and substitution effect

Must knowSlides 17-24
Point Session price Income Sessions Fruit
C 18 144 4 6
J 8 144 9 6
K 8 96 6 4
  • Price effect (C to J): the price falls from 18 to 8 and sessions rise from 4 to 9. A movement along the demand curve.
  • Income effect (J to K): income falls from 144 to 96 and sessions fall from 9 to 6. Demand shifts left, so both goods are normal.
Step by step

Splitting the price effect, slides 21-22

  1. Give Rakan a hypothetical pay cut of 48 SAR (to 96) so that at the new price he is back on his original indifference curve. His choice is K.
  2. Substitution effect: C to K, from 4 to 6 sessions (+2). The good became relatively cheaper, so he substitutes toward it. It always works this way.
  3. Income effect: K to J, from 6 to 9 sessions (+3), giving back the 48 SAR.
  4. Total price effect: 2 + 3 = +5 sessions.
Good Substitution effect of a price fall Income effect Demand curve
Normal Buy more Buy more Slopes down
Inferior Buy more Buy less Usually still slopes down
Memory trick

Substitution always Sides with the cheaper good

The substitution effect sides with the good that became cheaper every time. Only the income effect can turn around, for inferior goods.

Exam trap

Upward-sloping demand is extremely rare

It would require an inferior good whose negative income effect outweighs the substitution effect.

Remember
  • The price effect is the change in quantity when only price changes
  • The income effect is the change when only income changes
  • The substitution effect keeps the consumer on the same indifference curve
  • For normal goods both effects increase quantity when price falls
  • For inferior goods the income effect works against the substitution effect

Quick reference

Budget line

  • PsQs + PfQf = Y
  • Real income = Y ÷ P (intercept)
  • Relative price = Ps ÷ Pf (slope)
  • Price change: rotates
  • Income change: parallel shift

Indifference curves

  • Equal satisfaction
  • Higher is better
  • MRS = steepness, diminishes
  • Substitutes: straight
  • Complements: L-shaped

Best choice

  • On budget line
  • Highest curve reached
  • MRS = relative price

Rakan

  • C: 4 sessions, 6 kg
  • J: 9 sessions (price 8)
  • K: 6 sessions (income 96)
  • Substitution +2, income +3
