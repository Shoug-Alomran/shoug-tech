The big picture

In one sentenceA perfectly competitive firm can't choose its price, so it only chooses how much to sell, producing where marginal revenue equals marginal cost, and it shuts down if price falls below average variable cost.

This chapter brings every cost curve from Chapter 11 together, and the profit, break-even, loss and shutdown cases are exam staples. Al-Bustan Farm's numbers let you check each rule.

Where this fits

It uses Chapter 11's costs. Monopoly in Chapter 13 and monopolistic competition in Chapter 14 use the same MR = MC rule.

By the end you can

  • List the conditions for perfect competition
  • Explain why the firm is a price taker and why MR = P
  • Find the profit-maximizing output
  • Calculate economic profit or loss
  • Find the shutdown point

Lesson 1

The market and the price taker

Must knowSlides 3-7

Perfect competition has four conditions:

  1. Many firms sell identical products to many buyers.
  2. Nothing restricts entry: free entry and exit.
  3. Firms already in the market have no advantage over new ones.
  4. Sellers and buyers are well informed about prices.
Memory trick

Many Identical, Free, Fair, Informed

Many firms, Identical product, Free entry, Fair (no advantage), Informed buyers and sellers.

Al-Bustan Farm sells dates at Buraydah market, where hundreds of farms sell the same variety at 60 SAR a box. Ask 62 and buyers walk to the next stall. Ask 58 and you lose money for nothing. So it's a price taker.

The farm's demand curve is horizontal at the market price, and every extra box adds 60 to revenue. So MR = P = 60.

Economic profit = TR − TC, where TC includes normal profit. A firm with zero economic profit still earns normal profit.

Exam trap

The market's demand isn't horizontal

Only the single firm's demand is perfectly elastic. The whole market's demand slopes down as usual.

Remember
  • Many firms, identical products, free entry, no advantage, informed buyers
  • A single firm is a price taker
  • Its demand is horizontal at the market price, so MR = P
  • Total cost includes normal profit
Lesson 2

Choosing output

Must knowSlides 8-14
Boxes TR TC Profit MC of that box
5 300 210 90 32
6 360 246 114 36
7 420 294 126 48
8 480 366 114 72
If Then
MR > MC Produce more
MR < MC Produce less
MR = MC (MC rising) Best output

The 7th box brings in 60 and costs 48, adding 12 to profit. The 8th costs 72, which is more than 60, so stop at 7 boxes, profit 126 SAR.

Think of it like this

Picking fruit at a fixed price

You keep picking while the next box is worth more than the effort it costs. When the next box costs more than it sells for, you go home.

The MC curve above minimum AVC is the firm's supply curve: at a higher price, more boxes are worth producing. That's the law of supply.

Remember
  • Profit is maximized where MR = MC on the rising part of MC
  • Al-Bustan: 7 boxes at 60 SAR, profit 126
  • The rising MC curve above minimum AVC is the firm's supply curve
Lesson 3

Profit, break-even, loss and shutdown

Must knowSlides 15-22

Compare price with ATC at the best output.

Price Best output ATC there Result
60 7 boxes 42 Profit = (60 − 42) × 7 = 126
41 6 boxes 41 (min ATC) Break even: normal profit only
34 5 boxes 42 Loss = (42 − 34) × 5 = 40, which equals TFC
Profit or loss
Economic profit = (P − ATC) × Q

Shutdown point: at minimum AVC, where MC cuts AVC. For Al-Bustan, that's 34 SAR at 5 boxes. There the loss equals TFC (40), exactly what it would lose by shutting down.

Price vs costs Decision
P > ATC Produce; economic profit
ATC > P > AVC Produce in the short run; loss smaller than TFC
P = min AVC Indifferent: the shutdown point
P < AVC Shut down: loss would exceed TFC
Memory trick

Cover Variable, or Close

If price covers AVC, keep producing. If not, close temporarily.

Exam trap

A loss doesn't always mean shutting down

In the short run, the firm stays open as long as price covers AVC, since that loss is smaller than paying fixed costs with no revenue at all.

Try itPrice is 38. Should Al-Bustan produce?

Yes. 38 is above minimum AVC (34), so producing (6 boxes) loses less than shutting down.

Remember
  • Profit if P > ATC, break even if P = ATC, loss if P < ATC
  • Profit = (P − ATC) × Q
  • Shutdown point: minimum AVC, where loss equals TFC
  • Shut down if price is below AVC

Quick reference

Conditions

  • Many firms, identical products
  • Free entry and exit
  • No advantage, full information

Firm's demand

  • Horizontal at market price
  • MR = P

Output rule

  • MR = MC, MC rising
  • Al-Bustan: 7 boxes, profit 126

Results

  • P > ATC: profit
  • P = ATC: break even (41)
  • P < ATC: loss
  • Profit = (P − ATC) × Q

Shutdown

  • At minimum AVC (34, 5 boxes)
  • Loss = TFC there
  • P < AVC: shut down
