The big picture

In one sentenceAdding workers to a fixed kitchen first helps a lot and then less and less, and that one fact gives every cost curve its shape.

The handwriting: "all cost curves and shapes are important", and you "will be asked why MC is important". There's a table of numbers here, Qamar Bakery's, that the slides and the worked example both use, so practise filling it in. Once you see product curves as cost curves in a mirror, the chapter falls into place.

Where this fits

These curves feed straight into Chapters 12 to 14, where each market structure chooses output at MR = MC.

By the end you can

  • Distinguish short run from long run and explain sunk cost
  • Calculate TP, MP and AP
  • Explain increasing and diminishing marginal returns
  • Calculate TC, TFC, TVC, MC, AFC, AVC and ATC
  • Explain why AVC and ATC are U-shaped and why MC cuts them at their minimums
  • Show which curves shift when technology or factor prices change

A sunk cost has already been paid and can't be recovered. It shouldn't affect today's decisions.

Worked example

The worked example's quick question

The bakery paid 25,000 SAR for a decorating counter that can't be resold. Should that affect how many bakers it hires today? No. The money is gone whatever it decides.

Remember
  • Short run: at least one input fixed
  • Long run: all inputs variable
  • Sunk costs shouldn't affect current decisions
Lesson 2

Product curves

Must knowSlides 7-16

Qamar Bakery: kitchen rent 960 SAR a day, each baker 360 SAR a day.

Bakers TP (cakes) MP AP
0 0
1 12 12 12
2 30 18 15
3 40 10 13.3
4 48 8 12
5 54 6 10.8
  • MP = ΔTP ÷ ΔL. It sits between rows, plotted halfway.
  • AP = TP ÷ L.

Increasing marginal returns: the 2nd baker adds 18, more than the 1st's 12. Specialization helps. Diminishing marginal returns: the 3rd adds 10, 4th 8, 5th 6. Everyone shares the same ovens.

Law of diminishing returns
As a firm uses more of a variable input with a fixed input, marginal product eventually diminishes
Think of it like this

Cooks in a small kitchen

A second cook makes the kitchen run much better. The sixth cook mostly waits for a free oven.

Exam tip

MP pulls AP

When MP > AP, AP rises: the 2nd baker's 18 lifts the average from 12 to 15. When MP < AP, AP falls. MP crosses AP at AP's maximum, here 15 cakes with 2 bakers.

Remember
  • MP = ΔTP ÷ ΔL and AP = TP ÷ L
  • Increasing then diminishing marginal returns
  • Diminishing returns come from a fixed plant
  • MP crosses AP at AP's maximum
Lesson 3

Costs

Must knowSlides 17-28
Bakers Q TFC TVC TC AFC AVC ATC MC
1 12 960 360 1,320 80 30 110 30
2 30 960 720 1,680 32 24 56 20
3 40 960 1,080 2,040 24 27 51 36
4 48 960 1,440 2,400 20 30 50 45
5 54 960 1,800 2,760 17.8 33.3 51.1 60
Cost formulas
TC = TFC + TVC
MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ
AFC = TFC ÷ Q, AVC = TVC ÷ Q, ATC = TC ÷ Q = AFC + AVC
Step by step

MC of the 2nd baker's cakes

  1. TC rises from 1,320 to 1,680: an extra 360.
  2. Output rises from 12 to 30: an extra 18 cakes.
  3. MC = 360 ÷ 18 = 20 SAR a cake.

Curve shapes, which the handwriting says are all important:

Curve Shape Why
TFC Horizontal Fixed cost doesn't change
TVC Rises, flatter then steeper It's the TP curve flipped
TC TVC shifted up by TFC
AFC Falls continuously Same 960 spread over more cakes
AVC U-shaped, min 24 at 30 cakes Mirror of AP
ATC U-shaped, min 50 at 48 cakes AFC falling vs AVC rising
MC Falls then rises Mirror of MP

MC cuts AVC and ATC at their minimum points. When MC is below an average, the average falls; when above, it rises. It's the same "pulling" idea as MP and AP.

Why ATC is U-shaped: two forces. AFC falls as fixed cost is spread out, and AVC eventually rises due to diminishing returns. Up to 48 cakes the fall in AFC wins, then the rise in AVC wins.

Memory trick

Marginal Meets the Minimum

MC meets AVC and ATC at their minimums.

Noted in class

Why marginal cost matters

The handwriting says you'll be asked why MC is important: it decides whether producing one more unit is worth it, and firms compare it with marginal revenue in every later chapter.

Remember
  • TC = TFC + TVC; ATC = AFC + AVC
  • MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ
  • AFC always falls; AVC and ATC are U-shaped
  • MC cuts AVC and ATC at their minimum points
  • ATC's U-shape comes from spreading fixed cost and diminishing returns
Lesson 4

Product and cost are mirror images

Must knowSlides 29-32
Product curve Matching cost curve
MP highest (2nd baker, 18) MC lowest (20)
MP rising MC falling
AP highest (15 cakes) AVC lowest (24)

Technology improvements raise product curves and lower all cost curves.

Factor prices:

Change Shifts Doesn't shift
Higher fixed cost (rent 960 → 1,200) TFC, TC, AFC, ATC MC, AVC
Higher variable cost (wages) TVC, TC, AVC, ATC, MC AFC
Worked example

Rent rises to 1,200

At 48 cakes: AFC rises from 20 to 25, and ATC from 50 to 55. MC and AVC don't move.

Exam trap

A fixed-cost rise doesn't change MC

MC only measures the cost of extra output, and fixed cost doesn't change with output.

Remember
  • MC mirrors MP, and AVC mirrors AP
  • Better technology lowers the cost curves
  • A rise in fixed cost doesn't shift MC or AVC
  • A rise in wages shifts MC, AVC, ATC and TC

Quick reference

Time frames

  • Short run: plant fixed
  • Long run: all variable
  • Sunk costs are irrelevant

Product

  • MP = ΔTP ÷ ΔL
  • AP = TP ÷ L
  • MP crosses AP at max AP
  • Diminishing returns: fixed plant

Cost formulas

  • TC = TFC + TVC
  • MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ
  • ATC = AFC + AVC

Shapes

  • TFC flat; AFC falls
  • AVC, ATC U-shaped
  • MC cuts both at minimum

Qamar

  • Min AVC 24 at 30 cakes
  • Min ATC 50 at 48 cakes

Shifts

  • Rent up: TFC, TC, AFC, ATC
  • Wage up: also MC, AVC
