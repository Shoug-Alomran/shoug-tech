SLIDE BLOCK 1

01 Multi-level marketing (التسويق الشبكي)

Individuals sell products directly to the public through direct sales and word of mouth. Distributors earn commissions not only from their own personal sales but also from the sales made by the individuals they recruit into the network.

As the layers of participants increase, early joiners earn greater commissions, which can reach thousands of riyals. That pushes distributors to keep hiring new workers, combining personal sales with network recruitment.

Examples: Avon, Telecom Plus and Discovery Toys.

SLIDE BLOCK 2

02 Pyramid schemes (التسويق الهرمي)

A business model where members are promised financial returns primarily for enrolling new people into the program, rather than for selling actual products or services.

As recruitment expands, the model quickly becomes mathematically impossible to sustain. Most members fail to profit, so these schemes are unsustainable and frequently illegal.

They may resemble MLM, but money flows exclusively upward with no genuine movement or sale of products. Participants try to make money without providing real goods, services or sustainable effort.

SLIDE BLOCK 3

03 MLM vs. pyramid scheme

Feature Multi-level marketing Pyramid scheme
What is it? A marketing strategy A fraudulent investment scheme
Setup and core focus Selling products or services directly to consumers through a network of independent distributors Recruiting new members rather than selling actual products
Promised compensation Commissions from direct retail sales, plus recruiting agents and earning a percentage of their sales Almost exclusively enrollment or recruitment fees paid by new members joining the chain
Product A real channel for marketing goods and services to consumers Often fake, overpriced or non-existent, used as a front for pure recruitment
Legality in law Legal Illegal
Ruling in Islam Illegal Illegal
The row most people get wrong
MLM is legal in law but still forbidden in Islam. Being a real company with real products does not settle the Shariah ruling.
SLIDE BLOCK 4

04 Why it is forbidden (الحكم الشرعي)

The Standing Committee for Academic Research and Issuing Fatwas has stated that this practice is forbidden. The main reason is that the drive is to earn commission, not the product itself, which leads to:

1. Usury
It involves the practice of usury.
2. Gharar
It is a deceiving practice of marketing: the more levels there are, the more commission there is.
3. Taking people’s money unjustly
The slide poses this as a question: why?
Answering the slide’s “why?”
The slide gives no written answer. A reasoned one from its own definitions: those at the top earn from what later joiners pay, while most members fail to profit, so money moves without a fair exchange of goods or effort. Check this against what was said in class.
Mnemonic — GUT
Gharar · Usury · Taking money unjustly. A GUT check tells you why it fails.
SLIDE BLOCK 5

05 How to make it permissible

1. No upfront costs
Joining is entirely free, with no required subscription fees or mandatory product purchases.
2. Genuine product sales
Profit comes directly from marketing real, fairly-priced products, not recruitment fees or commission chains.
3. Direct-sale commissions only
Commissions are paid solely on direct, personal sales, with no multi-tier payouts from downline recruits (“third-party marketers”).
Mnemonic — DUG
Direct-sale commissions · Upfront costs: none · Genuine products. It also fits the slide’s advice: dig into a company before joining.
SLIDE BLOCK 6

06 Before you join

Before working for a company or buying from it, search thoroughly into:

  • its history,
  • how its employees are paid,
  • the types of products it sells.
A person should target to earn the profit from marketing the item, not on marketing the commissions.
