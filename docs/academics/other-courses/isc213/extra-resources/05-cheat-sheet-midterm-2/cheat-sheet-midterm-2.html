Lecture 5 · Securities

01 Securities and their two types

Built from the slides
This sheet follows the wording of the Lecture 5–8 slides. Midterm 2 covers Lectures 5–8; Lecture 9 (Murabaha) is not on it.
"A security is a financial instrument that represents an ownership position or a credit, issued by companies or governments in forms of shares or bonds that can be traded in the stock market 'bourse'."Securities — الأوراق المالية
T
Title deeds — shares
Equal parts of a company's capital. Owning one means owning part of the company, with shareholder rights such as voting and dividends. An equity interest.
D
Debt deeds — bonds
A promise from a bank or company to pay a fixed amount on a set date in exchange for interest — usually because the company needs a loan to expand. A loan, not ownership.
Mnemonic — "Shares own, Bonds owe"
A share is a title (you own a slice). A bond is a debt (the company owes you).
Lecture 5 · Markets

02 From a regular market to the Bourse

Regular marketA place or system where buyers and sellers meet to exchange goods, services, or financial instruments. Because they deal freely, the price of the same good evens out across the market quickly.
Stock exchangeA marketplace where securities (stocks and bonds) are bought and sold by brokers (for a client) and dealers (for their own accounts). It enables price discovery and liquidity and sets rules for fair trading.

Where the word "Bourse" comes from

Fact Detail
Origin A 13th-century inn in Bruges, Belgium — Huis Ter Beurze
Founder & year Robert van der Buerze, 1285
How it grew Its managers gave traders financial advice → the "Beurze Purse" → a hub only for trading commodities
Spread The Paris Bourse (18th century) drove modern stock markets across Europe
Examples today NYSE (New York), LSE (London)

Four things traded on a stock exchange

S
Stocks
Ownership in a company.
B
Bonds
Debt from governments or corporations.
C
Commodities
Gold, oil, crops — through futures contracts.
F
Foreign exchange
Currencies — usually a separate market.
Mnemonic — S.B.C.F.
"Stock Brokers Count Francs."
Common mistake
Brokers and dealers get swapped. A broker trades for a client; a dealer trades for their own account. Seats used to be bought for membership; today trading is mostly electronic.
Lecture 5 · Differences

03 Regular market vs. stock market

# Regular market Stock market (Bourse)
1 Seller meets the buyer face to face Deals are sometimes done through brokers and mediators
2 Goods are present in front of the dealers Goods are elsewhere (in stores or private banks)
3 Delivery and pricing happen after the buyer checks the goods Not so — the buyer does not inspect first
4 Buying and selling are real Can be speculation on price differences without paying the price or delivering the goods
Mnemonic — M.P.C.R.
Meeting, Presence, Checking, Reality — the four things a regular market has and the Bourse may lack.
Lecture 6 · Shares

04 Shares — definition and four characteristics

"Shares are deeds representing units of ownership in a company's capital. Each share has an equal value, cannot be split up, can be traded on the market, and proves ownership rights as a shareholder in that business."Definition of shares

Ownership can be public (traded on stock exchanges) or private (not publicly traded).

E
Equal in value
The capital is divided into equal parts.
I
Indivisible
If several people share one share, only one represents them before the company.
T
Tradable
Can be sold, bought, or traded.
N
Nominal value
A stated value in the deed, set by law with a maximum or minimum.
Mnemonic — E.I.T.N.
"Every Investor Trades Nominally." Bonds have the same four characteristics — see block 10.
Lecture 6 · Values

05 The four values of a share

Value What it is Does it change?
Nominal (par) The face value printed on the deed — the legal minimum issue price (e.g. $1 or $10) Fixed
Issue The price shares are offered at in an IPO or a new issue Set at issue
Real Assets minus liabilities, per share Moves with the company's profitability
Market The current trading price on the exchange Moves with supply and demand, speculation, politics and the economy
Mnemonic — N.I.R.M.
Printed → Offered → Earned → Traded: Nominal, Issue, Real, Market.
Common mistake
Real value comes from the company's books (assets minus liabilities). Market value comes from the exchange (supply and demand). Don't mix them.
Lecture 6 · First classification

06 By rights — ordinary vs. preferred shares

1 — Ordinary shares: equal value, equal rights

1
Remain
Can't be expelled or have shares taken without consent.
2
Vote
One vote per share in the general assembly.
3
Dividends
A share of the distributed profits.
4
Dispose
Sell, gift, or otherwise transfer the shares.
5
Monitor
Review the balance sheet and financial records.
6
Sue
File a liability lawsuit against the board for its errors.
7
Priority
First right to subscribe when new shares are issued.
8
Recoup
A share of what's left of the assets on liquidation.
Mnemonic — a shareholder's life
You stay, you vote, you earn, you sell, you watch, you sue, you buy first, and at the end you collect.

2 — Preferred shares: special rights to attract subscribers

a
Fixed profit
Paid whether the company profits or loses.
b
Full recoup
The whole share value back on liquidation.
c
Extra votes
More than one vote in the general assembly.
Ruling — Not permissible to issue
1. A fixed profit regardless of profit or loss is riba.
2. Treating shareholders unequally contradicts the justice Islam requires.
Mnemonic — "Riba and Rank"
Preferred shares fail twice: guaranteed profit (riba) and unequal rank (injustice).
Lecture 6 · Second classification

07 By recouping — capital vs. enjoyment shares

Capital shares Enjoyment shares
Nominal value returned early? No — not while the company operates (it would shrink the capital) Yes — paid back gradually before the company closes ("shares consumption")
Includes Both preferred and common shares Companies that use up their assets, e.g. petroleum drilling
How repaid — Annually and gradually, by lottery
Ruling Permissible Not permissible
Why enjoyment shares are prohibited
After recovering their money the holder is no longer a partner in the capital or the work, so any profit they still take is illegitimate money — consuming one another's wealth unjustly. The lottery adds manipulation and gharar (uncertainty).
Common mistake
"Enjoyment" sounds harmless. Remember it as "enjoying profit after you've already left" — that's why it's forbidden.
Lecture 6 · Third classification

08 By nature — in-kind vs. cash shares

K
In-kind shares (الأسهم العينية)
Issued for non-cash assets — property, equipment, intellectual property, any tangible or intangible asset.
C
Cash shares (الأسهم النقدية)
Bought with cash — the most common way companies issue shares.
The three classifications — R.R.N.
By Rights (ordinary / preferred), by Recouping (capital / enjoyment), by Nature (in-kind / cash).
Lecture 6 · Rulings

09 Ruling of dealing in shares, trading and zakat

Dealing in shares is permissible on five conditions

1
Halal activity
No alcohol, no usurious banks. Buying such shares is partnering in sin and transgression.
2
No riba
The company doesn't deal in usury, even in some transactions.
3
Known company
Transparent, with clear integrity and finances.
4
Not preferred
Fixed profit = riba; unequal treatment = injustice.
5
Not enjoyment
Profit after recovering capital = unjust wealth + gharar.
Mnemonic — H.R.K. + no P, no E
Halal, Riba-free, Known — and never Preferred or Enjoyment.

Trading and circulating shares

Rule Ruling
Buying shares with an interest-bearing loan Prohibited
Short selling (selling shares you don't own yet) Prohibited
Selling, transferring, or using shares as mortgage collateral Permissible — fully negotiable
Paying for shares in installments Permissible

Zakat on shares

Shareholder Zakat
Long-term investor (holds for dividends) None due from them directly, if the company pays zakat on their behalf (as in Saudi Arabia)
Speculator / active trader Value the whole portfolio at market price at the end of the lunar year and pay zakat on it
Lecture 7 · Bonds

10 Bonds — definition and their names

"Bonds are transferable deeds issued by companies or governments which represent long-term loans, for incremental financial revenue. Usually, they are allocated by public subscription."Bonds (debentures)
Characteristics — E.I.T.N. again
Equal in value, Indivisible against the company, Transferable, Nominal value — the same four as shares.

Terms used for bonds

Name Key facts
1 — Investment certificates a. Increasing nature — money stays with the bank up to ten years, principal + interest paid at the end.
b. Current yield — a fixed annual interest.
c. Increasing value with lottery draws — interest plus prize draws (sold as "encouraging saving").
2 — Treasury bills Short-term government bonds, usually three months, sold by tender (bidding). Highly liquid and guaranteed, so banks' best-seller — though the return is small.
3 — Loan bonds Negotiable, indivisible certificates of equal face value given for money lent to the company as a long-term loan, raised by public subscription. The issuer repays with interest.
Common mistake
Bonds are long-term loans — except treasury bills, which are short-term (three months). The prize certificates are still interest-bearing.
Lecture 7 · Corporate bonds

11 The three corporate bond types

Type Face value vs. amount paid What's special
Ordinary Equal Face value back at maturity + fixed interest on it
Redeemable with an issue premium Face value higher than the amount paid e.g. face 1000 SAR, paid 900 SAR → the 100 SAR is paid back at maturity. Interest is on the nominal value, not the issue value.
Guaranteed Equal Backed by a personal guarantee (government or bank) or an in-kind one (a mortgage on the company's real estate)
Mnemonic — O.P.G.
Ordinary, Premium, Guaranteed — "Only Pay Guaranteed".
Lecture 7 · Comparison

12 Bonds vs. shares

# Bond Share
1 — Nature A debt on the company A portion of the capital
2 — Profit Fixed, in profit or loss Nothing if the company makes no profit
3 — Rights No assembly, vote, or control Attends, votes, and monitors
4 — Dissolution Paid first Paid only after bonds and debts are settled
Mnemonic — N.P.R.D.
Nature, Profit, Rights, Dissolution — "No Partner Rights for Debt".
Lecture 7 · Ruling

13 Ruling of bonds and the lawful alternatives

Ruling — Prohibited
Bonds are based on riba, which the Qur'an and the Sunnah forbid.
"Allah's Messenger ﷺ cursed the one who accepts usury, the one who gives it, the one who records it and the two witnesses to it, saying, 'They are all the same.'"Jabir — reported by Muslim

Lawful alternatives

Who has a deficit Alternative
A — Companies Issue new shares, or form new partnerships / joint ventures
B — The government 1. Good loans from individuals (no riba)
2. Collect zakat early — the Prophet ﷺ took Al-Abbas's zakat two years in advance
3. Encourage voluntary donations
4. Impose exceptional taxes to cover the deficit
Mnemonic — L.Z.D.T.
Government: "Lend, Zakat, Donate, Tax".
Lecture 8 · Leasing

14 Leasing (Ijarah)

IjarahA contract transferring a known benefit (use) of an asset for a specified period in exchange for an agreed payment.
Legal conceptThe owner (lessor) gives the user (lessee) the right to use an asset — real estate, vehicles, equipment — for a period in return for rent.
"Allah, the Exalted, says: 'I will be on the Day of Resurrection against three (types of) people: One who makes a covenant in My Name and then breaks it; one who sells a free man as a slave and devours his price; and one who hires a workman and having taken full work from him, does not pay him his wages.'"Sahih Al-Bukhari
Mutual rights and obligations
# Owner (lessor) Tenant (lessee)
1 Deliver the asset in usable condition Pay the rent on schedule
2 Bear ownership risk and structural repairs Maintain the asset properly
3 Reclaim the asset when the contract ends Return the asset at the end of the term
Common mistake
Structural repairs belong to the owner; everyday maintenance belongs to the tenant. This split is exactly what lease ending with ownership breaks (block 16).
Lecture 8 · Lease ending with ownership

15 Lease ending with ownership — two comparisons

The bank leases an asset (a car, a house) to a client for a set period, at a rent usually above market rent, and promises to transfer ownership through a separate agreement once all installments are paid.

vs. installment sale
Installment sale Lease ending with ownership
Contracts One contract Two independent contracts (lease + ownership promise)
Ownership Transfers immediately; payment is deferred Transfers only at the end
Same in both The goal (owning the asset) and the payment (installments over time)
vs. standard lease
Lease ending with ownership Standard lease
Goal Ownership after full payment Temporary use only
Outcome Tenant first, owner through a second contract Tenant for the whole term
Contracts Two (lease + transfer) One (lease)

Lecture 8 · Ruling

16 Ruling of lease ending with ownership

As practised — Not allowed
The name and the reality of the contract contradict each other:
1. Inflated rent. The "rent" is above market because it secretly includes the price. If the contract ends early or the client can't pay, they lose what they paid and the asset.
2. Unclear contract. Is it a sale or a lease? Each has its own rules — making the tenant carry ownership risk and major repairs turns it into an invalid hybrid.
3. Uncertainty and unfair loss. Keeping all payments as rent after a mid-term default exploits the client.
Mnemonic — I.U.U.
Inflated, Unclear, Unfair.

Permissible when four conditions are met

1
Fixed period
The lease period is specified and lease rules apply throughout.
2
Fixed installments
Each amount is set, with no increase for late payment.
3
Transfer by gift or nominal sale
At the end, per the earlier promise between owner and tenant.
4
No down payment
So the tenant can't lose it if they can't finish paying.
Mnemonic — P.I.T.D.
Period, Installments, Transfer, no Down payment.
