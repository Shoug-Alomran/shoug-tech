L11 · Blocks 1–2

1. Define gambling, distinguish it from betting, and give six forms it takes according to the lecture.

Show model answer

Gambling is wagering money or something of material value on an event with an uncertain outcome, with the primary intent of winning additional money or goods. In the lecture’s distinction, gambling is playing games of chance for money, while betting is risking money on a future event that depends on other people’s effort.

Forms: casino games; non-casino games such as lotteries and scratch cards; Pokémon cards; sports betting; shop contests based on drawing that require a purchase; and competitions that require paying for a call or message.

L11 · Blocks 3–4

2. Give the ruling on gambling with its evidence, then explain the ruling when no money is involved.

Show model answer

Ruling: gambling is haram. The verse calls intoxicants, gambling, al-Ansaab and al-Azlaam an abomination of Shaytaan’s handiwork and commands avoiding them.

With no money: Allah says Satan wants to cause animosity and hatred through intoxicants and gambling and to avert you from the remembrance of Allah and from prayer. These are the main reasons for the prohibition, not only the money, so a game is not allowed if it causes these sins, even with no monetary wager.

L11 · Block 5

3. Explain the wisdom behind the prohibition of gambling.

Show model answer

Families destroyed: wealth is lost through haram means; rich families become poor and proud souls are humiliated.

Luck replaces work: earnings rest on luck and wishful thinking instead of hard work.

Unwell: it causes stress, illness and nervous breakdowns, breeds hatred and, in most cases, leads to crime, suicide, insanity and chronic illness.

Bad behavior: it pushes the gambler toward alcohol, drugs, bad company, cheating and deception.

L11 · Block 6

4. Describe the three kinds of competition excluded from gambling, with the evidence and reasoning for each.

Show model answer

Third-party sponsored: the prize comes from a non-participant, so competitors do not contribute to the prize pool and risk no financial loss.

Horse racing, camel racing and archery: based on the hadith “There should be no (money) prizes for competitions except in camel-racing, horse-racing and archery.” Jurists agree unanimously, and Ibn al-Qayyim explains they were permitted to encourage Muslims to learn horse-riding and prepare for Jihad.

Gift vouchers and store rewards: given to customers who buy over a specified amount. The customer pays only for the goods, with no extra charge for entry, so there is no unlawful risk.

L11 · Blocks 7–8

5. State the four conditions for a permissible competition, then apply them to a contest where each entrant pays 50 riyals and the winner takes the total.

Show model answer

Conditions (LOSS): a Legitimate purpose and means; Obligations not neglected, with no haram acts; a lawful Source of prize, not funded by the contestants (except horse racing, camel racing and archery); and Safety from loss, so that each contestant either wins a prize or leaves without a loss.

Application: this contest fails two conditions. The prize is funded by the contestants and it is not one of the excepted contests, and every losing entrant loses 50 riyals. It is gambling.
