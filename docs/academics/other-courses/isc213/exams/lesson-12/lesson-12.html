L12 · Block 1

1. Define multi-level marketing and explain why its structure pushes distributors to keep recruiting.

Show model answer

In MLM, individuals sell products directly to the public through direct sales and word of mouth. Distributors earn commissions on their own personal sales and on sales by the people they recruit.

As the layers increase, early joiners earn greater commissions, which can reach thousands of riyals, so distributors are pushed to keep hiring new workers to maximize earnings from team sales. Examples include Avon, Telecom Plus and Discovery Toys.

L12 · Block 2

2. Define a pyramid scheme and explain why it cannot last.

Show model answer

A pyramid scheme promises members returns primarily for enrolling new people, rather than for selling actual products or services.

As recruitment expands, the model quickly becomes mathematically impossible to sustain. Most members fail to profit, money flows exclusively upward with no genuine sale of products, and participants try to make money without providing real goods, services or sustainable effort. Such schemes are unsustainable and frequently illegal.

L12 · Block 3

3. Compare multi-level marketing and pyramid schemes across the rows of the lecture’s chart.

Show model answer

What it is: a marketing strategy vs. a fraudulent investment scheme.

Core focus: selling to consumers through independent distributors vs. recruiting new members.

Compensation: commissions on direct sales plus a percentage of recruits’ sales vs. almost exclusively enrollment fees paid by new members.

Product: a real channel for goods and services vs. often fake, overpriced or non-existent, a front for recruitment.

Legality in law: legal vs. illegal. Ruling in Islam: illegal for both.

L12 · Block 4

4. Give the Islamic ruling on these practices and the reasons stated in the lecture.

Show model answer

The Standing Committee for Academic Research and Issuing Fatwas has stated that the practice is forbidden. The main reason is that the drive of this strategy is to earn commission and is not focused on the product itself.

That leads to three forbidden deeds: it involves usury; it is gharar, a deceiving practice of marketing in which more levels bring more commission; and it amounts to taking people’s money unjustly.

L12 · Blocks 5–6

5. State how this kind of sale can be made permissible, and the advice the lecture gives before joining a company.

Show model answer

Conditions (DUG): Direct-sale commissions only, paid on personal sales with no multi-tier payouts from recruits; no Upfront costs, with joining free and no required purchases; Genuine sales of real, fairly-priced products rather than recruitment fees or commission chains.

Advice: before working for a company or buying from it, search thoroughly into its history, how its employees are paid and the types of products it sells. Aim to earn the profit from marketing the item, not from marketing the commissions.
