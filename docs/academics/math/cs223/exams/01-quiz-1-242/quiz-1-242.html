Q1
Solve the linear system.$$\begin{cases}x + y + 2z = 3 \\ x + 2y + z = 1 \\ 2x + y + z = 0\end{cases}$$
1 pt

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. Augmented matrix: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 & 0\end{array}\right]$$
  2. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - R_1$ and $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 2R_1$: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & -2 \\ 0 & -1 & -3 & -6\end{array}\right]$$
  3. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 + R_2$: the last row is $-4z = -8$, so $z = 2$.
  4. Back-substitute: $y - z = -2 \Rightarrow y = 0$, then $x + y + 2z = 3 \Rightarrow x = -1$.
  5. Three pivots and no free variables, so the solution is unique. RREF: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2\end{array}\right]$$

Answer: $(x, y, z) = (-1,\ 0,\ 2)$

Q2
Solve the linear system and give the parametric description of its solutions.$$\begin{cases}x + y + z - 3t = 1 \\ 2x + y - z + t = -1\end{cases}$$
1 pt

Enter the four numbers separated by commas.

Worked solution
  1. Augmented matrix: $$\left[\begin{array}{cccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & -3 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & -1 & 1 & -1\end{array}\right]$$
  2. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1$, then $R_2 \leftarrow -R_2$, then $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - R_2$: $$\left[\begin{array}{cccc|c}1 & 0 & -2 & 4 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & -7 & 3\end{array}\right]$$
  3. Pivots in the $x$ and $y$ columns: $x, y$ are basic, $z, t$ are free.
  4. $x = -2 + 2z - 4t$ and $y = 3 - 3z + 7t$.
  5. Parametric vector form: $$\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\\z\\t\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 3 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + z\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ -3 \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix}-4 \\ 7 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$$

Answer: Infinitely many solutions, two free variables.

Q3
Find the triplet $(a, b, c)$, if possible, such that $P(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ satisfies $P(-1) = 5$, $P(1) = 1$ and $P(2) = 2$.
1 pt

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. Each condition is a linear equation in $a, b, c$: $$\begin{cases}a - b + c = 5 \\ a + b + c = 1 \\ 4a + 2b + c = 2\end{cases}$$
  2. Subtract the first from the second: $2b = -4$, so $b = -2$ and $a + c = 3$.
  3. The third becomes $4a + c = 6$. Subtract $a + c = 3$: $3a = 3$, so $a = 1$ and $c = 2$.

Answer: $P(x) = x^2 - 2x + 2$, so $(a, b, c) = (1, -2, 2)$.

Q4
Solve the linear system.$$\begin{cases}x + 2z = 1 \\ -y + z = 2 \\ x - 2y = 1\end{cases}$$
1 pt

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. Augmented matrix: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & -2 & 0 & 1\end{array}\right]$$
  2. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - R_1$: $(0, -2, -2 \mid 0)$. Then $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 2R_2$: $(0, 0, -4 \mid -4)$, so $z = 1$.
  3. $-y + z = 2 \Rightarrow y = -1$, and $x + 2z = 1 \Rightarrow x = -1$.

Answer: $(x, y, z) = (-1, -1, 1)$, a unique solution.

Q5

1 pt
Worked solution
  1. Swap the rows and apply $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1$: $$\left[\begin{array}{cccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & -3 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -1\end{array}\right]$$
  2. The second row reads $0 = -1$.

Answer: The system is inconsistent: no solution.

Q6
Are the two matrices row equivalent? If so, show how one matrix can be transformed into the other using elementary row operations.$$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 & 5\end{bmatrix}\qquad \begin{bmatrix}3 & 4 & 5 \\ 2 & 2 & -3 \\ 2 & 4 & 8\end{bmatrix}$$
1 pt
Worked solution
  1. $\det\!\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 & 5\end{bmatrix} = -8 \neq 0$ and $\det\!\begin{bmatrix}3 & 4 & 5 \\ 2 & 2 & -3 \\ 2 & 4 & 8\end{bmatrix} = 16 \neq 0$.
  2. A square matrix with nonzero determinant has a pivot in every column, so both reduce to $I_3$.
  3. Row operations are reversible: reduce the first matrix to $I_3$, then apply the inverse of each step that took the second matrix to $I_3$, in reverse order. That chain turns the first matrix into the second.

Answer: Yes: both matrices are row equivalent to $I_3$, hence to each other.

Q7
For which values of $a$ and $b$ is the system consistent with a unique solution, and for which is it inconsistent?$$\begin{cases}x + ay = b \\ ax + y = b\end{cases}$$
1 pt

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. Coefficient determinant: $\det\begin{bmatrix}1 & a \\ a & 1\end{bmatrix} = 1 - a^2$.
  2. If $a \neq \pm 1$ the determinant is nonzero: one solution, $x = y = \dfrac{b}{1 + a}$.
  3. If $a = 1$ both equations are $x + y = b$: consistent for every $b$, with infinitely many solutions.
  4. If $a = -1$: $x - y = b$ and $-x + y = b$. Adding them gives $0 = 2b$, so the system is inconsistent when $b \neq 0$ and has infinitely many solutions when $b = 0$.

Answer: Unique for $a \neq \pm 1$ (any $b$). Inconsistent exactly when $a = -1$ and $b \neq 0$.
