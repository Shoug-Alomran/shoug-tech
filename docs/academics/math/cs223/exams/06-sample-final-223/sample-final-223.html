Question 1: Linear equations

6 PTS
i
Solve the system.$$\begin{cases}x + y + z = 6 \\ x + 2y + 3z = 14 \\ x + 4y + 7z = 30\end{cases}$$
3 pts

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - R_1$: $(0, 1, 2 \mid 8)$. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - R_1$: $(0, 3, 6 \mid 24)$. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 3R_2$: a zero row.
  2. RREF: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 0 & -1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 8 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{array}\right]$$
  3. $z$ is free: $x = -2 + z$, $y = 8 - 2z$.

Answer: $(x, y, z) = (-2, 8, 0) + z(1, -2, 1)$

Note on the original key: The answer table on the paper has a single box for each of x, y, z, but the system is consistent with a free variable, so it has infinitely many solutions.

Question 2: Matrix algebra

6 PTS
a
Find the rank and the nullity of $A = \begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & -1 & 4 \\ 2 & -4 & 3 & 5 \\ -1 & 2 & 6 & -7\end{bmatrix}$.
1 pt

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1$: $(0, 0, 5, -3)$. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 + R_1$: $(0, 0, 5, -3)$. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - R_2$: zero row.
  2. Pivots in columns 1 and 3: rank 2, and nullity $= 4 - 2 = 2$.

Answer: rank 2, nullity 2

b
Show that $A = \tfrac15\begin{bmatrix}-1+2i & -4-2i \\ 2-4i & -2-i\end{bmatrix}$ is unitary ($i^2 = -1$).
1 pt
Worked solution
  1. For a complex matrix, unitary means $A^{H}A = I$ with $A^H = \overline{A}^{\,T}$ (the conjugate transpose).
  2. Column 1 has squared length $\tfrac{1}{25}(|{-1+2i}|^2 + |2-4i|^2) = \tfrac{5 + 20}{25} = 1$, and so does column 2.
  3. Their Hermitian inner product is $\tfrac{1}{25}\left[(-1-2i)(-4-2i) + (2+4i)(-2-i)\right] = \tfrac{1}{25}\left[(0 + 10i) + (0 - 10i)\right] = 0$.

Answer: Yes: $A^HA = I$.

Note on the original key: The hint on the paper says $U^TU = I$. With complex entries you need the conjugate transpose: the plain $A^TA$ here has diagonal $\tfrac{(1-2i)^2}{5}$ and $\tfrac{(2+i)^2}{5}$, not 1.

c
Are $A = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ inverses of each other?
2 pts
Worked solution
  1. $AB = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 0 \\ 3 & 5 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 2\end{bmatrix} \neq I$.
  2. In fact $\det A = 0$ (column 3 = column 1 + column 2), so $A$ has no inverse at all.

Answer: No.

d
Find the inverse of $A = \begin{bmatrix}-8 & 17 & 2 & \tfrac13 & -1 \\ 4 & 0 & \tfrac25 & -9 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 13 & 4 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & -3 & -5 & 7\end{bmatrix}$ if it exists.
2 pts
Worked solution
  1. Row 3 is all zeros, so $\det A = 0$ (expand along that row).
  2. A matrix with zero determinant is not invertible.

Answer: No inverse.

Question 3: Determinants

7 PTS
a
Find $\det A$ using elementary row operations, $A = \begin{bmatrix}2 & 3 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 & 7 \\ 8 & 9 & 1\end{bmatrix}$.
1 pt

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - \tfrac52R_1$: $(0, -\tfrac32, -3)$. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 4R_1$: $(0, -3, -15)$.
  2. $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 2R_2$: $(0, 0, -9)$. Replacements keep the determinant.
  3. $\det A = 2 \cdot (-\tfrac32)(-9) = 27$.

Answer: $\det A = 27$

b
Find $\det B$ by making the boxed entry $b_{23} = 1$ a pivot for its column, $B = \begin{bmatrix}5 & 4 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & \boxed{1} & -2 \\ -5 & -7 & -3 & 9 \\ 1 & -2 & -1 & 4\end{bmatrix}$.
2 pts

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. Clear column 3 with $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 2R_2$, $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 + 3R_2$, $R_4 \leftarrow R_4 + R_2$ (determinant unchanged):$$\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 0 & 5 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 & -2 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 & 0 & 2\end{bmatrix}$$
  2. Expand along column 3: $\det B = (-1)^{2+3}\cdot 1\cdot\begin{vmatrix}1 & -2 & 5 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 & 2\end{vmatrix}$.
  3. The minor is $1(4 - 3) + 2(2 - 9) + 5(1 - 6) = 1 - 14 - 25 = -38$, so $\det B = 38$.

Answer: $\det B = 38$

c
Find $\det C$, $C = \begin{bmatrix}3 & 2 & 4 & 5 & 7 \\ 8 & 9 & 7 & 5 & 6 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5\end{bmatrix}$, using $\det D = -54$ for $D = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\ 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 2 & 4 & 5 & 7 \\ 8 & 9 & 7 & 5 & 6 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$.
2 pts

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. The rows of $C$ are rows 3, 4, 5, 2, 1 of $D$, so $C = PD$ for a permutation matrix $P$, and $\det C = \det P\cdot\det D$.
  2. The permutation $(3, 4, 5, 2, 1)$ splits into the cycles $(1\,3\,5)(2\,4)$: an even 3-cycle times one swap, so it is odd and $\det P = -1$.
  3. $\det C = -\det D = -(-54) = 54$.

Answer: $\det C = -\det D = 54$ (using the given value)

Note on the original key: Computing $D$ directly gives $\det D = +54$, so the printed value has a sign slip. With the true value, $\det C = -54$. Both answers are accepted here; the row-permutation argument is what the question tests.

d
Find $\det U$, $U = \begin{bmatrix}2 & 3 & 4 & 7 & 8 \\ -1 & 5 & 3 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & 1 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 & -1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 & 2 & 6\end{bmatrix}$.
2 pts

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. $U$ is block upper triangular: a $2\times2$ block on top-left and a $3\times3$ block on bottom-right, zeros below.
  2. $\begin{vmatrix}2 & 3 \\ -1 & 5\end{vmatrix} = 10 + 3 = 13$.
  3. $\begin{vmatrix}2 & 1 & 5 \\ 3 & -1 & 4 \\ 5 & 2 & 6\end{vmatrix} = 2(-6 - 8) - 1(18 - 20) + 5(6 + 5) = -28 + 2 + 55 = 29$.
  4. $\det U = 13 \cdot 29 = 377$.

Answer: $\det U = 377$

Is $\mathbf v = \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 10\end{bmatrix}$ in $\operatorname{Col}A$ for $A = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 4 & -6\end{bmatrix}$? If so, give the combination of columns.

  1. Solve $c_1 + 3c_2 = -2$, $4c_1 - 6c_2 = 10$: $c_1 = 1$, $c_2 = -1$.

0.5 pts

  1. Column 3 = column 1 + column 2, so Col $A$ is a plane. Reduce $[A \mid \mathbf v]$:
  2. $R_2 - R_1$: $(0, -1, -1 \mid 1)$; $R_3 - 2R_1$: $(0, -1, -1 \mid 4)$; subtract: $(0, 0, 0 \mid 3)$, inconsistent.

Answer: No.

Is $\mathbf v = \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$ in $\operatorname{Col}A$ for $A = \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & 1 \\ 9 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$? If so, give the combination.

For $A = \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & 3 \\ 5 & -4 & -4 \\ 7 & -6 & 2\end{bmatrix}$ find a basis of $\operatorname{Nul}A$, the nullity, a basis of $\operatorname{Col}A$ and the rank.

c
$\operatorname{Col}A$ has basis $\left\{(2, -3, 1, 8, 7),\ (-3, 2, 1, -9, 6)\right\}$ and $\operatorname{Nul}A$ has a basis of exactly 2 vectors. How many columns does $A$ have?

Worked solution
  1. rank $= \dim\operatorname{Col}A = 2$ and nullity $= 2$.
  2. Rank theorem: $n = \text{rank} + \text{nullity} = 4$.

Question 5: Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

8 PTS
a
Find the eigenvalues of $A = \begin{bmatrix}0 & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 & 0\end{bmatrix}$.
2 pts

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. $A = I - J$ where $J$ is the all-ones matrix, whose eigenvalues are $3, 0, 0$.
  2. So $A$ has eigenvalues $1 - 3 = -2$ and $1 - 0 = 1$ (twice). Expanding $\det(A - \lambda I) = -(\lambda + 2)(\lambda - 1)^2$ agrees.

Answer: $\lambda = -2,\ 1,\ 1$

2 pts

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. Lower triangular: the eigenvalues are the diagonal entries.

Answer: $\lambda = 1,\ 2,\ 3$

Question 6: Orthogonality

3 PTS
Let $\mathbf u = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf v = \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf w = \begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 2 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}$.
a
Find $\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf u}\mathbf v$.
1.5 pts

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. $\dfrac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf u}{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u}\mathbf u = \tfrac43(1, 1, 1)$.

Answer: $\left(\tfrac43, \tfrac43, \tfrac43\right)$

b
Let $\mathbf z = \mathbf v - \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf u}\mathbf v$ (perpendicular to $\mathbf u$). Find the projection of $\mathbf w$ onto $\operatorname{Span}\{\mathbf u, \mathbf z\}$.
1.5 pts

Fractions, decimals, sqrt(…) and lists separated by commas all work.

Worked solution
  1. $\mathbf z = (\tfrac23, -\tfrac13, -\tfrac13)$, orthogonal to $\mathbf u$.
  2. $\dfrac{\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf u}{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u} = -\tfrac13$ and $\dfrac{\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf z}{\mathbf z\cdot\mathbf z} = \dfrac{-2/3}{2/3} = -1$.
  3. $\hat{\mathbf w} = -\tfrac13(1, 1, 1) - (\tfrac23, -\tfrac13, -\tfrac13) = (-1, 0, 0)$.

Answer: $(-1, 0, 0)$
