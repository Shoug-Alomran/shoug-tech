The big picture

In one sentenceEconomic growth is the sustained expansion of production possibilities: potential GDP grows when the supply of labor grows or, more importantly for living standards, when labor productivity grows through physical capital, human capital and technology.

Chapter 6 is on Major I. The group work and student sheet focus on four skills: calculating growth rates of real GDP and of real GDP per person, the Rule of 70, telling growth (the PPF shifts out) from a recovery (moving back to the PPF), and reading the labor market and production function tables to find potential GDP and labor productivity. Know what a rise in population does compared with a rise in productivity.

Where this fits

Chapter 4 defined real GDP per person; Chapter 5 defined potential GDP and full employment. This chapter explains why potential GDP grows. Chapter 7 shows how saving funds the investment in capital that drives growth.

By the end you can

  • Calculate the growth rate of real GDP and of real GDP per person
  • Use the Rule of 70 to find the doubling time
  • Distinguish economic growth from a business cycle recovery
  • Use the aggregate production function and labor market to find potential GDP
  • Explain the effects of population growth and of labor productivity growth
  • Explain why labor productivity grows, and outline the growth theories and policies

Lesson 1

The basics: growth rates

Must knowSlides 4-8

Economic growth is a sustained expansion of production possibilities, measured as the increase in real GDP over a given period. The standard of living depends on real GDP per person, which rises only when real GDP grows faster than the population.

Growth rates
Growth rate of real GDP = (real GDP this year − real GDP last year) ÷ real GDP last year × 100
Growth rate of real GDP per person ≈ growth rate of real GDP − population growth rate
Step by step

Group work, Problem 1: Mexico, real GDP 18,163 → 18,526 billion pesos; population 123.5 → 124.7 million

  1. Real GDP growth = (18,526 − 18,163) ÷ 18,163 × 100 = 363 ÷ 18,163 × 100 = 2.0%.
  2. Real GDP per person: 2017 = 18,163 ÷ 123.5 = 147,070 pesos; 2018 = 18,526 ÷ 124.7 = 148,565 pesos.
  3. Growth of real GDP per person = (148,565 − 147,070) ÷ 147,070 × 100 = 1.0% (about 2.0% − 1.0% population growth).
  4. Doubling time (Rule of 70) = 70 ÷ 1.0 ≈ 70 years.

Growth versus a business cycle expansion. Real GDP can rise for two different reasons:

Recovery (business cycle expansion) Economic growth
The economy moves from inside the PPF back to the PPF The PPF itself shifts outward
Unemployed resources go back to work Potential GDP increases
Temporary, a return to full employment Sustained
Exam trap

A return to full employment isn't growth

The student sheet asks which situation is economic growth. Only an outward shift of the PPF. Moving from inside the PPF to the PPF, falling unemployment during a recovery, or returning to full employment are all business cycle expansions.

Try itConcept check: India has averaged 7% GDP growth since 2007. Which is true?

India's PPF has been shifting rightward (outward) since 2007. Sustained growth means production possibilities expand; it isn't a move from inside to beyond a fixed PPF.

Try itStudent sheet: real GDP rises from 520 billion to 546 billion. Growth rate?

(546 − 520) ÷ 520 × 100 = 26 ÷ 520 × 100 = 5%.

Try itStudent sheet: population grows 2% and real GDP grows 5%. Growth of real GDP per person?

About 3% a year (5% − 2%).

Remember
  • Growth rate = change in real GDP ÷ last year's real GDP × 100
  • Growth of real GDP per person ≈ real GDP growth − population growth
  • Growth shifts the PPF out; a recovery moves the economy back to the PPF
Lesson 2

The magic of sustained growth: the Rule of 70

Must knowSlides 9-10

Small differences in growth rates add up to huge differences in living standards over time.

Rule of 70
Years for a variable to double ≈ 70 ÷ annual growth rate (%)
Growth rate Doubling time
1% 70 years
2% 35 years
7% 10 years
Memory trick

"70 divided by the rate"

Doubling time = 70 ÷ rate. Check: 7% → 10 years, 10% → 7 years. The two numbers always multiply to about 70.

Step by step

Group work, Problem 2: China 57,163 → 60,334 yuan per person; India 98,028 → 104,191 rupees per person (2017 → 2018)

  1. China's growth = (60,334 − 57,163) ÷ 57,163 × 100 = 5.5%, so it doubles in 70 ÷ 5.5 ≈ 12.6 years.
  2. India's growth = (104,191 − 98,028) ÷ 98,028 × 100 = 6.3%, so it doubles in 70 ÷ 6.3 ≈ 11.1 years.
  3. India doubles first, around 2028 (2017 + 11 years).
Exam trap

Compare growth rates, not levels

India's numbers are bigger, but they're in a different currency. Only the growth rates decide who doubles first.

Remember
  • Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate
  • A few points of growth make an enormous difference over decades
  • Compare countries by growth rates, not by numbers in different currencies
Lesson 3

Long-term growth trends

Good to knowSlides 11-14
  • United States: from 1901 to 2021, real GDP per person grew at about 2% a year on average, but the rate was not constant: it was low during the Great Depression, high in the 1960s, and slower after the productivity slowdown.
  • World: real GDP per person in some economies (Hong Kong, Korea, Singapore, Taiwan, and later China) grew much faster than in the U.S., so they caught up. Many others, including much of Africa and Central and South America, grew slowly, so the gap widened.
Try itGroup work, Problem 12: IMF projects advanced economies growing about 2% and developing economies 4.5–4.8%. Are gaps in real GDP per person shrinking?

Shrinking, broadly: developing economies are projected to grow more than twice as fast as advanced economies, so they're catching up. (Strictly, it depends on population growth, since a fast-growing population can eat up higher real GDP growth.)

Remember
  • U.S. real GDP per person: about 2% a year over the long run
  • Fast growers like China and the Asian economies are catching up
  • Many poorer economies are falling further behind
Lesson 4

How potential GDP is determined

Must knowSlides 15-21

Potential GDP depends on two things:

  1. The aggregate production function: real GDP rises as labor hours rise, holding capital and technology constant, with diminishing returns (each extra hour adds less).
  2. The aggregate labor market: the demand for labor slopes down (firms hire more hours at a lower real wage, because of diminishing marginal product), and the supply of labor slopes up (people supply more hours at a higher real wage).

Labor market equilibrium determines the real wage rate and the full-employment quantity of labor. Plug that quantity into the production function and you get potential GDP.

Step by step

Group work, Problems 15–16: find the equilibrium and potential GDP

  1. Read the labor table: at a real wage of $40 an hour, labor supplied = labor demanded = 35 hours. Above $40 there's a surplus of labor; below $40, a shortage.
  2. Read the production function at 35 hours: real GDP = $2,625 (potential GDP).
  3. Labor productivity = real GDP ÷ hours = 2,625 ÷ 35 = $75 per hour.
Memory trick

Two diagrams, one answer

Labor market first (where LD meets LS → hours), then production function (hours → potential GDP). "Find the hours, then read the output."

Remember
  • Production function: more labor → more real GDP, with diminishing returns
  • Labor demand slopes down, labor supply slopes up
  • Equilibrium gives the full-employment quantity of labor
  • Potential GDP = output on the production function at that quantity of labor
  • Labor productivity = real GDP ÷ labor hours
Lesson 5

What makes potential GDP grow?

Must knowSlides 22-29

Potential GDP grows because of growth in the supply of labor or growth in labor productivity.

Aggregate hours depend on average hours per worker, the employment-to-population ratio and the working-age population. The two sources have very different effects:

Effect on… Population increases Labor productivity increases
Labor supply Increases (shifts right) No change
Labor demand No change Increases (shifts right)
Real wage rate Falls Rises
Employment (hours) Increases Increases
Production function No change Shifts up
Potential GDP Increases Increases
Real GDP per hour of labor Falls (diminishing returns) Rises
Living standards Don't rise Rise
Memory trick

"People add output, Productivity adds prosperity"

More people add to total output but not to output per person. Only higher productivity raises the real wage and the standard of living.

Exam trap

Population growth doesn't raise living standards

The student sheet's key point: a bigger population raises total real GDP but, with diminishing returns, real GDP per hour of labor falls and the real wage falls. Living standards rise only when labor productivity grows.

Try itGroup work, Problem 17: labor productivity increases. What happens?

The demand for labor increases (each hour produces more, so firms want more hours). The supply of labor doesn't change. Employment and the real wage rise, the production function shifts up, potential GDP increases, and real GDP per person increases.

Remember
  • Potential GDP grows with labor supply or labor productivity
  • Population growth: more hours, lower real wage, lower productivity
  • Productivity growth: higher demand for labor, higher real wage, higher real GDP per person
  • Only productivity growth raises living standards
Lesson 6

Why labor productivity grows

Must knowSlides 30-33

Preconditions. The fundamental precondition is an incentive system, created by three institutions:

  • Firms, which organize production
  • Property rights, which protect ownership of resources and what they produce
  • Markets, which let people trade

With those in place, labor productivity grows through:

Source How it works
Physical capital growth More and better machines per worker (saving and investment)
Human capital growth Education, training and on-the-job experience; the most fundamental source
Technological advances Discovering and applying new technologies; usually needs new capital to put to work
Memory trick

"Machines, Minds, Methods"

Productivity grows from machines (physical capital), minds (human capital) and methods (technology). Underneath them: firms, property rights and markets.

Try itGroup work, Problem 13: a large increase in investment raises labor productivity. What happens?

a) Potential GDP increases. b) Employment increases (labor demand rises). c) The real wage rate rises.

Try itGroup work, Problem 18: India's labor productivity growth fell from 14% a year to 3.7%, while capital per worker rose but output per unit of capital fell. What's happening?

Labor productivity is still growing, but much more slowly. Capital productivity is falling: each extra unit of capital adds less output (diminishing returns). Slower productivity growth means slower growth of potential GDP and of living standards.

Remember
  • Preconditions: firms, property rights, markets create incentives
  • Sources: physical capital, human capital, technology
  • More capital raises productivity, potential GDP, employment and the real wage
Lesson 7

Growth theories and policies

Just skimSlides 34-49
Theory Core idea Prediction
Classical Growth raises real GDP per person above subsistence, causing a population explosion that drives it back down (Malthus) Growth is temporary; living standards return to subsistence
Neoclassical Growth comes from technological change; diminishing returns to capital mean growth stops if technology stops (Solow) Growth rates should converge across countries
New growth Real GDP per person grows because people choose to innovate in pursuit of profit; knowledge is not subject to diminishing returns (Romer) Growth can persist indefinitely

The evidence: growth is linked to saving and investment, education, openness to trade and stable, rule-of-law institutions.

Five policies for faster growth: create incentive mechanisms (property rights, markets), encourage saving, encourage research and development, improve the quality of education, and encourage international trade.

Memory trick

Classical = Crowded, Neoclassical = Needs tech, New = Never stops

Classical: population growth crowds out gains. Neoclassical: growth needs outside technology. New growth: innovation is a choice, so growth never has to stop.

Remember
  • Classical: population growth returns living standards to subsistence
  • Neoclassical: technology drives growth; diminishing returns to capital
  • New growth: profit-seeking innovation keeps growth going
  • Policies: incentives, saving, R&D, education, trade

Quick reference

Growth rates

  • %Δ real GDP = Δ ÷ last year × 100
  • Per person ≈ GDP growth − population growth
  • Rule of 70: doubling = 70 ÷ rate

Growth vs recovery

  • Growth: PPF shifts out
  • Recovery: back to the PPF

Potential GDP

  • LD down, LS up → full-employment hours
  • Production function → potential GDP
  • Productivity = real GDP ÷ hours

Population up

  • LS right, real wage down
  • Potential GDP up
  • Productivity down

Productivity up

  • LD right, real wage up
  • Potential GDP up
  • Real GDP per person up

Sources

  • Physical capital
  • Human capital
  • Technology
  • Needs firms, property rights, markets
