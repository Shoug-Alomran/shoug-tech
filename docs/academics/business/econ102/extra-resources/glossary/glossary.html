Chapter 1 · What Is Economics?

Ceteris paribus"Other things remaining the same."
IncentiveA reward that encourages an action or a penalty that discourages it.
MarginalThe effect of a little more or a little less of something.
Normative statementAbout what ought to be; an opinion that can't be tested.
Opportunity costThe highest-valued alternative you give up to get something.
Positive statementAbout what is; can be tested against facts.
ScarcityWe want more than our resources can provide, so we must choose.

Chapter 4 · Measuring the Value of Production: GDP

Consumption (C)Households' spending on consumer goods and services.
DepreciationFall in the value of capital from wear and tear and obsolescence.
Final goodBought by its final user, not used up to make something else.
Intermediate goodBought by a firm and used up in producing another good.
Investment (I)Firms' purchases of new capital, buildings and additions to inventories.
Net exports (X − M)Exports minus imports.
Nominal GDPOutput valued at the prices of the current year.
Real GDPOutput valued at the prices of a base year.

Chapter 5 · Monitoring Jobs and Inflation

CPI basketThe fixed set of goods and services a typical urban household buys.
Discouraged workerWants a job but has stopped looking because they think none is available.
Labor forceEmployed + unemployed.
Natural unemploymentFrictional + structural unemployment.
Price levelThe average level of prices, measured by a price index.
UnemployedWithout work but available and actively looking (or waiting to be recalled or to start).
Working-age populationPeople aged 16 and over who aren't in jail, hospital or the armed forces.

Chapter 6 · Economic Growth

Aggregate production functionHow real GDP changes as the quantity of labor changes, with capital and technology constant.
Economic growthA sustained expansion of production possibilities, measured as the increase in real GDP.
Human capitalThe knowledge and skills people get from education, training and experience.
Labor productivityReal GDP per hour of labor.
Potential GDPReal GDP produced when the economy is at full employment.
Real wage rateThe money wage rate divided by the price level: what an hour of work buys.

Chapter 7 · Finance, Saving, and Investment

Crowding outA budget deficit raises the real interest rate and reduces private investment.
Financial capitalThe funds firms use to buy physical capital.
IlliquidCan't meet a payment right now, even though net worth may be positive.
InsolventNet worth (assets − liabilities) is negative.
Net present valuePresent value of future returns minus the cost today.
Physical capitalTools, machines, buildings and other things made to produce goods and services.
SavingDisposable income minus consumption; a flow that adds to wealth.
WealthThe value of everything a person owns; a stock.

Chapter 8 · Money, the Price Level, and Inflation

Currency drain ratioThe ratio of currency held by the public to deposits.
Desired reserve ratioThe fraction of deposits banks choose to hold as reserves.
FOMCFederal Open Market Committee, the Fed's main policy-making body.
Means of paymentA method of settling a debt.
Monetary baseCurrency (notes and coins) + banks' deposits at the Fed.
Money multiplierChange in the quantity of money ÷ change in the monetary base.
Velocity of circulationThe average number of times a dollar is used in a year to buy final goods: V = PY ÷ M.

Chapter 9 · The Exchange Rate and the Balance of Payments

AppreciationA rise in the value of a currency in terms of another currency.
Debtor nationA country that, over its history, has borrowed more from abroad than it has lent.
DepreciationA fall in the value of a currency in terms of another currency.
Exchange rateThe price at which one currency exchanges for another.
Net borrowerA country borrowing more from the rest of the world than it lends to it.
Official reservesA government's holdings of foreign currency.
Real exchange rateThe relative price of domestic and foreign goods and services.

Chapter 10 · Aggregate Supply and Aggregate Demand

Aggregate demandThe relationship between the quantity of real GDP demanded and the price level.
Aggregate supplyThe relationship between the quantity of real GDP supplied and the price level.
Inflationary gapReal GDP above potential GDP.
LASLong-run aggregate supply: real GDP supplied when real GDP = potential GDP.
Recessionary gapReal GDP below potential GDP.
SASShort-run aggregate supply: real GDP supplied when money wages and potential GDP are fixed.
StagflationRising price level combined with falling real GDP.

Chapter 11 · Expenditure Multipliers

Aggregate planned expenditure (AE)Planned C + I + G + X − M.
Autonomous expenditureSpending that doesn't change when real GDP changes.
Consumption functionThe relationship between consumption and disposable income.
Disposable income (YD)Income minus taxes plus transfer payments.
Induced expenditureSpending that changes when real GDP changes.
MPCMarginal propensity to consume: ΔC ÷ ΔYD.
MPSMarginal propensity to save: ΔS ÷ ΔYD.
MultiplierChange in equilibrium real GDP ÷ change in autonomous expenditure.

Chapter 12 · The Business Cycle, Inflation, and Deflation

Cost-push inflationInflation that starts with an increase in costs (money wages or raw material prices).
DeflationA persistently falling price level; the inflation rate is negative.
Demand-pull inflationInflation that starts because aggregate demand increases.
DisinflationA fall in the inflation rate; prices still rise, but more slowly.
Long-run Phillips curveVertical at the natural unemployment rate.
Short-run Phillips curveThe inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment, with expected inflation and the natural rate fixed.
StagflationA rising price level combined with falling real GDP.

Chapter 13 · Fiscal Policy

Automatic fiscal policyA change in fiscal policy triggered by the state of the economy, with no new law.
Budget balanceReceipts − outlays.
Budget deficitOutlays exceed receipts; the balance is negative.
Cyclical balanceActual balance − structural balance; caused by the business cycle.
Discretionary fiscal policyA policy action that needs a new act of Congress.
Fiscal policyUsing the federal budget (spending, taxes, transfers) to influence the economy.
Government debtTotal amount of government borrowing: the sum of past deficits minus past surpluses.
Structural balanceThe budget balance that would occur if real GDP were at potential GDP.

Chapter 14 · Monetary Policy

Contractionary policyRaising the policy rate to slow spending and inflation.
Emergency liquidityCentral bank lending to keep banks and markets working in a crisis.
Expansionary policyLowering the policy rate to boost spending and employment.
Macroprudential regulationRules that reduce risk across the whole financial system.
Monetary policyCentral bank decisions that influence interest rates, the quantity of money and economic activity.
Policy interest rateThe short-term rate the central bank targets (the federal funds rate in the U.S.).
Transmission mechanismThe chain from a policy rate change to real GDP, jobs and inflation.
