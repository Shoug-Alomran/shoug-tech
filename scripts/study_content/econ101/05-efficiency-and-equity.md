## Benefit, cost and surplus | Slides 13-25 | must
**Demand is marginal benefit; supply is marginal cost.** A market demand curve is the horizontal sum of individual demands: at each price, add everyone's quantities.

::plot Consumer surplus sits above the price; producer surplus sits below it | quantity | price | 10 | 10
area consumer 2: 0,9 5,5 0,5
area producer 4: 0,1 5,5 0,5
line D 2: 0,9 9,1.8
line S 5: 0,1 9,8.2
dot equilibrium: 5,5
::

| Surplus | Formula | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| **Consumer surplus** | Value (willingness to pay) − price paid | Triangle below demand, above price |
| **Producer surplus** | Price received − marginal cost | Triangle above supply, below price |
| **Total surplus** | CS + PS | Both triangles |

::steps Compute both from the plot above
Equilibrium: price 5, quantity 5. Demand starts at 9 and supply at 1.
Consumer surplus = ½ × base × height = ½ × 5 × (9 − 5) = **10**.
Producer surplus = ½ × 5 × (5 − 1) = **10**.
Total surplus = **20**.
::
::example One buyer at a time
Three buyers would pay up to 30, 25 and 15 for a meal, and the price is 15. Their consumer surpluses are 15, 10 and 0, a total of **25**.
::
::key
- Demand is marginal benefit and supply is marginal cost
- Market demand adds individual quantities at each price
- Consumer surplus is the area below demand and above price
- Producer surplus is the area above supply and below price
- Use ½ × base × height
::

## Is the competitive market efficient? | Slides 26-40 | must
At the competitive equilibrium **marginal benefit = marginal cost**, so **total surplus is as large as it can be**. Adam Smith's "invisible hand" makes self-interested choices produce this result.

| Quantity | MB vs MC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Less than equilibrium | MB > MC | **Underproduction**, deadweight loss |
| Equilibrium | MB = MC | Efficient |
| More than equilibrium | MB < MC | **Overproduction**, deadweight loss |

**Obstacles to efficiency:** price and quantity regulations (ceilings, floors, quotas), taxes and subsidies, externalities, public goods and common resources, monopoly, and high transaction costs.

::mnemonic Producing Too Little or Too Much: Both Waste
**Deadweight loss** happens on **both** sides of the equilibrium. Under- and overproduction both shrink total surplus.
::
::key
- At competitive equilibrium marginal benefit equals marginal cost
- Total surplus is maximized at equilibrium
- Underproduction and overproduction both cause deadweight loss
- Obstacles: regulations, taxes, subsidies, externalities, public goods, monopoly, transaction costs
::

## Is the competitive market fair? | Slides 41-56 | know
| View | Says it's unfair if | Key ideas |
|---|---|---|
| **"It's not fair if the result isn't fair"** | Incomes are very unequal | Utilitarianism; the "big tradeoff" between equality and efficiency |
| **"It's not fair if the rules aren't fair"** | Rules or processes are unfair | Symmetry principle: people in similar situations treated similarly; property rights and voluntary exchange |

::trap Efficiency isn't fairness
A market can be efficient and still produce an outcome many people think is unfair.
::
::key
- Fair results view focuses on equal outcomes
- Fair rules view focuses on property rights and voluntary exchange
- Efficient outcomes are not necessarily fair
::

~ less, marginal benefit, exceeds, lost surplus
> When less than the efficient quantity is produced, marginal benefit exceeds marginal cost for the missing units. The surplus those units would have created is lost.
