## Scarcity and the two big questions | Slides 3-26 | must

**Scarcity** is our inability to satisfy all our wants. It affects everyone, even the very rich, whose time is limited. **Economics is the social science that studies the choices that individuals, businesses, governments and entire societies make as they cope with scarcity**, and the incentives that influence those choices.

| Branch             | Studies                                                                 | Example                     |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Microeconomics** | Choices of individuals and businesses, and how they interact in markets | Why did abaya prices rise?  |
| **Macroeconomics** | The national and global economy as a whole                              | Why is unemployment rising? |

**Big question 1: How do choices determine what, how and for whom goods are produced?**

- **What?** Depends on the country's resources. Agriculture, industry or services.
- **How?** Using **factors of production**.
- **For whom?** Depends on the incomes people earn.

| Factor               | Means                                                      | Earns        |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| **Land**             | "Gifts of nature": land, minerals, water                   | **Rent**     |
| **Labor**            | Work time and effort; quality rises with **human capital** | **Wages**    |
| **Capital**          | Tools, buildings, machines                                 | **Interest** |
| **Entrepreneurship** | Organizing the other three                                 | **Profit**   |

::mnemonic CELL
The four factors: **C**apital, **E**ntrepreneurship, **L**and, **L**abor.
::
::class The factors of production
The handwriting says "must know in detail". Be ready to name each factor and what it earns.
::

**Big question 2: Can choices made in self-interest also serve the social interest?** **Self-interest** is what you think is best for you. **Social interest** is what's best for society, and it has two parts: **efficiency** (you can't make someone better off without making someone else worse off) and **fair shares**.

::key

- Scarcity is the inability to satisfy all wants
- Economics studies choices under scarcity
- Microeconomics covers individuals and markets, macroeconomics the whole economy
- Four factors: land, labor, capital, entrepreneurship
- Rent, wages, interest and profit are what those factors earn
  ::

## Six ideas that define economic thinking | Slides 27-34 | must

| Idea                                  | Meaning                                         | Everyday example                          |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **1. A choice is a tradeoff**         | Getting more of one thing means less of another | An hour on TikTok is an hour not studying |
| **2. People choose rationally**       | Compare costs and benefits                      | You study because the grade is worth it   |
| **3. Benefit is what you gain**       | Measured by what you're willing to give up      |                                           |
| **4. Cost is what you must give up**  | The **highest-valued alternative**              |                                           |
| **5. Most choices are at the margin** | "A little more or a little less"                | One more hour of revision?                |
| **6. Choices respond to incentives**  | Change costs or benefits and behaviour changes  | A discount brings shoppers                |

::analogy Opportunity cost at the weekend
You could go to a concert, watch a film at home or sleep. You choose the concert. The opportunity cost isn't all the other options, **only the best one you gave up**.
::
::tip The rule at the margin
If marginal benefit exceeds marginal cost, do a bit more. If it's less, do a bit less. This rule returns as MR = MC in Chapters 12 to 14.
::
::trap Opportunity cost isn't only money
The handwriting calls it "the next best choice you are sacrificing". The cost of attending university includes the wage you could have earned.
::
::key

- A choice is a tradeoff
- Rational choice compares costs and benefits
- Opportunity cost is the highest-valued alternative given up
- Choices at the margin weigh extra benefit against extra cost
- Incentives change behaviour
  ::

## Economics as a science, and reading graphs | Slides 35-90 | know

| Statement type | About                | Example                                   | Can be tested?            |
| -------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Positive**   | What **is**          | "Raising the minimum wage reduces hiring" | Yes                       |
| **Normative**  | What **ought to be** | "The minimum wage should be higher"       | No, it's a value judgment |

::mnemonic Positive Proves, Normative is Nice-to-have
**P**ositive statements can be **p**roved or disproved. **N**ormative ones are opinions about what's **n**ice.
::

Economists build **economic models** and test them against facts, and the appendix gives you the graph tools:

| Pattern                   | Meaning                                          |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Positive** relationship | Variables move together, upward slope            |
| **Negative** relationship | They move in opposite directions, downward slope |
| **Maximum or minimum**    | Curve peaks or bottoms out, slope zero there     |
| **Unrelated**             | Horizontal or vertical line                      |

::formula Slope
slope = rise / run = Δy / Δx
::

The handwriting says slope is **important for marginal analysis**. A straight line has one slope. A curve's slope varies, measured at a point by a **tangent** or across an **arc**.

::tip Shifts versus movements
With more than two variables, one is held constant (_ceteris paribus_). Change that variable and the curve **shifts**. This reappears in Chapter 3.
::
::check Is "Saudi unemployment fell last year" positive or normative?
**Positive.** It's a statement about facts that can be checked.
::
::key

- Positive statements are testable; normative statements are value judgments
- Slope is the change in y divided by the change in x
- A positive relationship slopes up and a negative one slopes down
  ::
