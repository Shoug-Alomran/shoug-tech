## What monopolistic competition is | Slides 3-7 | must
A market with:

- A **large number of firms**.
- Firms that compete on **product quality, price and marketing**.
- **Free entry and exit.**

**Many firms** means each has a small share, firms ignore each other's moves, and **collusion is impossible**. The handwriting notes that collusion is common in oligopoly but not here.

**Product differentiation:** Bunn Café's coffee differs a little from other cafés' in its beans, recipe, location and service. It's a close substitute but not a perfect one, so Bunn faces a **downward-sloping demand curve** and has **some control over price**.

| Way to compete | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Quality** | Design, reliability, service, location |
| **Price** | Downward-sloping demand allows a price choice |
| **Marketing** | Advertising and packaging |

::analogy Cafés in Riyadh
Hundreds of cafés, each a little different. Raise your price and some customers leave, but not all, since they like your specific coffee or location.
::
::key
- Many firms, differentiated products, free entry and exit
- Each firm faces a downward-sloping demand curve
- Firms compete on quality, price and marketing
- Collusion is impossible with so many firms
::

## Short-run profit and loss | Slides 8-11 | must
In the short run, the firm acts like a **single-price monopoly**: produce where MR = MC and read the price from demand.

| Season | Output (MR = MC) | Price | ATC | P − ATC | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Busy** (cooler months) | 200 cups | 30 SAR | 14 SAR | +16 | **Profit 3,200 SAR** a day |
| **Slow** (summer) | 50 cups | 10 SAR | 14 SAR | −4 | **Loss 200 SAR** a day |

::steps Four steps (slide 11)
Find the output where MR cuts MC.
Go up to the demand curve to read the price.
Read ATC at that same output.
Profit or loss = (P − ATC) × Q.
::

**Long run:** economic profit attracts new cafés, which takes customers from each firm, until profit is zero. Losses make some cafés exit, until the rest break even.

::mnemonic Profit Pulls entrants, Loss Lets firms leave
**P**rofit **p**ulls new firms in, and **l**osses **l**et firms leave. Either way, in the long run, firms break even.
::
::key
- In the short run the firm acts like a monopoly: MR = MC, price from demand
- It can make a profit, break even or make a loss in the short run
- Entry removes profit and exit removes losses in the long run
::

## The three market structures | Slides 12-13 | must
| | Perfect competition | Monopolistic competition | Monopoly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number of firms | Many | Many | One |
| Product | Identical | **Differentiated** | No close substitutes |
| Barriers to entry | None | None | Natural, ownership or legal |
| Firm's demand curve | Horizontal at market price | **Slopes down** | The market demand curve |
| Output rule | MR = MC | MR = MC | MR = MC |
| Short run | Profit, break even or loss | Profit, break even or loss | Profit, break even or loss |
| Can economic profit last? | No | **No** | **Yes** |
| Example | Date farms | Cafés, abaya shops | A town's only water supplier |

::mnemonic Everyone uses MR = MC
The output rule is **the same** in all three. What changes is the demand curve and whether entry is possible.
::
::trap Monopolistic competition doesn't keep profits
It has "monopoly" in the name, but free entry means profit disappears in the long run, as in perfect competition.
::
::key
- All three structures produce where MR = MC
- Monopolistic competition: many firms, differentiated products, free entry
- Only monopoly keeps economic profit in the long run
::
