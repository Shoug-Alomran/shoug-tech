## Demand | Slides 4-20 | must
If you **demand** something, you want it, can afford it and plan to buy it.

**The law of demand:** other things remaining the same, the higher the price of a good, the smaller the quantity demanded. Why? **The substitution effect** (buyers switch to alternatives) and **the income effect** (a higher price makes your budget go less far).

A **demand curve** is also a **willingness-and-ability-to-pay** curve: it slopes down because each extra unit is worth less to buyers.

**The most important distinction in the chapter:**

| | Change in quantity demanded | Change in demand |
|---|---|---|
| Caused by | The good's **own price** | Anything else |
| On the graph | **Movement along** the curve | **The whole curve shifts** |

::plot A movement stays on one curve, and a shift creates a new one | quantity | price | 11 | 10
line D1 2: 1,9 8,2
line D2 5: 3,9 10,2
dot A: 3,7
dot B: 6,4
::

Six factors **shift** demand:

| Factor | Demand increases (shifts right) when |
|---|---|
| **R**elated goods | A substitute's price rises, or a complement's price falls |
| **I**ncome | Income rises, for a normal good |
| **P**references | People like it more |
| **P**opulation | More people |
| **E**xpected future prices | Price is expected to rise |
| **E**xpected future income or credit | Income expected to rise, credit easy |

::mnemonic RIPE demand
**R**elated goods, **I**ncome, **P**references and **P**opulation, **E**xpected prices and **E**xpected income. The P and the E each count twice.
::
::check The price of coffee rises. What happens to the demand for tea (a substitute)? And to sugar for coffee (a complement)?
Demand for tea increases: it shifts right. Demand for sugar decreases: it shifts left.
::
::key
- Law of demand: higher price, smaller quantity demanded
- Own price causes a movement along the curve
- Anything else shifts the curve
- Six shifters: related goods, income, preferences, population, expected prices, expected income
::

## Supply | Slides 21-36 | must
**Law of supply:** the higher the price, the greater the quantity supplied. A supply curve is a **minimum-supply-price** curve: it slopes up because marginal cost increases.

Own price causes a **movement along** the supply curve. These six things shift it:

| Factor | Supply increases (shifts right) when |
|---|---|
| **F**actor prices | Input costs fall |
| **R**elated goods produced | A substitute in production gets cheaper, or a complement in production gets dearer |
| **E**xpected future prices | Price expected to fall (sell now) |
| **T**echnology | Technology improves |
| **S**uppliers (number) | More sellers enter |
| **N**ature (state of) | Good weather, no disasters |

::mnemonic Supply FRETS about Nature
**F**actor prices, **R**elated goods, **E**xpected prices, **T**echnology, **S**uppliers, and **N**ature.
::
::trap Expected price works opposite ways
If prices are expected to **rise**: buyers want more **now** (demand rises) while sellers hold stock back (supply falls). Both push today's price up.
::
::key
- Law of supply: higher price, greater quantity supplied
- Supply is a minimum-supply-price curve
- Six shifters: factor prices, related goods, expected prices, technology, number of suppliers, state of nature
::

## Market equilibrium | Slides 37-42 | must
Equilibrium is the price at which **quantity demanded equals quantity supplied**.

::example Find the equilibrium (illustrative numbers)
| Price | Qd | Qs | Situation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 16 | 4 | **Shortage** of 12 |
| 2 | 13 | 7 | Shortage of 6 |
| 3 | 10 | 10 | **Equilibrium** |
| 4 | 7 | 13 | **Surplus** of 6 |

Below equilibrium a **shortage** pushes the price up. Above equilibrium a **surplus** pushes it down. Equilibrium is at a price of 3 and a quantity of 10.
::
::tip Equilibrium from equations
Set the two equal. If Qd = 19 − 3P and Qs = 1 + 3P, then 19 − 3P = 1 + 3P, so P = 3 and Q = 10.
::
::key
- At equilibrium quantity demanded equals quantity supplied
- Below equilibrium there is a shortage, and price rises
- Above equilibrium there is a surplus, and price falls
::

## Predicting price and quantity | Slides 43-52 | must
::steps Four-step method
Does the event affect buyers (demand) or sellers (supply)?
Which factor changed, and does the curve shift right or left?
Draw it, or use the table below.
Read off the new price and quantity.
::

| Change | Price | Quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Demand up | Up | Up |
| Demand down | Down | Down |
| Supply up | **Down** | Up |
| Supply down | **Up** | Down |

When **both** curves shift, one result is always uncertain:

| Demand | Supply | Price | Quantity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up | Up | **?** | Up |
| Down | Down | **?** | Down |
| Up | Down | Up | **?** |
| Down | Up | Down | **?** |

::mnemonic Same way: Quantity's sure. Opposite: Price is sure.
When both shift the **same** way, you know **quantity**. When they shift **opposite** ways, you know **price**.
::
::example Worked headlines
**"Drought cuts date harvest."** Supply decreases: price up, quantity down.

**"Incomes rise, and a new machine cuts production cost for a normal good."** Demand up and supply up: quantity up; price uncertain.
::
::class Reading the uncertain case
The handwriting says: the result depends on the magnitude of each shift. Write "ambiguous" unless the question tells you which is larger.
::
::key
- Decide which curve moves and which way
- More demand raises price and quantity
- More supply lowers price and raises quantity
- Both same direction: quantity is known and price is uncertain
- Opposite directions: price is known and quantity is uncertain
::

Explain the difference between a change in demand and a change in quantity demanded.
~ price, movement, shift, curve, other factors
> A change in the good's own price causes a change in quantity demanded, a movement along the curve. A change in any other factor causes a change in demand, a shift of the whole curve.
@short 37
Explain how a shortage returns a market to equilibrium.
~ shortage, below, price rises, quantity
> At a price below equilibrium, quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied. Buyers bid up the price, which raises quantity supplied and lowers quantity demanded until they are equal.
