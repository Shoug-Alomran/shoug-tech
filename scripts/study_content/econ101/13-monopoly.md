## How monopoly arises | Slides 2-6 | must

A monopoly has two features:

1. **No close substitutes.** The handwriting's example is Saudi Electricity: nothing practical replaces electricity.
2. **A barrier to entry.**

| Barrier       | Meaning                                                         | Example                                                 |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Natural**   | Economies of scale: one firm supplies the market at lowest cost | Electricity, water distribution                         |
| **Ownership** | One firm owns most of a key resource                            | Diamond mines                                           |
| **Legal**     | The government restricts entry                                  | Public franchise, government licence, patent, copyright |

::mnemonic NOLe barriers
**N**atural, **O**wnership, **Le**gal.
::
::key

- A monopoly has one firm, no close substitutes and a barrier to entry
- Natural monopoly comes from economies of scale
- Legal barriers include franchises, licences, patents and copyrights
  ::

## Why marginal revenue is less than price | Slides 7-10 | must

Sahra Tours has the only franchise to run canyon tours. Fixed cost is 225 SAR a day.

| Tours | Price   | TR      | MR  | TC      | MC  | ATC    | Profit  |
| ----- | ------- | ------- | --- | ------- | --- | ------ | ------- |
| 2     | 160     | 320     | 140 | 250     | 15  | 125    | 70      |
| 3     | 140     | 420     | 100 | 270     | 20  | 90     | 150     |
| **4** | **120** | **480** | 60  | **300** | 30  | **75** | **180** |
| 5     | 100     | 500     | 20  | 350     | 50  | 70     | 150     |
| 6     | 80      | 480     | −20 | 440     | 90  | 73.3   | 40      |

To sell one more tour, the monopoly must **lower the price on every tour**, not just the extra one.

::steps From 2 to 3 tours
**Revenue gain:** the 3rd tour sells for 140.
**Revenue loss:** the first 2 tours now sell for 140 instead of 160, a loss of 2 × 20 = 40.
MR = 140 − 40 = **100**, which is less than the price of 140.
::
::tip Competition versus monopoly
In perfect competition MR is horizontal at the price. For a monopoly, **MR is below the demand curve and falls faster**.
::
::key

- To sell more, a monopoly must lower the price on all units
- So MR is less than price
- The MR curve lies below the demand curve
  ::

## Output, price and profit | Slides 11-18 | must

::steps The four-step graph method (the handwriting's)
**Output:** find where MR = MC. Sahra: **4 tours**.
**Price:** go **up** to the demand curve. Buyers pay **120**.
**Cost:** read ATC at that output: **75**.
**Profit:** (P − ATC) × Q = (120 − 75) × 4 = **180 SAR** a day.
::

::plot Monopoly: output from MR = MC, price from the demand curve | tours per day | SAR per tour | 7 | 210
line D 2: 0,200 6.5,70
line MR 5: 0,200 4.5,20
curve MC 4: 1,10 3,20 4,40 5,60 6,95
dash - 1: 0,120 4,120
dot MR=MC: 4,40
dot price: 4,120
::

From 3 to 4 tours MR (60) > MC (30), so profit rises by 30. From 4 to 5, MR (20) < MC (50), so profit falls by 30.

|                           | Perfect competition  | Single-price monopoly         |
| ------------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Price vs MR               | P = MR               | P > MR                        |
| At best output            | P = MC               | **P > MC**                    |
| Can economic profit last? | No: entry removes it | **Yes: barriers block entry** |

::trap The price isn't read where MR = MC
That intersection gives the **quantity**. The price comes from going **up to the demand curve**. Reading 40 as the price is the most common graph mistake.
::
::mnemonic Cross, then Climb
Find where MR and MC **cross** for the quantity, then **climb** up to demand for the price.
::
::key

- Produce where MR = MC
- Charge the highest price on the demand curve for that quantity
- Profit = (P − ATC) × Q
- A monopoly's price exceeds its marginal cost
- Barriers let monopoly profit last in the long run
  ::
