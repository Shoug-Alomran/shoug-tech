## A program, not a project | Slide 3 | must

A **project** ends when its deliverable is complete. **EA never finishes**, because the enterprise keeps changing and so must its architecture. Therefore it needs ongoing governance, staffing and budget.

::analogy A garden, not a building
You finish constructing a house and hand over the keys. A garden needs tending forever. EA is the garden.
::
::trap "We finished the EA"
Any statement treating EA as a one-time documentation exercise is wrong.
::
::key

- A project ends, but EA is continuous
- EA needs ongoing governance, staffing and budget
  ::

## The four parts of the plan | Slides 4-10 | must

| Part                                | Question                | What lives in it                                          | University example                                                               |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. EA Program Management**        | How is the program run? | Roles, sponsorship, governance, budget, staffing          | Chief Architect reports to the CIO; $250K annual budget; quarterly board updates |
| **2. Current Architecture Summary** | What exists today?      | Baseline of all five levels, known gaps                   | Three disconnected student-record systems                                        |
| **3. Future Architecture Summary**  | Where are we headed?    | Target state, sequencing, tactical and strategic horizons | One cloud platform by Q3 2027, phased rollout                                    |
| **4. Glossary & References**        | What do our terms mean? | Terms, acronyms, standards                                | Defines "advising portal"; cites PDPL and TOGAF 9.2                              |

::mnemonic Please Check Future Glossaries
**P**rogram management, **C**urrent summary, **F**uture summary, **G**lossary. In order.
::

How they work together:

- **Part 1** provides the governance that makes the other three possible.
- **Part 2** is the baseline that every decision is measured against.
- **Part 3** defines the target and the sequence for closing the gap.
- **Part 4** keeps everyone speaking the same language.

::tip Why a glossary deserves its own part
When an executive, a business owner and an engineer use one term differently, projects go wrong. The handwriting on slide 8 calls it eliminating the communication barrier that jargon creates.
::
::check Where would you put "the plan to retire the old payroll system within 18 months"?
**Part 3**, Future Architecture Summary, as part of the sequencing plan.
::
::key

- Four parts: program management, current summary, future summary, glossary and references
- Part 1 covers roles, governance, budget and staffing
- Part 2 is the as-is baseline
- Part 3 has the to-be target and sequencing plan
- Part 4 provides shared vocabulary and standards

- EA: never finishes

- 1 Program Management: how it runs
- 2 Current Summary: what exists
- 3 Future Summary: where we go
- 4 Glossary & Refs: what terms mean

### Horizons

- Tactical: 1-2 years
- Strategic: 3-10 years
