## Why the links matter | Slides 3-5 | must

To support an enterprise holistically, EA must link **strategy, business and technology, so that strategy drives the other two, not the other way around**.

EA supports both: **top-down** (executive planning across the whole enterprise) and **bottom-up** (management planning within each line of business).

Strategy is documented using three things:

| Element                   | Meaning                                              | Example                                        |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Strategic goals**       | Primary objectives, typically taking several years   | Achieve open banking compliance                |
| **Strategic initiatives** | Activities, programs and projects enabling the goals | Launch an API platform                         |
| **Strategic measures**    | Outcome measures showing when a goal is met (KPIs)   | Third-party integrations live within 12 months |

::mnemonic GIM: Goals, then How, then Proof
**G**oals say what, **I**nitiatives say how, **M**easures say whether it worked.
::
::trap An initiative isn't a goal
"Launch an API platform" is what you **do**. "Achieve compliance" is what you **want**.
::
::key

- Strategy should drive business and technology planning
- EA supports top-down and bottom-up planning at once
- Three elements: goals, initiatives, measures
  ::

## Business and technology links | Slides 6-9 | must

Strategy creates business requirements, and technology supports solutions for them. At the business level, EA documents three things:

| Item                                     | Question                                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Supporting strategic goals**           | Which business activities support which goals?       |
| **Documentation of business activities** | How are products and services created and delivered? |
| **Identifying supporting technologies**  | What technologies do those requirements call for?    |

**The warning on slide 8:** technology is a resource that enables information and other flows. It should **not drive** business and strategy planning, particularly in resource-constrained enterprises that can't afford duplicative, non-strategic technologies.

::analogy Buying a tool, then looking for a job for it
You wouldn't buy a forklift because forklifts are exciting and then look for something to lift. You start with what needs lifting.
::
::steps Amanah Bank through the three links
**Strategy.** Goal: open banking compliance. Initiative: launch an API platform. Measure: integrations live within 12 months.
**Business.** The new API platform directly supports the goal, and the activity is documented.
**Technology.** An API gateway that specifically supports this activity, rather than a general-purpose purchase.
::
::check A company buys an AI tool since competitors did, then looks for a use for it. What's wrong?
Technology is driving strategy. It should begin with a strategic goal and a business requirement, and then identify a technology that supports it.
::
::key

- Strategy creates business requirements and technology supports them
- EA documents supporting activities, business activities and supporting technologies
- Technology shouldn't drive strategy, especially in resource-constrained firms
