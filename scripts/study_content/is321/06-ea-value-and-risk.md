## Why EA is worth it | Slides 3-8 | must
EA's unique capability is combining views of strategy, business and technology so the enterprise can see itself now and in future. That produces three **pillars of value**:

| Pillar | Benefit |
|---|---|
| **Improved planning** | Supports top-down and bottom-up planning together, and gives projects a reference baseline |
| **Improved decision-making** | An up-to-date repository shows gaps and overlaps, so decisions are faster |
| **Improved communication** | A 24×7 gallery of documents and shared vocabulary, which is especially useful for dispersed teams |

::mnemonic PDC: Please Don't Complain
**P**lanning, **D**ecision-making, **C**ommunication. Say it to the executive asking why EA costs so much.
::

The **ten value factors**, as numbered on slides 7 and 8:

| # | Factor | Why it saves money |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shorter planning cycles | Online repository speeds up planning |
| 2 | More effective planning meetings | A common baseline reduces ambiguity |
| 3 | Shorter decision cycles | Faster crosswalk of strategy, business and technology |
| 4 | Improved reference information | Information is gathered the same way every time |
| 5 | Fewer duplicate resources | Overlaps become visible, enabling consolidation |
| 6 | Reduced re-work | Avoids duplicate or conflicting capabilities |
| 7 | Better resource integration | Comparing current and future requirements surfaces gaps |
| 8 | Fewer people in a process | Process reengineering removes repetitive steps |
| 9 | Improved communication | A common language reduces misunderstood requirements |
| 10 | Reduced cycle time | Every resource evaluated from the same holistic viewpoint; linked to agility |

::tip Group them to remember them
**Time:** 1, 2, 3, 10. **Information:** 4, 9. **Resources:** 5, 6, 7, 8.
::
::key
- Three pillars: planning, decision-making, communication
- Ten quantifiable value factors translate EA value to the bottom line
- Most factors save time, improve information or reduce duplicated resources
::

## What it costs | Slides 9-10 | must
Costs should be viewed across the **full program lifecycle**: implementation, maintenance and refreshment.

| # | Cost category | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Staffing & administration | Chief Architect and EA team salaries |
| 2 | Stakeholder sessions | Meetings, facilities, materials |
| 3 | Repository technology | Computers, applications, web development |
| 4 | Documentation | Interviews and materials for views and the plan |
| 5 | Modeling tools | EA modeling applications (e.g. ArchiMate tools) |
| 6 | Ongoing updates | Regular, typically annual, updates |

Two patterns to learn:

- **Starting costs more than maintaining.** Establishing EA costs more than updating it, because you're building new processes and capabilities.
- **Labour is by far the largest cost**, followed by supporting technology, then facilities.

::analogy Building, then maintaining a house
Construction is the big bill. After that come smaller, regular maintenance bills. And in construction, as in EA, it's the skilled labour you pay for the most.
::
::key
- Six cost categories across the lifecycle
- Initial setup costs more than ongoing maintenance
- Labour is the largest cost
::

## What can go wrong, and what to do | Slides 11-12 | must
Risk relates to **uncertainty about a potential cause of failure**. Managing risk means lowering or eliminating that uncertainty.

| Risk | What happens | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Financial** | Setup needs more funding than updates; budgets get cut | Solidify budgets; don't be the first to adopt new EA tools |
| **Loss of key personnel** | Few have these skills; losing them delays everything | Ensure trained back-ups exist on the EA team |
| **Lack of acceptance** | Stakeholders resist the shift to EA-level planning | Strengthen executive support |
| **Schedule delays** | Slippage makes documentation outdated | Use a detailed methodology and program management skills |

::mnemonic Risks raise red FLAgS
**F**inancial, **L**oss of key personnel, **A**cceptance, **S**chedule.
::
::trap Not a one-time activity
Slide 12 says risk identification and mitigation is an **ongoing management review item**.
::
::check Your only certified architect has resigned. Which risk, and what should have been done?
**Loss of key personnel.** There should have been a trained back-up through cross-training.
::
::key
- Four risks: financial, loss of personnel, lack of acceptance, schedule delays
- Five mitigations: executive support, solid budgets, trained back-ups, detailed methodology, program management skills
- Risk management is an ongoing review item
::

## AI-based risk analysis | Slides 13-16 | know
| Use | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Identifying risks** | Scans the repository for patterns that a manual review would miss | Flags a line of business with no documented architect |
| **Predictive risk modeling** | Trained on previous programs | Programs with no sponsor update in year two faced delays 70% of the time |
| **Cost and value optimization** | Models the trade-offs across value and cost | Hiring a second architect vs slower updates |

::key
- AI identifies risks that manual reviews might miss
- Predictive models make risk management proactive
- AI can model cost and value trade-offs
::
