== ch1 | 262 | Ch 1 | CH 1–1.3 | Principles, domains and tailoring (recap)
> Midterm material. Recap the lists and the scenario cues; full notes are in the [[Midterm Cheat Sheet|/academics/software-engineering/se423/extra-resources/midterm-cheat-sheet/]].
[grid]
[card Portfolio, program, project, product]
- **Portfolio:** projects, programs and operations managed as a group for strategic goals.
- **Program:** related projects managed together for benefits you could not get separately.
- **Project:** temporary endeavour to create a unique product, service or result.
- **Product:** an artifact that is produced; **product management** spans its whole life cycle (introduction, growth, maturity, decline) and can start programs or projects at any point.
- Value chain: deliverables → **outcomes** → benefits → **value** (worth, judged differently by each stakeholder).
!trap Product management lasts the product's life; project management ends with the project. Read the word twice.
[/card]
[card Project vs general management (Final 251)]
- **Project management:** temporary, unique work; a cross-functional team assembled for the project; goals of scope, time, cost, quality; the PM leads often without formal authority.
- **General (operations) management:** ongoing, repetitive work; permanent functional staff; efficiency and stability; line authority.
[/card]
[card The 12 principles and their cues]
[table]
Principle | Scenario cue
Stewardship | Security flaw found; fix it even if it delays (integrity, care)
Team | Collaborative, accountable environment
Stakeholders | University wants feature X, students want Y
Value | Limited resources: pick the feature with most benefit
Systems thinking | Database change affects payment and registration
Leadership | Motivate, influence, coach
Tailoring | Plan made for a small project, now large and complex
Quality | Build quality into processes and deliverables
Complexity | Many interacting parts, ambiguity
Risk | Optimize responses to threats and opportunities
Adaptability and resiliency | Absorb a shock, recover
Change | New regulation changes the requirements
[/table]
[/card]
[card Eight performance domains]
Stakeholders · team · development approach and life cycle · planning · project work · delivery · measurement · uncertainty.
!hook "Some Teams Develop Plans, Projects Deliver Measured Uncertainty".
[/card]
[card Key compares (Ch 1.2)]
- **Predictive** (requirements known) vs **hybrid** vs **adaptive** (high uncertainty, short iterations).
- **Contingency reserve:** identified risks, PM controls. **Management reserve:** unknown unknowns, management controls.
- **Leading** indicators predict (change request rate); **lagging** measure afterwards.
- Dependencies: mandatory, discretionary, external, internal.
- Threat responses: avoid, escalate, transfer, mitigate, accept. Opportunity: **exploit** (make sure it happens), escalate, share, **enhance** (raise the chance), accept.
[/card]
[card Tailoring (repeated)]
- Deliberately adapting the approach, governance and processes to the context.
- Four steps: select initial approach → tailor for the organization → tailor for the project → improve continuously.
- Situations: poor quality → more verification; unsure team → more guidance and training; slow approvals → streamline.
- Model = thinking strategy; method = means to an outcome; artifact = template or document.
- Models: Situational Leadership II, OSCAR, Theory X/Y/Z, hygiene vs motivators, ADKAR, Kotter, Cynefin, Stacey, Tuckman, Drexler/Sibbet, Salience.
[/card]
[/grid]

== ch23 | 205 | Ch 2–3 | CH 2–3 | Organizational context and team (recap)
[grid]
[card Organization structures]
[table]
Structure | Strength | Weakness
Functional (grouped by similar activities) | No disruption, deep expertise | Silos, slow response, weak project focus
Project (teams for temporary assignments) | PM has sole authority, fast decisions | Expensive, people lose home base
Matrix | Dynamic, efficient use of people | Two bosses, conflict
[/table]
PMO forms: supportive (low control), controlling (moderate), directive (high). Models: weather station, control tower, resource pool.
[/card]
[card Project selection (Activity 4)]
[calc]
payback          = years before cumulative cash turns positive
                   + unrecovered / next year's cash flow
discounted CF    = CF_t / (1 + r + inflation)^t       (here 1.11)
NPV              = sum of discounted CF - initial investment

AlManar (r 8% + inflation 3%):
payback     Z 2.8 yrs  <  X 3.0  <  Y 3.6
disc. pay.  Z 3.32     <  X 3.51 <  Y 4.43
NPV         Z 342,325  >  X 258,423  >  Y 100,790   -> choose Z
[/calc]
!tip Then the strategic reflection: the numbers favour Z, but say which strategic goal each project serves (Y = sustainability) before recommending.
[/card]
[card Tuckman (repeated)]
**Forming** (polite, unclear roles) → **storming** (conflict over roles and approach) → **norming** (agreed norms, trust) → **performing** (self-directed, productive) → **adjourning** (closure, recognition).
[/card]
[card Team needs pyramid]
Trust → managed conflict → commitment → accountability → results.
- Trust: vulnerability-based (admit mistakes, ask for help).
- Commitment = **clarity + buy-in**: everyone's view is heard, decisions are clear even when not unanimous.
- Accountability: peers hold each other to standards.
[/card]
[card Conflict techniques]
Confronting / problem solving (best: root cause) · collaborating · compromising · smoothing / accommodating · forcing · withdrawal / avoiding.
[/card]
[card Communication paths and RACI]
- Paths = n(n − 1) / 2. **7 members → 21** (Final 242). Keep teams small (7 ± 2).
- RACI: **R**esponsible (does the work), **A**ccountable (one owner), **C**onsulted, **I**nformed.
- Consensus steps: pose the problem → solicit options → discuss pros and cons → work toward the most beneficial → confirm agreement.
- Technical leadership (known fix, leader directs) vs **adaptive** (no known answer, leader frames questions and mobilizes the team).
[/card]
[/grid]

== ch4 | 190 | Ch 4 | CH 04 | Development approach, agile, Scrum and DevOps
> New since the midterm. Scrum has the most practice questions of any topic (17).
[grid]
[card Process landscape]
- Process = activities; process model = their order; methodology = process + roles, skills, techniques, work products, milestones, standards, quality.
- **Ad hoc** (code and fix) · **prescriptive:** sequential (waterfall), evolutionary (iterative, spiral), unified process · **adaptive:** lean and agile.
- Project life cycle (the project's phases) ≠ SDLC (the software's phases).
[/card]
[card Waterfall]
- Suits: clear stable requirements, proven technology, low complexity, enough time.
- Failure symptoms: late integration, late risk resolution, adversarial stakeholders, requirements frozen too early.
- Fixes scope, then estimates time and cost. Agile fixes time and cost, then estimates scope.
[/card]
[card Iterative / evolutionary]
Phases: inception → **elaboration** (most critical: architecture and main risks) → construction → transition. Suits unclear requirements, new technology, high complexity.
[/card]
[card Agile Manifesto]
Individuals and interactions over processes and tools · working software over documentation · customer collaboration over contract negotiation · responding to change over following a plan.
!trap "Over" means both sides have value; the left side is valued more.
[/card]
[card Scrum]
- **Product owner:** product-focused, owns and orders the backlog. **ScrumMaster:** team-focused, removes impediments, coaches the process. Team of 5–8, self-organizing.
- Sprint 2–4 weeks: planning → **daily scrum** (15 min) → review (demo) → retrospective.
- Artifacts: **product backlog**, **sprint backlog**, potentially shippable increment. Velocity = points done per sprint.
- Backlog item states: ready for consideration → ready for refinement → ready for implementation. Activities: refine, estimate, create, prioritize.
[/card]
[card XP practices]
User stories (incremental planning), small releases, test-driven development, continuous integration, refactoring, pair programming, collective ownership, simple design, on-site customer, sustainable pace.
[/card]
[card DevOps (repeated)]
- Development and operations as one team; continuous integration, delivery and deployment.
- PM best practice: collaborative culture, **MVP** mindset, on-time quality delivery, map dependencies, let tools automate.
- Metrics: **deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate, MTTR**.
[/card]
[/grid]
!tip "Which approach for this case?" Name it, give two features of the case that fit (requirement stability, risk, team size and location, regulation), and say what you lose. That is the Artifact 2 analysis in miniature.

== ch5 | 0 | Ch 5 | CH 05 | Risk management
[grid]
[card Definition and kinds]
- Risk = an uncertain event with a **positive or negative** effect.
- Known risks · predictable unknowns · unpredictable unknowns · unknowable.
- **Project** risks (plan, schedule, cost) · **technical** (quality and timeliness of the software) · **business** (market, strategy, sales, management, budget).
[/card]
[card Process]
Identification → analysis (probability × impact) → planning (responses) → monitoring and control.
- Plan contents: methodology, roles, budget, timing, categories (RBS), probability/impact definitions and matrix, tolerances, reporting, tracking.
[/card]
[card Identification techniques (pick 2 and explain)]
- **Brainstorming:** team lists risks freely, then groups them.
- **Delphi:** anonymous experts, repeated rounds until consensus (no dominant voice).
- Interviews, **SWOT**, **checklists** (past projects: size, business, customer, process, technology, environment, staff), **assumptions analysis**, **root cause / fishbone**, documentation reviews.
[/card]
[card Analysis]
[calc]
risk exposure  RE = probability x size of loss
example        25% x 4 weeks = 1 week of exposure
scale: <20% highly unlikely | 20-40 unlikely | 40-60 even
       60-80 likely | >80 highly likely
[/calc]
Probability-impact matrix, then the 80/20 rule: a few risks carry most exposure, so manage the top 10 list.
[/card]
[card Responses]
- Threats: **avoid** (remove the cause, cut scope) · **transfer** (insurance, fixed-price contract) · **mitigate** (lower probability or impact) · **accept** (contingency plan or reserve) · escalate.
- Opportunities: **exploit** · **share** · **enhance** · accept · escalate.
- **Residual risk** remains after the response; **secondary risk** is created by the response.
[/card]
[card Register and monitoring]
- **Risk register:** ID, description, category, probability, impact, exposure, first indicator (trigger), owner, response, status. The Artifact 2 sheet used exactly these columns.
- **RMMM:** risk mitigation, monitoring and management plan.
- Monitoring: top 10 list, reassessments, audits, workarounds, **miniature milestones**.
[/card]
[/grid]
!tip Final 251 Q2d: name two identification techniques, explain how each finds risks, then mitigation (a response per risk), prioritization (exposure or P×I matrix) and monitoring (register reviewed each status meeting, triggers, top 10 list).

== ch6 | 45 | Ch 6 | CH 06 | Estimation and COCOMO
[grid]
[card Methods: pros and cons]
[table]
Method | How | Pro | Con
Expert judgment | Experienced people estimate | Fast, uses experience | Subjective
Analogy | Compare with a finished similar project | Real data | Needs a truly similar project
Top-down | Total first, split into parts | Quick, early | Misses detail
Bottom-up | Estimate each WBS work package, add up | Most accurate | Slow, needs a WBS
Wideband Delphi | Anonymous rounds to consensus | Reduces bias | Takes time
Algorithmic (LOC, FP, COCOMO) | Formula from size | Repeatable | Size must be estimated first
Priced to win | Whatever wins the contract | Wins bids | Unrealistic
[/table]
!tip "Validate your estimates" (Final 251): use two methods and compare, check against historical data, review with the team (Delphi), and re-estimate as the cone of uncertainty narrows.
[/card]
[card LOC vs function points]
- **LOC:** easy to count after coding; language-dependent, unknown early.
- **Function points:** count inputs, outputs, inquiries, internal files, external interfaces; weight simple/average/complex (inputs ×3/×4/×6); adjust by complexity factor. Language-independent, usable from requirements.
- Code reuse: more than 50% modified counts as new.
[/card]
[/grid]
### COCOMO
[grid wide]
[card Equations and constants]
[calc]
effort        E    = a (KLOC)^b          person-months
time          TDEV = c (E)^d             months
staff         S    = E / TDEV            people
productivity  P    = LOC / E             LOC per person-month

mode           KLOC     a     b      c     d
organic        2-50     2.4   1.05   2.5   0.38
semi-detached  50-300   3.0   1.12   2.5   0.35
embedded       >300     3.6   1.20   2.5   0.32
[/calc]
!trap Productivity uses **LOC**, not KLOC: 20 KLOC = 20,000 LOC.
[/card]
[card Final 251: 20 KLOC, organic]
[calc]
E    = 2.4 x 20^1.05   = 2.4 x 23.23 = 55.76 PM
TDEV = 2.5 x 55.76^0.38 = 2.5 x 4.61 = 11.52 months
S    = 55.76 / 11.52   = 4.84  ~ 5 people
P    = 20,000 / 55.76  = 358.7 LOC/PM
[/calc]
Final 242, 32 KLOC organic: E = 91.33 PM, TDEV = 13.90 months, S = 6.57, P = 350.4 LOC/PM.
[/card]
[card Activity 8: 95 KLOC, embedded]
[calc]
E    = 3.6 x 95^1.20   = 850.30 PM
TDEV = 2.5 x 850.3^0.32 = 21.65 months
S    = 850.30 / 21.65  = 39.3 people
client plan: 17 people, 14-18 months -> 850 / 17 = 50 months
[/calc]
!tip Basic COCOMO uses size only. **Intermediate COCOMO** multiplies effort by an adjustment factor from 15 cost drivers (product, hardware, personnel, project: RELY, CPLX, ACAP, TOOL…).
[/card]
[card Three-point (PERT) and other ideas]
[calc]
expected  E  = (O + 4M + P) / 6
std dev   sd = (P - O) / 6
O = 4, M = 6, P = 14  ->  E = (4 + 24 + 14)/6 = 7 days, sd = 1.67
[/calc]
- Cone of uncertainty: estimates converge as the project progresses.
- Effort vs duration: duration = effort / people (labor time vs clock time).
- Over-estimates waste time (Parkinson's Law, Student Syndrome); under-estimates cause rushing and defects.
- Present estimates as ranges with ± qualifiers; separate target from committed dates.
[/card]
[/grid]

== ch62 | 330 | Ch 6.2 | CH 6.2 | Scheduling and tracking
> Question 4 of both finals: draw the network, run the passes, find the critical path. Practise until it takes under ten minutes.
[grid]
[card WBS (repeated)]
- **Process (activity) WBS:** lists activities; used by the PM. **Product (entity) WBS:** lists the things built; used by engineers. Hybrid is allowed, but stay consistent.
- **Work package:** smallest unit, about 1–2 people for 1–2 weeks.
- Built top-down, bottom-up, by analogy or brainstorming.
[/card]
[card Forward and backward pass]
[calc]
forward:   ES = max(EF of predecessors)     EF = ES + duration
backward:  LF = min(LS of successors)       LS = LF - duration
slack      = LS - ES = LF - EF
critical path = longest path = all zero-slack activities
[/calc]
Six-compartment node: ES · duration · EF on top, LS · slack · LF below, task name in the middle.
[/card]
[card Leads, lags, what-if]
- **Lag (FS+1d):** wait one day after the predecessor finishes.
- **Lead (FS−1d):** start one day before it finishes (fast-tracking).
- Resource levelling removes over-allocation (one person on two tasks) and may lengthen the schedule.
[/card]
[/grid]
[grid wide]
[card Worked: Activity 9 "Wishlist"]
[table]
Task | Dur | Pred | ES | EF | LS | LF | Slack
A Kickoff | 1 | – | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | **0**
B Specs | 2 | A | 1 | 3 | 1 | 3 | **0**
C Build API | 4 | B | 3 | 7 | 5 | 9 | 2
D DB schema | 3 | B | 3 | 6 | 6 | 9 | 3
E UI mockups | 3 | B | 3 | 6 | 3 | 6 | **0**
F UI components | 5 | E | 6 | 11 | 6 | 11 | **0**
G Test cases | 3 | B | 3 | 6 | 8 | 11 | 5
H Integrate API+DB | 2 | C, D | 7 | 9 | 9 | 11 | 2
I System test | 3 | F, G, H | 11 | 14 | 11 | 14 | **0**
J Deploy | 1 | I | 14 | 15 | 14 | 15 | **0**
[/table]
Critical path **A → B → E → F → I → J = 15 days**. Other paths: A-B-C-H-I-J = 13, A-B-D-H-I-J = 12, A-B-G-I-J = 10.
!tip What-if: G after E with a 1-day lag ends on day 10 (no change). F starting 1 day before E finishes (lead) ends on day 10, so I starts at 10 and the project drops to **14 days**; both A-B-E-F-I-J and A-B-E-G-I-J become critical.
[/card]
[card Gantt vs burndown (Final 251 Q6)]
- **Gantt chart:** bars for tasks on a timeline; shows start, duration, finish, milestones (zero-length diamonds, e.g. "beta released") and dependencies (arrows, e.g. testing after coding).
- Gantt advantages: easy to read and communicate; easy to update; shows overlap and progress against plan.
- Gantt limitations: vague about how much work remains; does not always show dependencies; hides the effect of early/late starts; cluttered for big projects.
- **Burndown chart:** x-axis = **time** (days/sprints), y-axis = **work remaining** (points or hours). Actual line above the ideal line = behind schedule; below = ahead.
[/card]
[/grid]
[grid]
[card Compressing the schedule]
- **Crashing:** add resources to critical-path activities. Shorter, but **costs more**.
- **Fast-tracking:** run sequential activities in parallel. Shorter, but **more risk and rework**.
- Brooks' law: adding people to a late project makes it later (ramp-up, more communication paths).
[/card]
[card Critical chain]
CPM plus resource limits. Remove padding from tasks and pool it into a **project buffer** at the end and **feeder buffers** where non-critical chains join the critical chain.
[/card]
[card Tracking]
[calc]
CPI = EV / AC    SPI = EV / PV
  (below 1 = bad)
CV = EV - AC     SV = EV - PV
  (negative = bad)
% complete = done effort
             / total effort
velocity = points done
           per iteration
[/calc]
!trap Earned value gives credit only for 100% complete tasks in the course's rule, to avoid the "90% done" illusion.
[/card]
[/grid]

== ch7 | 150 | Ch 7 | CH 07 | Quality
[grid]
[card QA vs QC vs testing]
- **Quality assurance:** prevents defects through the process (SQA group, SQA plan, audits, standards). Process-focused.
- **Quality control:** checks the results against standards (reviews, inspections). Product-focused.
- **Testing** measures quality; it does not create it.
!trap QA = prevent (process); QC = detect (product).
[/card]
[card Success criteria and factors]
- Success: on schedule, within budget, meets requirements, high quality.
- Factors: executive support, user involvement, experienced PM, clear objectives, minimized scope, standard infrastructure, firm requirements, formal methodology, reliable estimates.
- Failures are usually caused by social factors (communication, leadership) more than technology.
[/card]
[card Metrics]
- Measure (one value) → measurement (the act) → metric (a measure relating values, e.g. defects/KLOC).
- **Product** metrics (static vs dynamic), **process** metrics (private vs public), **project** metrics.
- Size-oriented (per KLOC) vs function-oriented (per FP).
[/card]
[card SQA group]
Prepares the SQA plan, reviews activities and work products for compliance, audits, records non-compliance and reports it to management. Also: think small, say no to gold-plating, management by walking around (MBWA).
[/card]
[/grid]

== ch8 | 300 | Ch 8 | CH 08 | Stakeholders and communication
[grid]
[card Stakeholder management process]
Identify → understand and analyze → prioritize → engage → monitor.
- Inputs: project charter, procurement documents, enterprise environmental factors, process assets.
- Techniques: stakeholder analysis, **power/interest grid**, **salience model**, expert judgment, meetings.
- Outputs: **stakeholder register**, stakeholder engagement (management) plan, change requests.
!tip Final 242 Q3a asked for two inputs, two techniques and two outputs, and what each does.
[/card]
[card Power / interest grid]
[table]
 | Low interest | High interest
High power | Keep satisfied | **Manage closely**
Low power | Monitor | Keep informed
[/table]
[/card]
[card Salience model]
Attributes: **power, urgency, legitimacy**. One attribute: dormant (P), discretionary (L), demanding (U). Two: dominant (P+L), dangerous (P+U), dependent (L+U). All three: **definitive**, the top priority.
[/card]
[card Engagement levels (Final 242 table)]
[table]
Level | Meaning | Best method
Unaware | Doesn't know about the project | Push (announcements, briefings) to build awareness
Resistant | Knows, opposes change | Interactive (meetings, workshops) to hear concerns
Neutral | Knows, neither for nor against | Push and pull (reports, portal)
Supportive | Knows, supports | Pull + regular push updates
Leading | Actively drives success | Interactive (joint planning, steering)
[/table]
Record current (C) and desired (D) level per stakeholder in the assessment matrix.
[/card]
[card Communication]
- Types: internal/external, formal/informal, vertical/horizontal, written/oral, verbal/nonverbal.
- **Push** (memos, emails, reports: sent out) · **Pull** (portals, wikis: they fetch it) · **Interactive** (meetings, calls: two-way, best for complex or sensitive issues).
- Reports: status (where we are now), progress (what was done in the period), trend (performance over time), forecast (prediction), variance (planned vs actual), earned value.
[/card]
[card Breakdown example (Final 251 Q3)]
A requirement change agreed in a hallway is never written down; developers build the old version, the client rejects the demo, rework costs two weeks. **Prevent:** communication management plan (who needs what, when, how), written change requests, regular status meetings, a single shared source of truth, confirm understanding.
[/card]
[/grid]

== ch9 | 20 | Ch 9 | CH 09 | Change management and maintenance
[grid]
[card Maintenance types]
- **Corrective:** fix faults.
- **Adaptive:** fit a new environment (OS, hardware, law).
- **Perfective:** new or changed requirements, improvements.
- Costs 2–100× development. Cost factors: team stability, contractual responsibility, staff skills, program age and structure.
[/card]
[card Integrated change control]
Recognize the need → submit a request (RFC/ECO) → evaluate impact on scope, time, cost, risk → change report → **change control board** decides → implement → audit → release and update baselines.
- Agile: no CCB; the **customer (PO) owns product changes**, the team owns process changes, via the backlog.
[/card]
[card Configuration management]
Version and change control of software configuration items (SCIs): identification, version control, change control, configuration audit, status reporting.
[/card]
[card Rolling out]
- **Flash-cut** (switch everything at once: fast, risky) vs **parallel operation** (old and new together: safe, costly).
- Always have a **back-out plan**; plan data conversion, training, documentation, release checklist.
[/card]
[card Lehman's laws]
Continuing change · increasing complexity · large program evolution · organizational stability · conservation of familiarity. Change strategies: maintenance, architectural transformation, re-engineering.
[/card]
[card Project in trouble]
Options: cut size, increase productivity, slip the schedule. Recovery: **people** (morale, remove problem staff), **process** (miniature milestones, fix classic mistakes), **product** (stabilize requirements, trim features).
[/card]
[/grid]

? Final 251 :: How does project management help an organization achieve its strategic objectives?

? Final 251 :: Why does a project scope statement matter, and how does it reduce risk?
= It defines exactly what is (and is not) delivered.
+ It lists deliverables, acceptance criteria, exclusions, assumptions and constraints. A shared baseline prevents scope creep and gold-plating, makes estimates and the WBS possible, and gives change control something to compare against, so misunderstandings surface early instead of at delivery.

+ The triple constraint: adding scope needs more time or money; cutting time (crashing) raises cost; cutting cost may reduce scope or quality. Planning balances them according to what the sponsor fixes.

? Final 242 :: How do crashing and fast-tracking affect cost and risk?
