== governance | 210 | Ch 1 | CH 01 | Risk governance and control
[grid]
[card Why manage cyber risk]
Risk is inherent to every enterprise; avoiding all risk also avoids its opportunities. The aim is to take the right risks and keep them at an **acceptable** level.
[/card]
[card ISACA frameworks]
- **COBIT:** the overall governance and control model for IT (processes).
- **Val IT:** getting value from IT investments.
- **Risk IT:** managing IT-related business risk; fills the gap between generic enterprise risk frameworks and narrow IT security standards.
!hook COBIT = Control, Val IT = Value, Risk IT = Risk; they overlap around IT-related events.
[/card]
[card Business-driven security]
Security exists to support business requirements (CIA of the business's information), driven by policy → standards → procedures → guidelines, and by security planning.
[/card]
[/grid]

== terms | 190 | Ch 2 | CH 02 | Risk fundamentals
[grid]
[card Definitions (NIST SP 800-30)]
- **Risk:** the likelihood that a threat exploits a vulnerability, and the resulting impact on the organization's assets.
- **Threat:** potential cause of harm. **Vulnerability:** weakness. **Asset:** anything of value. **Impact:** the harm.
- Risk = f(probability of threat × vulnerability, impact).
[/card]
[card Residual risk]
Controls filter risk down; what remains is **residual risk**. It is never zero: management decides what residual level is acceptable (risk appetite).
!trap Inherent risk = before controls; residual = after controls.
[/card]
[card Risk responses]
- **Avoid:** stop the risky activity.
- **Transfer (share):** insurance, outsourcing.
- **Mitigate (reduce):** apply controls.
- **Accept:** cost of control exceeds the loss; document it.
[/card]
[card Control types]
- By function: **preventive**, **detective**, **corrective** (also deterrent, recovery, compensating).
- By nature: **administrative** (policies, training), **technical/logical** (firewalls, encryption), **physical** (locks, guards).
[/card]
[card Risk register]
Lists each risk with description, likelihood, impact, score, owner, response and status; reviewed regularly.
[/card]
[/grid]

== threats | 150 | Ch 3 | CH 03 | Threats and vulnerabilities
[grid]
[card Threat agents]
Insiders, hackers, criminals, hacktivists, nation states, competitors, natural events. Threats target the **network**, **host** or **application** layer.
[/card]
[card Vulnerability management]
- **CVE:** standard IDs for publicly known vulnerabilities.
- **CVSS:** 0–10 severity score (base, temporal, environmental).
- Cycle: discover → prioritize → remediate (patch, configure) → verify → monitor.
[/card]
[card Threat modeling]
- **STRIDE:** spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, DoS, elevation of privilege.
- **DREAD:** damage, reproducibility, exploitability, affected users, discoverability (rating).
- **PASTA** (risk-centric, 7 stages), **OCTAVE** (organizational, self-directed).
[/card]
[/grid]

== lifecycle | 262 | Ch 4 | CH 04 | The risk management lifecycle
[grid]
[card Steps in risk management]
1. Identify risks (assets, threats, vulnerabilities).
2. Assess risks (quantitative or qualitative).
3. Evaluate controls with a **cost-benefit analysis (CBA)**.
4. Acquire or install the appropriate controls.
5. Oversee processes (monitor).
6. Summarize the findings (report).
[/card]
[card Communities of interest]
- **General management** sets strategy and risk appetite.
- **IT management** supports the technology.
- **Information security** leads risk assessment and controls.
Each is accountable for risk in its area.
[/card]
[card Assets]
- Inventory people, procedures, data, software, hardware, networking.
- Classify and categorize by sensitivity and criticality; value by replacement cost, revenue impact, recovery cost.
!tip Identify assets first, value them after: valuing too early skews the inventory.
[/card]
[/grid]

== assess | 330 | Ch 5 | CH 05 | Risk assessment approaches
[grid wide]
[card Quantitative formulas]
[calc]
SLE  = asset value (AV) x exposure factor (EF)
ARO  = how often per year (once a month -> 12, once in 10 years -> 0.1)
ALE  = SLE x ARO
value of control = ALE(before) - ALE(after) - annual cost of control
                 > 0 -> worth buying
[/calc]
!trap ARO for "once every 5 years" is **0.2**, not 5. The control's cost must be **annualized** before comparing.
[/card]
[card Worked: stolen laptops]
[calc]
laptop value incl. data      = 2,000      EF = 100%
SLE = 2,000 x 1.0            = 2,000
5 laptops stolen per year -> ARO = 5
ALE before = 2,000 x 5       = 10,000
cable locks cut ARO to 1     -> ALE after = 2,000
locks cost 50 x 100 laptops  = 5,000 / year
value = 10,000 - 2,000 - 5,000 = 3,000 > 0 -> buy
[/calc]
[/card]
[/grid]
[grid]
[card Qualitative assessment]
- Subjective ratings (low/medium/high) of probability and impact; a **probability × impact matrix** ranks risks.
- Pros: fast, no exact numbers needed, includes intangibles. Cons: subjective, hard to justify spending.
- Quantitative pros: objective money values for CBA. Cons: hard to get accurate data, time-consuming.
[/card]
[card Risk determination formula]
Risk = (asset value × likelihood of the vulnerability) − % mitigated by current controls + uncertainty of knowledge.
Use it to fill a **ranked vulnerability risk worksheet**.
[/card]
[card Assessment basics]
- When: during the SDLC, when evaluating a control, after implementing it; repeat regularly (not one-time).
- Critical components **S-C-T**: **S**cope, **C**ritical areas, **T**eam (independent of the area assessed to avoid conflict of interest).
[/card]
[/grid]

== perform | 45 | Ch 6 | CH 06 | Performing a risk assessment
[grid]
[card Prepare]
**D-R-I**: **D**efine the assessment (scope, e.g. the email server), **R**eview previous findings, **I**dentify the management structure.
[/card]
[card Eight-step plan]
1. Identify the scope. 2. Identify critical areas. 3. Identify the team. 4. Identify threats. 5. Identify vulnerabilities. 6. Assess the likelihood and impact. 7. Identify controls (in place and recommended). 8. Document the results.
[/card]
[card Operational characteristics]
Describe how the system works (data flows, users, dependencies) before judging risks. Value assets by **replacement** vs **recovery** cost.
[/card]
[card POAM]
Plan of Action and Milestones: tracks each corrective action, the responsible person, resources and due dates until the risk is closed.
[/card]
[/grid]

== test | 340 | Self-test | TEST | Check your understanding
[qs]
? Practice :: A server worth 50,000 loses 40% of its value in a flood expected once every 10 years. SLE, ARO, ALE?
= SLE 20,000; ARO 0.1; ALE 2,000

? Practice :: ALE before 12,000, after 3,000, control costs 4,000 per year. Buy it?
= Yes: 12,000 − 3,000 − 4,000 = 5,000 > 0.

? Practice :: A virus hits once a month causing 5,000 in losses. ALE?
= 60,000 (5,000 × 12)

? Practice :: What is residual risk?
= The risk remaining after controls are applied.

? Practice :: Buying cyber insurance is which response?
= Transfer

? Practice :: Stopping a risky online service entirely is which response?
= Avoid

? Practice :: Classify: security awareness training.
= Administrative, preventive control

? Practice :: Classify: an IDS raising an alert.
= Technical, detective control

? Practice :: Which framework focuses on getting value from IT investments?
= Val IT

? Practice :: Quantitative vs qualitative: which uses money values?
= Quantitative

? Practice :: What does CVSS provide?
= A 0–10 severity score for a vulnerability.

? Practice :: Which STRIDE threat does a forged login represent?
= Spoofing

? Practice :: What are the S-C-T components of a risk assessment?
= Scope, critical areas, team

? Practice :: What is a POAM?
= A plan of action and milestones that tracks corrective actions to completion.

? Practice :: NIST SP 800-30 defines risk as…
= The likelihood that a threat exploits a vulnerability, and the resulting impact on the organization.

? Practice :: Why should the assessment team be independent of the area assessed?
= To avoid a conflict of interest that hides weaknesses.
[/qs]
